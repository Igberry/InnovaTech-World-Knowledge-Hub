# VA Niches Handout

> A niche is the specific kind of VA work you focus on. Starting with a niche makes you easier to hire and
> easier to refer. This handout lists common niches. You do not have to pick today, but read it before
> Session B. Fuller pay and platform details come in Weeks 4 and 5.

## Why a niche helps

"I do all admin for anyone" is forgettable. "I help coaches keep a clean inbox and calendar" is memorable
and easy to refer. A niche also lets you get genuinely good at a focused set of tasks, which raises what
you can charge over time.

## Common VA niches

| Niche | What you do | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **General admin VA** | Inbox, calendar, data entry, files, research | Almost every busy business |
| **Executive assistant (EA)** | Manage a senior person's whole workflow and priorities | Founders, executives |
| **Social media VA** | Schedule posts, basic graphics (Canva), replies, simple reporting | Coaches, creators, small brands |
| **E-commerce VA** | Process orders, update listings, answer buyer questions | Online stores, sellers |
| **Real estate VA** | Listings admin, scheduling, follow-ups, coordination | Agents, agencies |
| **Coaching / course VA** | Manage students, bookings, content, community | Coaches, course creators |
| **Customer service VA** | Answer tickets, manage a help desk, write FAQs | Online services, shops |
| **Bookkeeping-lite VA** | Record invoices and receipts, expense tracking (no accounting degree needed) | Small businesses, freelancers |

## How to choose (you will do this in Session B)

A good niche sits where these overlap:

1. **Interest**, you will do it a lot, so you should not dread it
2. **Proof**, you have some experience or a transferable skill to point to
3. **Demand**, real clients need it (you will check platforms in Weeks 4 to 5)
4. **One-sentence clarity**, you can say what you do in a single clear sentence

## Generalist vs specialist

- **Generalist** (general admin): easier to start, more competition, lower starting pay.
- **Specialist** (a focused niche): a little harder to start, less competition, higher pay as you prove
  it.

Most people start slightly general and sharpen into a specialism as they learn what they enjoy and are
good at. You can always adjust.

> **Tip:** When torn between two niches, pick the one where you have the clearest proof and that you would
> not mind doing on a dull day. You can refine after your first client.
