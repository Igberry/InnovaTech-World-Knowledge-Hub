# Customer-Service Response Templates

> Reusable, friendly, professional replies a VA sends often. Adapt the tone to each client's brand. Keep
> them warm, clear, and short. Replace the [brackets]. Draft three of your own in Session B.

## 1. Acknowledge (we received your message)

> Hi [name], thanks for reaching out. I've got your message and I'm looking into it, I'll get back to you
> by [time/day]. In the meantime, [anything helpful]. Best, [name/brand]

## 2. Answer a common question

> Hi [name], great question. [Clear, direct answer in one or two lines.] If you'd like, I can also
> [helpful extra]. Let me know. Best, [name/brand]

## 3. Delay / need more time

> Hi [name], thanks for your patience. This is taking a little longer because [brief honest reason]. I
> expect to have it sorted by [time/day] and will update you then. Sorry for the wait. Best, [name/brand]

## 4. Handle a complaint (calm and solution-focused)

> Hi [name], I'm sorry about [the issue], that's understandably frustrating. Here's what I'm doing to fix
> it: [specific step]. [Timeline or next step.] Thank you for letting us know, it helps us do better. Best,
> [name/brand]

## 5. Follow up (gentle nudge)

> Hi [name], just following up on [topic]. No rush, I wanted to make sure it didn't get lost. Whenever
> you have a moment, [the one thing you need]. Thanks! Best, [name/brand]

## 6. Say no / set a boundary politely

> Hi [name], thank you for asking. I'm not able to [request], but I can [alternative you can offer]. Would
> that work? Best, [name/brand]

## Tone rules

- Lead with empathy on anything negative; acknowledge before you solve.
- Be specific about the next step and when it happens.
- Keep it short. Warm, not stiff; professional, not cold.
- Never argue or blame the customer, even when they are wrong.

> **Tip:** Save your best replies as templates in the client's tool (or your own notes) so a five-minute
> reply becomes a ten-second one, consistently on-brand.
