# Role-Play Scenarios (Week 3, Session C)

> In the Role-Play Lab, your mentor plays the client. Prepare a response to each scenario below, then
> deliver it live and get feedback on tone and professionalism. These clients are fictional.

## Scenario 1: A difficult email

**Client background:** You manage the inbox for Ada, a busy coach. A long-time customer emails, annoyed
that a reply took three days and their booking was missed.

**What the client (customer) says:** "This is the second time I've been ignored. I'm starting to wonder if
you even want my business. I need this sorted today."

**What a good reply achieves:** acknowledges the frustration genuinely, apologises without over-grovelling,
fixes or escalates the booking with a clear next step and time, and keeps the customer feeling valued.
*Prepare:* your written reply (use the complaint template as a starting point, then make it specific).

## Scenario 2: A scheduling conflict

**Client background:** You manage Ada's calendar. Two important people have been booked into the same
slot tomorrow, and both think the time is confirmed.

**What the client (Ada) says:** "I've just seen two meetings at 3pm tomorrow. How did this happen? Fix it,
but don't upset either of them."

**What a good reply achieves:** calm ownership (no excuses), a concrete fix (offer two or three new times
to the lower-priority attendee, keep the other), clear messages ready to send, and a note on how you'll
prevent it (a buffer/booking rule). *Prepare:* what you say to Ada, plus the reschedule message.

## Scenario 3: A customer complaint

**Client background:** You handle customer support for a small online store. A buyer received the wrong
item and wants a refund immediately.

**What the customer says:** "I ordered the blue one and got the red. I want my money back now, or I'm
leaving a bad review everywhere."

**What a good reply achieves:** empathy first, a clear path to resolution (replacement or refund per the
store's policy, which you confirm with the client if unsure), a realistic timeline, and a tone that calms
rather than inflames. *Prepare:* your reply, and note anything you'd check with the store owner first.

## How you'll be assessed

- Tone: warm, calm, professional, empathetic before solving
- Clarity: a specific next step and timeline
- Judgement: knowing what you can handle vs what to check with the client first

> **Tip:** For anything about money, refunds, or a VIP, it is fine, and smart, to say you'll confirm with
> the client before promising. Never invent a policy you're unsure of.
