# Rate Negotiation Scripts

> Short, polite scripts for the money conversation. Say your rate calmly, once, then stop talking. Replace
> the [brackets] with your researched numbers.

## Stating your rate

> "For [scope], my rate is [rate]. That covers [what's included]. Shall I put together a short plan?"

Say it as a simple fact. Don't apologise, don't over-explain.

## Responding to a lowball offer

> "Thanks, I understand budgets matter. My rate for this is [rate] because [brief value: reliability,
> proof, time saved]. I could fit the budget by adjusting the **scope**, for example [smaller package].
> Would that work?"

Adjust scope, not just price, so you don't train the client to undervalue you.

## When they say "that's too high"

> "I hear you. My rate reflects [value/proof]. If [rate] is beyond this project, we could start with a
> smaller [package] at [amount] and grow from there. Which would you prefer?"

## Defining scope before you start

> "To make sure we're aligned: this covers [tasks], up to [hours] a month, with [response time]. Anything
> beyond that we'd agree separately. Does that match what you need?"

## Holding a boundary (politely)

> "I'm not able to do [out-of-scope/underpriced thing], but I can [alternative]. Would that help?"

## Rules

- **Research first** so your rate is grounded, not a guess.
- **Silence is fine** after you state your rate, let them respond.
- **Flex scope, not just price.**
- **Get the final scope and rate in writing** before starting (Week 7 onboarding).

> **Tip:** The first few times feel uncomfortable, that's normal. Practise these out loud in your mock
> interview so they come easily when it's real.
