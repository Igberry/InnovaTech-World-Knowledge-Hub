# Sample Trial Task (Practice Under Time Pressure)

> Clients often ask for a short trial task. Practise this fictional one in about 45 minutes, as you would
> for a real client. It mirrors common VA work. (Model approach is kept by your mentor.)

## The brief (fictional client)

"BrightLeaf Coaching" needs help getting organised. In about 45 minutes, please:

1. **Inbox:** here are 8 sample emails (your mentor provides them). Sort them using a labelling system and
   say how you'd reply to the two most urgent (short drafts).
2. **Calendar:** schedule three provided meetings across a week with no clashes and clear titles; note the
   time zone handling.
3. **A quick win:** suggest one small improvement you noticed that would save the client time.

## How to approach it (good trial habits)

- **Read the whole brief first**; note what success looks like.
- **Clarify** anything genuinely unclear with one tidy question, don't guess on important points.
- **Do it the way you'd do real work**: tidy, labelled, clear.
- **Deliver with a short summary**: what you did, any assumptions, and your "quick win".
- **Keep to the time**; a client is often testing judgement and communication, not just output.

## Paid vs unpaid trials (your policy)

- A **short, paid** trial (or a small fixed fee) is reasonable and professional.
- Be cautious of **long "trials"** that look like real, unpaid project work, that's a red flag (see the
  scam checklist).

> **Tip:** Your summary message matters as much as the task. It shows how you'll communicate as their VA.
