# The 5-Line Proposal (A Framework That Gets Replies)

> Most proposals get ignored because they talk about the applicant, not the client. This framework flips
> that: proof-first and client-problem-led. Five short parts. Adapt, don't paste the same one everywhere.

## The five lines

1. **Hook (mirror the job post):** open with the client's exact need, in their words, so they instantly
   feel understood. *"You need someone to keep your Instagram consistent while you focus on coaching."*
2. **Proof:** one line of relevant evidence, a sample, a result, or a transferable skill. *"I've built a
   week of scheduled content and simple Canva graphics (sample below)."*
3. **Plan:** one concrete thing you'd do for them. *"I'd start by setting up a content calendar and
   batching a week ahead so you're never scrambling."*
4. **Availability / rate:** short and clear. *"I can start this week and work [hours]; my rate is [X]."*
5. **Call to action:** one easy next step. *"Want me to send a quick 3-post sample for your page?"*

## Before and after (illustrative)

**Weak (ignored):** "Hello, I am a hardworking and dedicated virtual assistant with many skills. I can do
anything you need. Please hire me, I will not disappoint."

**Strong (5-Line):** "You need steady Instagram content while you focus on coaching. I've built a week of
scheduled posts with simple graphics (sample below). I'd set up a content calendar and batch a week ahead
so you're never scrambling. I can start this week, [hours], at [rate]. Want a quick 3-post sample for your
page?"

## Rules

- **Tailor line 1 to every post.** It decides whether they read on.
- **Keep it short**, five lines, not five paragraphs.
- **Lead with them, not you.**
- **One clear next step**, make saying yes easy.

> **Tip:** Save a skeleton, but always rewrite the hook per post. A generic hook is why most proposals
> fail.
