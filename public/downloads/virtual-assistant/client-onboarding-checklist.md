# Client Onboarding Checklist - Template

> Use this the moment a client says yes. A calm, organised start is the best first impression you can make.
> Fictional sample. Replace with the real details for each client (and keep them private).

## 1. Agree the basics (before any work starts)

- [ ] Scope: exactly what I will and will not do
- [ ] Hours or package agreed and in writing
- [ ] Rate and payment schedule agreed and in writing
- [ ] Working hours and time zone confirmed
- [ ] How we will communicate (email / chat tool / calls) and how often
- [ ] Response-time expectation (e.g., within one business day)
- [ ] Who I report to, and who can give me tasks

## 2. Get access safely

- [ ] Access given through the client's proper sharing settings, not by them sending passwords
- [ ] I use a password manager; I never store passwords in plain text
- [ ] Two-factor authentication is on wherever possible
- [ ] I have only the access I need for my tasks (nothing more)

> **Privacy:** Never ask a client to email you a password. Use the tool's own "share" or "invite" feature, or a password manager's secure share. If a client insists on sending a password, ask them to change it right after the job.

## 3. Set up my workspace for this client

- [ ] A folder (cloud) for this client's files
- [ ] A task board or list for this client
- [ ] A time-tracking entry/project for this client
- [ ] Saved the SOPs we agreed (inbox, calendar, reporting)

## 4. First-week plan

- [ ] Day 1: short kickoff - confirm priorities and the first three tasks
- [ ] Day 2 to 4: do the first tasks; ask questions early
- [ ] Day 5: send the first weekly report; ask for feedback

## 5. Questions to ask the client at kickoff

1. What does a great week look like to you?
2. What are the three things you most want off your plate?
3. How do you like to communicate - short messages, calls, or written updates?
4. What should I never do without checking first?
5. Who else will I work with, and how do I reach them?

## 6. Red flags to watch for (protect yourself)

- Pressure to start before anything is agreed in writing
- Refusal to discuss payment or scope
- Requests to use your personal bank details for the client's business transactions
- Tasks that feel dishonest or unsafe

> **Warning:** If a "client" wants to send you money to buy things, forward payments, or receive funds on their behalf, stop. This is a common scam. See Week 4 of the Remote Ready course on spotting scams.
