# Fact-Check Worksheet (Check AI Output Before You Trust It)

> AI tools can state wrong things with total confidence. This worksheet is a quick routine to
> catch mistakes before they cause harm. Use it for anything that matters.

## The claim I am checking

- What the AI said: _______________________________________________
- Why it matters (what happens if it is wrong): _______________________

## Step 1: Is it the kind of thing AI often gets wrong?

Be extra careful with:
- [ ] Specific **facts and numbers** (dates, prices, statistics)
- [ ] **Names, quotes, and who said what**
- [ ] **Links and sources** (AI often invents URLs)
- [ ] **Laws, rules, medical or financial advice**
- [ ] **Very recent events** (the tool may not know them, or may guess)

## Step 2: Find an independent source

- [ ] I searched for the claim on a trusted, official, or well-known source
- [ ] The source is independent of the AI (not just the AI repeating itself)
- [ ] I found at least one source that confirms it

Source 1: _______________________
Source 2: _______________________

## Step 3: Check links and quotes directly

- [ ] Every link the AI gave actually opens and says what the AI claimed
- [ ] Any quote appears, word for word, in a real, findable place
- [ ] Any statistic traces back to who measured it and when

## Step 4: Decide

- [ ] **Confirmed** - independent sources agree. Safe to use.
- [ ] **Unconfirmed** - I could not verify it. Do not present it as fact.
- [ ] **Wrong** - sources disagree. Correct or remove it.

## Quick rules

- If you cannot verify it, do not publish it as fact.
- A confident tone from an AI is not evidence.
- For high-stakes topics (health, money, legal, safety), check with a qualified human.

> **Warning:** "The AI said so" is never a source. The source is the real, checkable place the
> fact comes from. If that does not exist, treat the claim as unproven.
