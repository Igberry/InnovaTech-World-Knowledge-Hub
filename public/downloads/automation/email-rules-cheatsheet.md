# Email Rules Cheat-Sheet (Built-In Automation, No New Tools)

> Your email already automates things with "rules" or "filters". These are safe, free quick wins.
> The exact menu names differ between Gmail, Outlook and others, so search "[your email] create a
> filter/rule" for the precise clicks. These are recipes, not step-by-step for one app.

## What an email rule is

A rule says: **WHEN an email matches something, DO an action automatically.** For example: when an
email is from receipts@..., label it "Finance" and skip the inbox.

## Useful rule recipes

| When an email... | Do this | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Is from a known receipts/billing sender | Label "Finance", archive | Keeps receipts tidy, inbox clean |
| Contains a word like "invoice" | Label "To pay" | Nothing gets lost |
| Is from your boss or best client | Star it / mark important | You see key people first |
| Is a newsletter you rarely read | Skip inbox, label "Read later" | Less inbox noise |
| Is an automated notification | Label by app, archive | Groups the noise away |

## Other built-in email automations

- **Templates / canned responses:** save replies you send often, then insert them in one click.
- **Signatures:** set a professional signature so you never retype it.
- **Snooze:** make an email come back to the top of your inbox at a chosen time.
- **Vacation / auto-reply:** tell senders you are away and when you will respond.

## Safety notes

- [ ] Do not auto-delete based on a weak rule, you may lose something important. Prefer "archive" or
      "label" over "delete".
- [ ] Test a new rule by checking what it catches for a few days before trusting it.
- [ ] Review your rules occasionally, senders and needs change.

> **Tip:** Rules plus a few saved templates can save a busy person an hour a day, with no new tools and
> no risk of anything leaving your own inbox.

> **Warning:** Avoid rules that auto-forward email outside your organisation unless you are certain it is
> safe and allowed. Forwarding can leak private information.
