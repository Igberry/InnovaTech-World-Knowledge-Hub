# Final Test Passages

Use a different passage for each test. Set the exact duration, do not paste text, and record the tool, keyboard, WPM and accuracy. These are original practice passages, not hiring tests.

## One-minute test

The team began with a short list and a clear plan. Each person checked one detail, saved the file, and asked for help when the source was unclear. Careful work is easier to review.

## Three-minute test

Every morning, a small team checks the notes left from the previous day. The first task is to read each item and confirm that the words are clear. A missing date or amount should be marked for review, not filled in by guesswork. After the list is checked, the team can decide what to do first and who will handle each task.

At the end of the shift, the team records what is finished and what still needs attention. A short, accurate note helps the next person continue the work. Save the document with a clear name, keep a backup copy, and remove any private details that are not needed for the task.

## Five-minute test

A useful work note is easy to understand. It identifies the task, gives the details that matter, and explains what should happen next. If someone else must act on the note, include a date or time where needed and check that names, amounts and instructions match the source.

Before sharing a document, read it from beginning to end. Check spelling, punctuation, spacing and the order of information. Compare numbers with the original record one group at a time. If the source is unclear, ask the person responsible for it. Never invent a customer name, payment amount or delivery promise to make a document look complete.

Small habits add up over time. Sit comfortably, return your fingers to the home row, type at a steady pace and pause when your hands feel tense. Practise weak keys in short sets, then use them in real sentences. Keep a log that shows what you practised and what you will change next time.

For every timed test, record the date, test length, keyboard and scoring method along with the result. A one-minute score is not the same as a five-minute score. Report the duration and accuracy whenever you share a WPM result. The purpose of a test is to learn what to practise, not to compare people who used different keyboards or scoring rules.
