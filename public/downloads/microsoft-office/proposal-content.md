## Page 2 — Executive summary

This practice proposal describes a small, organised catering plan for a fictional one-day training event. It outlines a sample menu, guest-count assumptions, the preparation and delivery process, a quality checklist, communication points, and invented example amounts. Every operational detail would need to be confirmed before a real event.

**Practice assumptions:** 40 guests; one lunch service; a venue in Yenagoa; a final menu and dietary information to be confirmed with the organiser. These are exercise inputs, not facts about an actual client.

## Page 3 — Understanding the request

Summarise the fictional organiser's needs:

- A clear lunch schedule that allows a short service window.
- A labelled menu with ingredients and a way to ask about dietary needs.
- A count of meals, a contingency plan for late changes, and a named decision-maker.
- A written confirmation before any purchase or preparation.

Use a short requirements table with columns for request, owner, confirmation needed and target date. Avoid collecting medical or dietary information in an open shared workbook.

## Page 6 — Quality, safety and accessibility

List practical checks such as clean packaging, readable labels, safe transport, an accessible collection point, a count before handover and a process to report an issue. Do not promise a medical or allergen guarantee. State that dietary details must be confirmed with the appropriate person and handled privately.

## Page 7 — Budget and terms to confirm

## Page 8 — Risks, assumptions and next steps

Identify likely planning risks: a late guest-count change, unavailable ingredient, power interruption, delayed delivery or unclear decision authority. For each, provide an owner and a practical response. Finish with decisions the fictional organiser would need to approve and a sign-off placeholder that is clearly marked **not a real agreement**.
