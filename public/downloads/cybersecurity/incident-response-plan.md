## Scenario 1: An account is hacked

1. **Change the password** immediately (from a device you trust). Change it anywhere you reused it.
2. **Turn on 2FA** if it was not on.
3. **Check recovery settings:** make sure the recovery email and phone are still yours (attackers change these).
4. **Sign out of all other sessions / devices** (most services have this option).
5. **Check for damage:** sent messages, new rules/forwarding, changed details, payments.
6. **Tell people** if the attacker may have messaged your contacts (common with scams).
7. **Report** to the platform.

## Scenario 2: Money or card details exposed

1. **Contact your bank / mobile money** at once; ask them to secure or freeze as needed.
2. **Change related passwords** and turn on 2FA.
3. **Watch for further fraud** over the next weeks.
4. **Report** to the bank and, where relevant, the authorities.

## Scenario 3: Device lost or stolen

1. **Lock or wipe it remotely** if you set that up (do this now if you have not).
2. **Change passwords** for accounts that were signed in on it, starting with email.
3. **Tell your bank** if payment apps were on it.
4. **Report** the loss as appropriate.

## Scenario 4: Suspected malware or ransomware

1. **Disconnect** the device from the internet to stop it spreading.
2. **Do not pay** a ransom; it rarely restores data and funds criminals.
3. **Seek help** from a trusted technician or your IT contact.
4. **Restore from a clean backup** once the device is cleaned (this is why backups matter).

## After any incident

- [ ] Note what happened and how it got in
- [ ] Fix that weak point (reused password, no 2FA, a clicked link)
- [ ] Update this plan and your security checklist

> **Important:** Speed matters most with email and money. Email resets everything else, so secure it first.
> With money, contact your bank immediately, minutes count.

> **Warning:** Attackers often change your recovery email/phone so you cannot get back in. Always re-check
> recovery settings after regaining access.
