# Phishing Red-Flags Cheat-Sheet

> Phishing is a fake message (email, SMS, DM, call) that tries to trick you into giving up passwords,
> money, or personal details, or into clicking something harmful. Use this to spot and stop it.

## The mindset

Attackers rely on you acting fast and not thinking. Your best defence is simple: **slow down and check.**
When a message pushes you to act urgently, that is itself a warning sign.

## Red flags (the more that match, the more suspicious)

- [ ] **Urgency or fear:** "Act now or your account will be closed / you will be fined."
- [ ] **Too good to be true:** you won a prize, a job, or money you did not expect.
- [ ] **Unexpected link or attachment**, especially asking you to log in or enable something.
- [ ] **Asks for secrets:** password, full card number, PIN, OTP/verification code. Real organisations do
      not ask for these.
- [ ] **Wrong or odd sender address** (look closely; small misspellings of a real domain).
- [ ] **Generic greeting** ("Dear customer") from a service that knows your name.
- [ ] **Spelling and grammar** that seem off for the supposed sender.
- [ ] **Payment pressure:** asked to pay by gift card, crypto, or to a personal account.
- [ ] **A "boss" or "client" messaging from a new number** asking for money or data urgently.

## What to do

1. **Do not click, reply, or pay.** Pause.
2. **Verify independently.** Contact the person or company using a number or site you already trust, not
   the details in the message.
3. **Check the link before clicking** (hover to see the real address; on mobile, long-press to preview).
4. **Never share an OTP / verification code** with anyone, even someone claiming to be support.
5. **Report and delete.** Report to the platform or your IT/security contact, then delete.

## The golden rules

> **Warning:** No real bank, company, or service will ever ask for your password or a one-time code. Anyone
> who does is trying to scam you. Hang up or delete.

> **Rule of thumb:** If a message makes you feel urgent, scared, or excited, stop. That feeling is the trap.
> Verify through a channel you trust before you do anything.

## If you already clicked or shared something

- Change the password for that account immediately (and anywhere you reused it)
- Turn on 2FA
- Tell your bank if money or card details were involved
- Follow your incident-response plan
