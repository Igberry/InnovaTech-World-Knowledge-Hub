Starting a career in tech can feel overwhelming, especially when most advice online assumes
you already have fast internet, a powerful laptop, and years of free time. This post is a
grounded starting point for people in Bayelsa and beyond who want to build real, useful skills.

## Start with one skill, not ten

It is tempting to try to learn everything at once. Pick **one** path - data analysis, web
development, or design - and go deep enough to build something you can show. A finished small
project beats five half-finished tutorials.

## Build in public

Create a free GitHub account early and put your practice work there. Employers and clients care
far more about what you can do than about certificates. Your project files, written up clearly,
become your portfolio.

> **Tip:** Keep a simple log of what you build each week. Over three months, that log becomes
> proof of steady progress - to others, and to yourself.

## Be honest about the timeline

Real skills take months, not days. Anyone promising a guaranteed job or quick income is not being
straight with you. What consistent practice *can* do is make you genuinely job-ready and give you
work to point to.

## Next steps

- Browse the [courses](/courses/) and pick one that fits where you are now.
- Set aside a few focused hours each week and protect that time.
- When you get stuck, write down exactly what you tried before asking for help - that habit is
  itself a core professional skill.

You do not need to have it all figured out to begin. You just need to start.
