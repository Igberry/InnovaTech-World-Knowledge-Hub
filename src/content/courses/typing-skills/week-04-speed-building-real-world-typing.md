**Goal:** Build a pace you can hold, type real work formats, and finish with three comparable tests and a small portfolio. This is a doing week, not a reading week.

## What you'll be able to do

- Use short speed bursts without wrecking accuracy or comfort
- Copy-type from a source with your eyes off the keyboard
- Produce a letter, email, product list and a transcription of your own voice
- Say how an employer test might measure speed and accuracy
- Report a real result honestly and set a maintenance routine

## Before you start (10 minutes)

- Open your weak-key report and pick two areas still worth practising.
- Open the [final-test passages](/downloads/typing-skills/final-test-passages.md) to know the format, but do not memorise them.
- Warm-up: one clean minute on a passage you already know, accuracy first.
- If an employer gave you a test format, read its own instructions - not every test scores the same way.

## Try it 1 - Build speed without forcing it

**Why it matters:** smooth, repeatable movement makes you faster. White-knuckle sprinting does not.

**Do this now:**
1. Run short bursts just above your comfortable pace, then drop back to a clean, slower pass. A good cycle: one minute quicker, two minutes controlled.
2. Stop the quick interval if your shoulders tense or accuracy drops a lot.
3. Try one-, three- and five-minute durations. Record the length with each result.
4. Do not compare a one-minute peak with a five-minute average as if they were the same measure.

**You've got it when:** your quick bursts stay clean and your shoulders stay loose.

## Try it 2 - Copy-type with eyes off the keyboard

**Why it matters:** copy typing from a source is the core of transcription, data entry and virtual-assistant work.

**Do this now:**
1. Put the source close to the screen. Read a few words ahead, type the current phrase, then check punctuation and line breaks.
2. Copy one paragraph without once looking down at the keys.
3. If the source has an obvious mistake, copy it exactly for the exercise and note the issue separately - do not silently "fix" a source. In real work, ask the owner.

**You've got it when:** you can copy a paragraph accurately without glancing at your hands.

## Try it 3 - Type real work documents

**Why it matters:** letters, emails and lists are the actual jobs people get paid to type.

**Do this now:**
1. Make a clean copy of the fictional [letter](/downloads/typing-skills/letter-to-type.md), [email](/downloads/typing-skills/email-to-type.md) and [product list](/downloads/typing-skills/product-list-to-type.md) in a word processor or spreadsheet.
2. Keep headings and line breaks readable. Use the supplied fictional names, products and Naira amounts exactly as written.
3. Proofread by comparing each section to the source, then scan the whole document for anything accidentally skipped.

**You've got it when:** each document matches its source and reads cleanly.

## Try it 4 - Transcribe your own voice note

**Why it matters:** typing while listening is a different gear, and it is a paid skill on its own.

**Do this now:**
1. Record a 20 to 30 second voice note you wrote yourself, such as a fictional meal description or a study plan.
2. Listen in short segments and type what you hear. Replay unclear parts once or twice.
3. Mark uncertain words in brackets rather than guessing.
4. Delete the recording when you are done, especially if it has any personal detail. Do not use copyrighted audio or a real person's voice note.

**You've got it when:** your transcript is accurate and honestly flags anything you were unsure of.

## Try it 5 - Prepare for an employer test, calmly

**Why it matters:** knowing the rules before you start is half the score.

**Do this now:**
1. Check the test type: speed, accuracy, data entry or formatting. Read the instructions first.
2. Confirm whether mistakes can be corrected, whether the text is supplied, the time limit, and whether the score is gross or net.
3. Before you start: check the keyboard works, close unrelated apps, silence non-urgent notifications, and take a short warm-up. If remote, check power and connection in advance.
4. Never have someone else sit a test for you.

**You've got it when:** you can list what to confirm before any typing test begins.

## Try it 6 - Final tests and an honest CV line

**Why it matters:** an honest, specific result is worth more than an inflated one that falls apart on the job.

**Do this now:**
1. Take one-, three- and five-minute tests with a consistent method. Record date, keyboard, duration, WPM, accuracy and the source of the test.
2. Use the supplied final passages but do not memorise them beforehand.
3. Write one honest CV line, for example: `Recent test: 32 WPM at 96% accuracy, five-minute test, [month/year], [tool].`
4. Do not claim a result you did not measure, and do not suggest one score guarantees performance on every task.

**You've got it when:** three recorded results and one honest, specific CV line are in your folder.

## Try it 7 - Set up your maintenance routine

**Why it matters:** the skill fades without light upkeep. A tiny routine keeps it.

**Do this now:**
1. Plan 15 minutes once or twice a week after the course.
2. Spend a few minutes on a weak-key drill, then type a paragraph or work-like document and proofread it.
3. Retake a test monthly or before any application that asks for one. If speed rises but accuracy drops, go back to slower clean practice.

**You've got it when:** your 15-minute routine is written down where you will see it.

## Read your portfolio as a stranger would

Before submitting a work sample, ask someone to read it without the source beside them. Can they tell the purpose, find the key details and say what happens next? Then compare it with the original to catch missing or changed words. Accuracy and readability are two different checks: a document can be exact but hard to read, or polished but wrong.

Keep the portfolio simple: descriptive file names, consistent headings, a separate folder per task, and a short note saying which items are fictional practice, what tool you used and when. Do not upload contact details, a private recording, or a screenshot with unrelated notifications.

## Choose your next target

Use your one-, three- and five-minute results to pick one goal for the next month. Short score high but five-minute result falls? Practise endurance at a relaxed pace. WPM steady but accuracy below target? Short clean-copy sessions and review the common error. Work samples showing errors the tests missed? Add proofreading and data-entry practice.

The suggested beginner benchmark is 35 WPM at 95% accuracy, but starting points, keyboards, layouts and access needs vary. The certificate test requirement is the learner's agreed personal target or 30 WPM at 95% accuracy, whichever is higher. This course builds job-ready typing skills and a record of real practice; a certificate score is not a promise of performance in any particular job. If you do not hit the target, document your progress and agree a continued plan with your facilitator.

## This week's build: your typing portfolio

The package that shows what you can do:

1. Three timed results (one, three and five minutes), each with accuracy and method.
2. A fictional formal letter, a business email and a one-page product list, all proofread against their sources.
3. A transcription of your own voice note, with uncertainty flagged honestly.
4. Your practice log plus a short note: your method, your next improvement goal, and how you checked the work samples.

*Quick win: your cleanest work document doubles as a sample you can show anyone who asks what you can type.*

### Grading guide

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Test results and improvement evidence | 40% |
| Work-typing quality and completeness | 40% |
| Practice log and reflection | 20% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] All three test durations are recorded with accuracy and method.
- [ ] The letter, email and product list are readable and proofread.
- [ ] The transcription uses self-created audio and flags uncertainty.
- [ ] The CV speed statement is based on a real recent test.
- [ ] A 15-minute maintenance routine is written down.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Beat your three-minute result by one clean WPM, accuracy held.
- Type a short thank-you email to a fictional customer and proofread it aloud.
- Draft your CV typing line and check every detail against your real test.

## Mentoring Session Plan (60 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Check posture and identify signs of tension |
| 10 min | Review the log and compare test methods |
| 15 min | Coach controlled speed intervals and copy-typing technique |
| 15 min | Run a three-minute test and review errors and accuracy |
| 10 min | Review one portfolio task and the learner's maintenance plan |
| 5 min | Set an individual next-step goal and recognise progress |

## Common Mistakes

- Sprinting through all tests and confusing fatigue with skill.
- Memorising a test passage instead of testing technique.
- Claiming mobile typing as physical-keyboard WPM.
- Guessing a word during transcription instead of marking it uncertain.
- Reporting only the fastest test without duration or accuracy.
- Treating a course target as an employer-wide requirement.

## Facilitator Notes

Offer a private testing space where practical. Explain each score method before the test and let learners take a short warm-up. Grade the portfolio against the stated rubric, not against one another's speed.
