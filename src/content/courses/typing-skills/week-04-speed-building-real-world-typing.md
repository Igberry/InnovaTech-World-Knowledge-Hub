**Goal:** Use steady technique in real work formats and finish with three comparable tests and a small portfolio.

## Learning Objectives

- Use short speed intervals without sacrificing accuracy or comfort.
- Type accurately from a source while keeping eyes on the source and screen.
- Prepare a formal letter, email, product list and self-recorded transcription.
- Explain how an employer typing test may measure speed and accuracy.
- Report a recent test result honestly and set a maintenance routine.

## Tools / Accounts Needed This Week

A physical keyboard, word processor, timer and the supplied original practice documents. A phone and a quiet room are optional for recording your own voice note.

## Pre-Session Resources (about 2 hours)

- Review your weak-key report and choose two remaining practice areas.
- Read the [final-test passages](/downloads/typing-skills/final-test-passages.md) before the final test; do not practise by memorising the test.
- Search YouTube for **"copy typing practice accuracy rhythm"**.
- If an employer has supplied a test format, read its own instructions; do not assume every test uses the same scoring rules.

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Build speed without forcing it

Use short bursts at a pace just above your comfortable level, then return to a clean, slower pass. A practical cycle is one minute at a slightly quicker pace, followed by two minutes at a controlled pace. Stop the quicker interval if your shoulders tense or accuracy drops substantially. The purpose is to practise smooth movement, not to prove you can maintain an uncomfortable speed.

Use one-, three- and five-minute durations. Short tests help you focus on a small challenge; longer tests show whether the technique stays comfortable. Record the test length and result. Do not compare a one-minute peak with a five-minute average as if they were the same measure.

### Lesson 2: Copy typing and source control

In copy typing, the source text is separate from the text you enter. Position it close to the screen if possible. Read a few words ahead, type the current phrase, then check punctuation and line breaks. Avoid looking down at the keyboard. When the source is on paper, keep it near the screen and adjust lighting to reduce repeated head movement.

A source document is not always correct. In this course, copy the text exactly for the exercise, then note any apparent errors separately instead of silently "fixing" the source. In real work, ask the document owner to resolve ambiguous or incorrect source details.

### Lesson 3: Real-world document tasks

The downloads include a fictional formal letter, a short business email and a product list. Create a clean copy of each in a word processor or spreadsheet. Keep headings and line breaks readable. Use the supplied fictional names, products and Naira amounts as written. Proofread by comparing each section to the source, then check the whole document for accidental omissions.

When typing a meeting note or simple transcript, mark unclear words rather than inventing them. Do not create a transcript of another person's recording unless you have permission to use it.

### Lesson 4: Transcription practice with your own voice

Record a short voice note that you wrote yourself, such as a fictional description of a meal or a plan for a study session. Keep it to 20–30 seconds. Listen in short segments and type what you hear. Replay unclear parts once or twice; mark uncertain words in brackets instead of guessing. Delete the recording when it is no longer needed, especially if it includes personal details.

This exercise measures listening and typing together, not just keyboard speed. Keep it separate from the formal WPM test. Do not download copyrighted audio or use a real customer's voice note.

### Lesson 5: Optional phone typing

Phone typing uses a different skill from physical-keyboard touch typing. If useful, practise with both thumbs, keep autocorrect in mind, and review names, dates, amounts and addresses before sending. Voice input and predictive text can change what you intended to write, so proofread the final message. Do not present a mobile typing score as your keyboard WPM.

### Lesson 6: Employer tests and calm preparation

Typing tests can measure speed, accuracy, data entry or document formatting. Read the instructions before beginning. Check whether mistakes can be corrected, whether the text is supplied, what time limit applies and whether the score is gross or net speed. Use the required keyboard and layout when possible.

Before a test, confirm that the keyboard works, close unrelated apps, silence non-urgent notifications and take a brief practice warm-up. If the test is remote, check power, connection and permitted tools in advance. During the test, keep a steady pace and follow the stated rules. Do not use another person to take a test for you.

### Lesson 7: Final testing and honest CV claims

Take one-, three- and five-minute tests using a consistent method. Record the date, keyboard, duration, WPM, accuracy and source of the test. Use the same scoring method for comparisons. The supplied final passages are original exercises; avoid memorising them before the test.

On a CV, describe typing ability in a specific and honest way, for example: "Recent test: 32 WPM at 96% accuracy, five-minute test, [month/year], [tool]." Do not claim a result you did not measure, and do not imply that one practice score guarantees performance in every work task.

### Lesson 8: Maintain the skill

After the course, practise for 15 minutes once or twice a week. Spend a few minutes on a weak-key drill, then type a paragraph or a work-like document and proofread it. Retake a test monthly or before an application that requests one. If speed rises but accuracy drops, return to slower clean practice.

### Review the portfolio as a reader

Before submitting a work sample, ask someone to read it without the source beside them. Can they identify the purpose, find the important details and tell what should happen next? Then compare it with the original to find missing or changed words. These are two different checks: a document can be accurate but difficult to read, or polished but inaccurate.

Keep the portfolio simple. Use descriptive file names, consistent headings and a separate folder for each task. Include a short note that says which items are fictional practice, what tool you used and when you completed the work. Do not upload contact information, a private voice recording, or a screenshot containing unrelated notifications. The portfolio should prove your process without exposing anyone's personal information.

### Choose the next practice target

Use your one-, three- and five-minute results to choose one goal for the next month. If the short score is high but the five-minute result falls, practise endurance at a relaxed pace. If WPM is steady but accuracy is below your target, use short clean-copy sessions and review the common error type. If the work samples contain errors that tests did not show, include proofreading and data-entry practice in the routine.

The suggested beginner benchmark is 35 WPM at 95% accuracy, but starting points, keyboards, layouts and access needs vary. The course certificate test requirement is at least the learner's agreed personal target or 30 WPM at 95% accuracy, whichever is higher. A certificate score is not a promise of performance in a particular job. If the target is not met, document progress and agree a continued practice plan with the facilitator.

For a fair final comparison, use the same keyboard and scoring method you used for your earlier tests. If the final test has to use a different tool, label the result as a new baseline rather than claiming a direct improvement. Keep screenshots cropped to the result area and remove names, browser profiles or unrelated tabs before sharing them.

## Practice Exercises

1. Complete three one-minute intervals with two-minute accuracy periods between them.
2. Copy one paragraph while looking at the source and screen, not at the keyboard.
3. Type the supplied letter and compare each paragraph with the source.
4. Type the email and check names, times and punctuation.
5. Enter the product list into a spreadsheet and verify each price and item.
6. Transcribe a self-recorded fictional voice note and mark uncertainty honestly.
7. Take the final one-, three- and five-minute tests and record the method.
8. Draft a one-sentence CV statement with the date and test conditions.

## Weekly Assignment (Due at Course End)

Submit three timed test results (one, three and five minutes), a formal letter, a business email, a one-page product list and a transcription of your own voice note. Include the practice log and a brief note describing your method, remaining improvement goal and how you checked the work samples. The certificate target is the learner's agreed personal goal or 30 WPM at 95% accuracy, whichever is higher; report results honestly even if that threshold is not reached.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Test results and improvement evidence | 40% |
| Work-typing quality and completeness | 40% |
| Practice log and reflection | 20% |

**Definition of done**

- [ ] All three test durations are recorded with accuracy and method.
- [ ] The letter, email and product list are readable and proofread.
- [ ] The transcription uses self-created audio and flags uncertainty.
- [ ] The CV speed statement is based on a real recent test.
- [ ] A 15-minute maintenance routine is written down.

## Common Mistakes

- Sprinting through all tests and confusing fatigue with skill.
- Memorising a test passage instead of testing typing technique.
- Claiming mobile typing as physical-keyboard WPM.
- Guessing a word during transcription instead of marking it uncertain.
- Reporting only the fastest test without duration or accuracy.
- Treating a course target as an employer-wide requirement.

## Facilitator Notes

Offer a private testing space where practical. Explain each score method before the test and let learners take a short warm-up. Grade the portfolio against the stated rubric, not against one another's speed.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why do short speed intervals alternate with slower accuracy work?
2. What details make a typing test result understandable and honest?
3. How should you handle an unclear word in a self-recorded transcription?
4. What is one sustainable way to maintain typing skills after Week 4?
