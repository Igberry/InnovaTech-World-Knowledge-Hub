**Goal:** This week AI stops being a toy and becomes a work tool. You'll use it on real tasks, break a job into
steps to see exactly where a human must check, and write up one repeatable workflow you could hand to someone
else. Mostly doing.

## What you'll be able to do

- Apply AI to common work tasks (spreadsheets, planning, drafting, light coding help)
- Break a task into steps and mark where AI helps and where a human must check
- Document a repeatable AI-assisted workflow others could follow
- Spot which steps could be automated later

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your AI chat assistant, a spreadsheet app, and the
  [AI workflow outline](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/ai-workflow-outline.md).
- Warm-up: think of **one task you do over and over** (a weekly update, a price list, a sign-up sheet). That's
  your workflow for this week - write it at the top of your notes.

## Try it 1 - Put AI on a real work task

**Why it matters:** beyond writing, AI speeds up spreadsheets, planning, and light coding - but you always test
the result on your real task.

**Do this now:**
1. Ask: *"Explain how to write a formula that adds up column B where column A says 'Paid'."*
2. Test it on a **small, safe copy** of your data, not the real thing.
3. Try a planning task too: *"Turn this goal into a step-by-step checklist."*

**You've got it when:** you've run an AI suggestion and confirmed it works on your own data.

> **Tip:** For spreadsheet or code help, always test on a small, safe copy first. A formula that looks right can
> still do the wrong thing on your actual data.

## Try it 2 - Break the task into steps

**Why it matters:** the key skill is seeing a job as **steps**, then deciding, for each one, whether AI helps and
where your judgement is non-negotiable.

**Do this now - map a task like "prepare the weekly update from our sales notes":**

| Step | AI helps? | Human check |
|---|---|---|
| Gather the week's notes | No (you do this) | - |
| Summarise the notes | Yes | Check against the notes for accuracy |
| Draft the update message | Yes | Edit into our voice; verify any numbers |
| Decide what to highlight | No (judgement) | You decide |
| Send it | No | You send |

Now draw the same table for *your* repeated task.

**You've got it when:** your table clearly shows where AI saves time and where a human must step in.

## Try it 3 - Document the workflow

**Why it matters:** writing it down turns a clever one-off into a reliable process you (or a teammate) can repeat.

**Do this now - fill the [workflow outline](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/ai-workflow-outline.md) with:**
1. The **job** it does and how often.
2. The **steps**, marking which use AI and which a human checks.
3. The **exact prompts** for the AI steps.
4. The **quality checks** before the result is "done".

**You've got it when:** someone else could follow your write-up and get the same good result.

## Try it 4 - Keep a human in charge

**Why it matters:** the best workflows build the checks in so they always happen. If you ship it, you own it.

**Do this now - add these checks into your workflow so they're never skipped:**
1. Verify facts, numbers, and links (the Week 5 routine).
2. Confirm no private or confidential data was used.
3. Read the result for sense, tone, and fit.
4. Add disclosure if it's expected.

**You've got it when:** your workflow can't reach "done" without the checks happening.

> **Important:** "The AI did it" is never an excuse for a mistake that goes out under your name. If you ship it,
> you own it. The checks are part of the job, not optional extras.

## Make it yours - spot what could be automated later

Once a workflow is clear and repeatable, parts of it can sometimes be **automated** to run with less manual
effort - the subject of the InnovaTech [Automation](/courses/) path. For now, notice which of your steps are so
repetitive and rule-based that automating them later would help. Documenting the workflow is the first step
toward that.

### Watch out for

- Using AI output (a formula, a snippet) without testing it on real data
- Not separating steps, so AI is trusted where human judgement was needed
- Skipping the built-in checks when busy
- Treating a one-off clever result as if it were a reliable process
- Putting confidential data into a tool to "speed up" a work task

## This week's build: one documented AI-assisted workflow

Take a real, repeatable task and turn it into a workflow you could hand off:

1. A completed **workflow outline**: the job, the steps, which use AI, and the human checks
2. The **exact prompts** for the AI steps, saved and reusable (in your prompt log)
3. One **worked run** of the workflow, showing the result and the checks you did
4. A note on which step could be **automated later**, and why

*Tip: pick a task you genuinely repeat, so the write-up saves you time every week from now on.*

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Workflow is clear, with steps and human checks well placed | 35% |
| Prompts are specific and reusable | 20% |
| A real run shows the workflow producing a checked result | 30% |
| Thoughtful note on future automation | 15% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] Workflow outline completed
- [ ] Reusable prompts included
- [ ] One real run with its result and checks
- [ ] An automation candidate identified with a reason

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Get AI to explain a small piece of code or a formula, then verify the explanation line by line.
- Save the exact prompts for your task to the prompt log so next week is one paste away.
- Draft an agenda or a plan from a brain-dump, then refine it to fit your real situation.

## Facilitator Notes

The two ideas to land: break tasks into steps, and keep a human in charge of checks. The step table is the core
exercise; do it live with a task learners recognise. Insist that AI-suggested formulas or code are tested on
safe sample data before use. Use this week to tee up the Automation course by naming which steps are automatable.
Keep reinforcing privacy and fact-checking. Keep tasks realistic and local.
