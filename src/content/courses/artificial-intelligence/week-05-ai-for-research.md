**Goal:** This week you'll use AI to get oriented on a topic fast, learn with it like a patient tutor, and run
a quick fact-check routine that saves your reputation. The highlight: catching a confident, plausible claim that
turns out to be false. Lots of doing.

## What you'll be able to do

- Use AI to get a fast, useful starting overview of a topic
- Run a four-step routine to verify any AI claim
- Use AI as a study aid without it becoming cheating
- Turn messy information into clear structure
- Know when to leave AI and go to primary sources

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your AI chat assistant, a web browser (to verify), and the
  [fact-check worksheet](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/fact-check-worksheet.md).
- Warm-up: pick a topic you're curious about and ask *"explain [topic] simply, with the key terms I should know."*
  Keep the answer - you'll verify parts of it shortly.

## Try it 1 - Use AI to start research, never to finish it

**Why it matters:** AI gets you oriented in minutes, but (from Week 1) its "facts" may be wrong, old, or
invented. It points you where to look; real sources confirm what's true.

**Do this now:**
1. Ask for an overview of your topic and the main questions to ask.
2. Make a short list of the **claims you'd need to verify** (facts, numbers, names, dates, links).
3. Notice how quickly you got oriented - and how none of it is proven yet.

**You've got it when:** you have a tidy overview *and* a list of things still to check.

> **Note:** Some AI tools can search the web and show sources. That helps, but you still check the sources
> actually say what the AI claims. A cited source is only useful if it is real and relevant.

## Try it 2 - Catch it being confidently wrong (the fun part)

**Why it matters:** this is the core skill of the whole course. A few minutes of checking stops you passing on
false information.

**Do this now - run the four-step routine on one claim:**
1. **Spot the risky bit:** a fact, number, quote, or link.
2. **Find an independent source:** search for it on a trusted, official, or well-known site.
3. **Check it directly:** open the link; confirm the quote exists word for word.
4. **Decide:** confirmed (use it), unconfirmed (don't present as fact), or wrong (correct or remove).

Bonus challenge: ask AI for *"three statistics about internet use in Bayelsa State, with sources"* and try to
verify each one. Note any you cannot confirm.

**You've got it when:** you've personally confirmed one claim and caught one you couldn't verify.

> **Warning:** "The AI said so" is never a source. The source is the real, checkable place the fact comes from.
> If it doesn't exist, treat the claim as unproven.

## Try it 3 - Learn with AI like a patient tutor

**Why it matters:** used honestly, AI helps you understand and practise faster - as long as you still do the
thinking.

**Do this now:**
1. *"Explain [topic] simply, with one example."*
2. *"Give me five practice questions on [topic]"* - answer them yourself, then ask it to mark you.
3. *"I think [X]. Is that right? Where am I going wrong?"*
4. *"Make a one-week study plan for [goal], 30 minutes a day"* - then adjust it to your real life.

**You've got it when:** you've learned something and can explain it without looking back.

> **Important:** Learning with AI only works if you still do the thinking. If you copy answers without
> understanding, you learn nothing and the gaps show later. Let AI explain; you do the practice.

## Try it 4 - Turn mess into order

**Why it matters:** these tasks are low-risk and save real time - you can see straight away if the result is right.

**Do this now:**
1. Paste a page of messy notes and ask for a clear outline or table.
2. Check nothing was invented or dropped.
3. Ask it to group a long list into sensible categories, or draft a checklist from a brain-dump.

**You've got it when:** you've organised something you actually needed sorted.

## Make it yours - know when AI is not enough

Go to primary or expert sources, not AI, when:

- The topic is **high-stakes** (health, money, legal, safety)
- You need **current, exact** facts (prices, laws, recent events)
- The answer must be **authoritative** (official rules, specifications)

AI can still help you prepare good questions for those sources. It just shouldn't be the final word.

### Watch out for

- Treating an AI overview as finished research
- Trusting AI-provided sources without opening them
- Using AI to produce work dishonestly instead of to learn
- Skipping the fact-check on "small" claims that turn out to matter
- Relying on AI for high-stakes or very recent information

## This week's build: a verified research summary

Pick a topic you care about and produce a summary you'd trust enough to share:

1. A **one-page summary** of the topic, started with AI and written in your own words
2. A **fact-check log**: at least **three claims** you verified, each with the source and verdict
3. One claim you found to be **wrong or unverifiable**, and how you handled it
4. A short note on **where you'd go beyond AI** for this topic, and why

*Tip: choose something local and useful - a market trend, a study topic, a how-to - so the summary earns its keep.*

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Summary is clear, useful, and in your own words | 25% |
| Three or more claims genuinely verified with real sources | 35% |
| A wrong/unverifiable claim caught and handled honestly | 20% |
| Sound judgement on AI's limits for the topic | 20% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] One-page summary in your own words
- [ ] At least three claims with sources and verdicts
- [ ] One caught error or unverifiable claim, handled
- [ ] A note on where primary sources are needed

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Open every link in one AI answer and tally how many were real and relevant.
- Ask AI to quiz you on a topic you're learning, then grade yourself honestly.
- Take a high-stakes question and list the primary sources you'd check instead of AI.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 min | Recap; "use AI to start research, not finish it" |
| 10-40 min | Live fact-check: take an AI answer and verify it as a group, step by step |
| 40-60 min | AI as study aid: generate practice questions; discuss honest use |
| 60-80 min | Organising a messy set of notes with AI |
| 80-90 min | When to go beyond AI; brief for Week 6 |

## Facilitator Notes

Fact-checking is the core skill this week and a highlight of the whole course. Run a live verification on an AI
answer that contains a plausible but false claim or a fake source; the moment learners catch it is the lesson.
Stress honest study use (AI to learn, not to cheat) and the high-stakes exceptions. Make sure learners actually
open links rather than trusting citations. Keep topics local and practical.
