**Goal:** In about two hours, get everything ready so Week 1 is pure hands-on. You'll create two free AI
accounts, set up a tidy place to save your work, and lock in one privacy habit that keeps you safe. Almost no
reading - you set it up by doing it.

## What you'll be able to do

- Log in to a free AI chat assistant and a free AI image tool
- Say, in one line, what you must never paste into an AI tool
- Save prompts and files so nothing gets lost
- Use AI on limited power and data without stress

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open a web browser and have a working email address ready.
- Warm-up: on paper or in a note, write the one task you most want AI to help you with this course. Keep it -
  you'll test it in Week 1.

## Set it up 1 - Create your two accounts

**Why it matters:** this whole course is about using AI, not building it. Two free tools cover everything.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a free account on **one AI chat assistant**. Use a professional email and a strong password.
2. Create a free account on **one AI image tool** (you mainly need this from Week 4).
3. Turn on two-step verification if it is offered.
4. Open the [tool comparison worksheet](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/ai-tool-comparison.md) and jot down
   what you chose and why.

**You've got it when:** you can open both tools and send one test message in the chat assistant.

> **Note:** You never need to pay for anything in this course. Free tiers have daily limits (a number of
> messages or images), so plan your practice around them. Do not share logins in ways that break a tool's rules.

## Set it up 2 - Learn the one privacy habit (the part that protects you)

**Why it matters:** AI tools can store what you type. Assume anything you paste could be seen by someone else.

**Do this now:**
1. Say this rule out loud: *"When in doubt, leave it out."*
2. Make a short "never paste" list in your notes: passwords, bank or payment details, ID numbers, and anyone
   else's private information.
3. Quick test: think of something sensitive you might have pasted, then write how you would **strip or fake the
   details** first (change real names and numbers).
4. Skim the [responsible-use checklist](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/responsible-use-privacy-checklist.md)
   so you know what is in it.

**You've got it when:** you can name three things you will never paste into an AI tool without looking.

> **Privacy:** A simple test - would you be happy if what you are about to paste showed up on a public website?
> If not, do not paste it, or remove the sensitive parts first.

## Set it up 3 - Build a tidy workspace

**Why it matters:** the people who get the most from AI keep their best prompts and files one click away.

**Do this now:**
1. Make one **document** called "Prompts that work" to collect prompts (or use the Week 2 prompt log).
2. Make one **folder** for images and files you create.
3. Bookmark both AI tools in your browser.

**You've got it when:** your prompt document and your folder both exist and are easy to find.

## Set it up 4 - Plan for power and data

**Why it matters:** in real life you work around patchy power and limited data. A small plan saves your work.

**Do this now:**
1. Charge your laptop and phone when you can, and keep a power bank handy. Save your work often.
2. Note that text chat uses little data, while generating images uses more - do the heavy image tasks when
   your data is healthy.
3. Plan to read, draft, and prepare prompts offline, then use the tools in short, focused bursts.

**You've got it when:** you have a one-line plan for what you do on low power or low data.

## One more habit: never trust blindly

AI tools can sound completely confident and still be wrong. From day one, treat every answer as a **draft to
check**, not a fact. You'll build a simple fact-checking routine in Weeks 5 and 7.

> **Warning:** AI tools "hallucinate" - they invent facts, sources, and quotes that look real. This is normal
> and expected. Your job is to catch it. Never pass on an AI's facts, numbers, or links without checking them.
