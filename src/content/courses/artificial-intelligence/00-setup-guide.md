**Goal:** Set up the free AI tools you will use in this course, learn the privacy basics that keep you safe,
and make a plan for using AI on limited power and data.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this setup you can:

- Create a free account on an AI chat assistant and an AI image tool
- Explain the privacy basics: what not to put into an AI tool, and why
- Set up a simple way to save prompts and track your work
- Make a plan for using AI on limited power and data

## 1. The kinds of AI tool you will use

This course is about using AI, not building it. You will mainly use two kinds of tool. Both have free ways
to start. Tools and free tiers change often, so **check the current details on each tool's own website**.

| Tool type | What it does | How you will use it |
|---|---|---|
| AI chat assistant | Answers questions, writes, summarises, explains, plans | Most weeks |
| AI image tool | Creates and edits images from a description | Week 4 |

> **Note:** You do not need to pay for anything to do this course. Where a free tier has limits (a number of
> messages or images per day), plan your practice around them. Do not share account logins in ways that break
> a tool's rules.

## 2. Create your accounts

- Create a free account on **one AI chat assistant**. Use a professional email.
- Create a free account on **one AI image tool** (you will mainly need this from Week 4).
- Turn on any security options offered (a strong password, two-step verification).

Use the [tool comparison worksheet](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/ai-tool-comparison.md) to record what
you chose and why. Do not sign up for many tools at once; one of each is plenty to start.

## 3. Privacy basics (read before you type anything into an AI tool)

This is the most important part of the setup. AI tools can store what you type. Before you paste anything in:

- **Never** paste passwords, bank or payment details, or ID numbers.
- **Never** paste someone else's private information without their consent.
- **Never** paste confidential or client work you are not allowed to share.
- If the information is sensitive but you still need help, **remove or change the identifying details** first
  (use fake names and numbers).

> **Privacy:** Assume anything you type into an AI tool could be stored or seen by others. When in doubt,
> leave it out. You will use the [responsible-use checklist](/downloads/artificial-intelligence/responsible-use-privacy-checklist.md)
> throughout the course.

## 4. One more habit: never trust blindly

AI tools can sound completely confident and still be wrong. From day one, treat every answer as a **draft to
check**, not a fact. You will learn a simple fact-checking routine in Weeks 5 and 7.

> **Warning:** AI tools "hallucinate" - they invent facts, sources, and quotes that look real. This is normal
> and expected. Your job is to catch it. Never pass on an AI's facts, numbers, or links without checking them.

## 5. Set up a simple workspace

- A **document** to collect prompts that work (or use the prompt log from Week 2)
- A **folder** for images and files you create
- Bookmark your AI tools so they are one click away

## 6. Plan for power and data

AI tools need the internet, and some use a fair amount of data. Plan around it:

- **Power:** charge your laptop and phone when you can; keep a power bank; save your work often.
- **Data:** text chat uses little data; generating images uses more. Do the heavy tasks when your data is
  healthy, and the reading and planning when it is low.
- **Offline:** you can plan prompts, read lessons, and draft by hand offline, then use the tools in short,
  focused bursts.

## 7. Setup checklist

- [ ] An AI chat assistant account created
- [ ] An AI image tool account created
- [ ] I have read and understood the privacy basics
- [ ] A document or folder ready to save prompts and files
- [ ] A power-and-data plan

## Self-Check Questions

1. Name three things you must never paste into an AI tool.
2. Why should you treat every AI answer as a draft to check, not a fact?
3. Which uses more data: text chat or generating images, and how will you plan around that?
