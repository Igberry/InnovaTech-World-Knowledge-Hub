**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have a published landing page, built free, with one headline, one offer, and one call to action, plus proof from a real 5-second clarity test that a stranger understood it. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Write a headline that says what you offer and who it helps
- Sketch a one-screen wireframe before you build
- Write a single, strong call to action
- Build and publish a landing page using only free tools
- Run a 5-second clarity test and act on what you learn

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your offer sheet and your Week 1 journey doc.
- Warm-up: open any three websites you like and, for each, read only the first screen. Write one line on whether you instantly understood what they offer. Most landing pages fail this, and yours will not.

## Try it 1 - Write the headline

**Why it matters:** the headline is the one thing every visitor reads. A clear headline that names the benefit and the audience keeps people on the page. A clever but vague one loses them.

**Do this now:**
1. Fill this in: "[Main benefit] for [who it is for]." Example: "Home-cooked meals delivered, for busy families in Lagos."
2. Write two more versions, changing the wording.
3. Read all three aloud and pick the one a stranger would understand fastest.

**You've got it when:** you have one headline that names both the benefit and the audience in plain words.

## Try it 2 - Sketch a one-screen wireframe

**Why it matters:** a wireframe is a rough box-layout you draw before building. It stops you fiddling with colours before the structure is right.

**Do this now, on paper or in a blank Canva frame, stack these boxes top to bottom:**
1. Headline box at the top.
2. One line of supporting text under it.
3. One image box (a photo of the product or a happy customer).
4. Three short benefit bullets.
5. One button box with your call to action.

**You've got it when:** you have a simple boxes-only sketch of one screen, no real design yet, in the order above.

## Try it 3 - Write one strong call to action

**Why it matters:** a page with many choices gets no action. One clear action, written as a command starting with a verb, lifts results.

**Do this now:**
1. Write your button text as an action: "Order my first meal", "Book a free call", "Get the price list".
2. Make it about the visitor's gain, not your work. "Send message" is weak, "Get my free quote" is strong.
3. Delete any second or third action. One page, one job.

**You've got it when:** your page has exactly one call to action, written as a benefit-led command.

## Try it 4 - Build the page for free

**Why it matters:** you do not need a developer or hosting. A free Canva website or a styled Google Form can collect real leads today.

**Do this now, choose one route and build your wireframe into it:**
1. **Canva website:** open Canva, search "Website", pick a one-page template, and replace every bit of text and the image with yours. Then click "Publish website" and choose the free option.
2. **Or Google Form:** open https://forms.google.com, put your headline as the form title, your supporting line as the description, add a header image, and add two or three short questions ending with "How can we reach you?".
3. Copy the live link when it is published.

**You've got it when:** you have a public link that opens your headline, offer, and one call to action on a real page.

## Try it 5 - Run a 5-second clarity test

**Why it matters:** you are too close to your own page to judge it. A fresh person reading for 5 seconds tells you the truth about clarity.

**Do this now:**
1. Show the top of your page to a friend for 5 seconds, then hide it.
2. Ask three questions: "What do we offer? Who is it for? What should you do next?"
3. Write down their exact answers.

**You've got it when:** you have a real person's answers to all three questions written down.

## Try it 6 - Fix the weakest part

**Why it matters:** a test is only useful if you act on it. The point they got wrong is the part to rewrite.

**Do this now:**
1. Compare their answers to what you meant. Circle the one they got wrong or were unsure about.
2. Rewrite only that element (usually the headline or the button) to be plainer.
3. Re-test on a second person to confirm the fix.

**You've got it when:** a second person answers all three questions correctly after your edit.

## Weekly Assignment

Build and submit one published landing page for your real offer, plus a short test report. Submit:

1. The live link to your published page (Canva website or Google Form).
2. Your three headline options with the chosen one marked.
3. Your one call to action, written as a benefit-led command.
4. A short test report: the two testers' exact answers to the three questions, what you changed, and the result of the re-test.

Done looks like: a working public link where a stranger instantly sees what you offer, who it helps, and what to do next, backed by evidence that two real people understood it.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Recap funnels; why the landing page is where the "lead" stage is won or lost |
| 15-40 min | Headline clinic: rewrite three vague headlines into clear benefit-led ones together |
| 40-70 min | Build time: each learner assembles their page live while the mentor circulates |
| 70-90 min | Run live 5-second tests across the room; set the assignment and brief Week 3 |

## Facilitator Notes

The biggest trap is learners polishing colours and fonts before the message is clear, so hold them on the wireframe and headline until those are strong. Many will try to add several calls to action; insist on one. The 5-second test often surprises people, which is the point, so make sure every learner tests on a real stranger, not a classmate who already knows their offer. Keep tools free and simple; a Google Form page is a perfectly valid landing page for a beginner.

2. Why should a landing page have only one call to action?
3. What does a 5-second clarity test tell you, and how do you run one?
