**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have navigated a real GA4 account, pulled your top traffic sources, read a weekly snapshot, and written one specific decision based on what the numbers say. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Find your way around the main GA4 reports without getting lost
- Identify your top traffic sources and what they mean
- See or mark a conversion or key event
- Read a simple weekly report and say what changed
- Turn one data point into one concrete decision

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com) and sign in with a free Google account.
- If you do not run a website yet, open the GA4 demo account that Google provides for practice (search "GA4 demo account" in Google's help), or use any site you help manage.
- Warm-up: in a document, write the one question you most want data to answer, for example "where do my visitors come from?"

## Try it 1 - Explore the main reports

**Why it matters:** GA4 looks busy at first. Once you know the four or five reports that matter, the rest is noise you can ignore.

**Do this now:**
1. In the left menu, open Reports.
2. Click into Life cycle, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition.
3. Open Engagement, then Pages and screens.
4. Open Reports snapshot at the top and read the summary cards.
5. In your document, write one line on what each of these three reports tells you.

**You've got it when:** you can open Traffic acquisition, Pages and screens, and the Reports snapshot without hunting through menus.

## Try it 2 - Find your top traffic sources

**Why it matters:** knowing where people come from tells you which channels to do more of and which to drop. This is the most useful question analytics answers.

**Do this now:**
1. Open Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition.
2. Set the date range at the top right to the last 28 days.
3. Look at the Session default channel group column: Organic Search, Direct, Social, Referral, and so on.
4. Sort by sessions and write down your top three sources in your document.
5. Note which source brings the most engaged sessions, not just the most visits.

**You've got it when:** you can name your top three traffic sources and say which one brings the most engaged visitors.

## Try it 3 - See a conversion or key event

**Why it matters:** visits do not pay the bills, actions do. A key event (a signup, a purchase, a contact click) is what you actually want more of.

**Do this now:**
1. Open Reports, then Engagement, then Events to see what is being tracked.
2. Open the Key events report (older accounts call these Conversions) to see which events are marked as important.
3. If you own the property and nothing is marked, open Admin, then Events, and toggle one event, such as a form submit or a click, to mark it as a key event.
4. In your document, write which action you would most want to count as a key event and why.

**You've got it when:** you can point to at least one key event in the account and explain what real action it represents.

## Try it 4 - Read a simple weekly report

**Why it matters:** a report you read every week is worth more than a perfect dashboard you never open. Simple and regular beats complex and ignored.

**Do this now:**
1. Open Reports snapshot and set the date range to the last 7 days.
2. Write down four numbers: total users, sessions, key events, and engagement rate.
3. Change the date range to the 7 days before that and note the same four numbers.
4. In your document, write whether each number went up or down.

**You've got it when:** you have a simple four-line weekly report comparing this week to last week.

## Try it 5 - Compare two time periods

**Why it matters:** a single number means little. "Up or down from last period" is what tells you if your work is paying off.

**Do this now:**
1. In any report, find the date range and turn on Compare.
2. Set it to compare the last 28 days with the previous 28 days.
3. Read the percentage change next to each number (green up, red down).
4. Pick the one metric with the biggest change and write one sentence on a likely reason.

**You've got it when:** you can read a period-over-period percentage change and name one plausible reason for the biggest mover.

## Try it 6 - Decide one concrete change

**Why it matters:** data is only useful if it changes what you do. The whole point of measuring is to make one better decision.

**Do this now:**
1. Look back at your top traffic sources from Try it 2.
2. Pick the source that brings engaged visitors cheaply or for free (often Organic or Social).
3. Write one specific action to do more of it this month, for example "post twice a week on the channel that sends engaged visitors".
4. Write one thing you will stop or shrink because the data shows it does little.

**You've got it when:** you have written one clear "do more of this" and one "do less of this", each tied to a number you saw.

## Weekly Assignment

Produce a one-page weekly analytics report with a decision.

Steps:
1. Capture a screenshot of your Traffic acquisition report showing your top three sources.
2. Write a simple weekly snapshot: users, sessions, key events, and engagement rate, this week versus last week.
3. Identify one key event in the account and explain what action it counts.
4. State one concrete change you will make and the number that led you to it.
5. Keep the whole thing to a single page that a busy manager could read in two minutes.

Done looks like: one screenshot, a four-number week-over-week snapshot, a named key event, and one data-backed decision, all on a single page.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Why measuring beats guessing; the few reports that matter |
| 15-45 min | Live tour: everyone opens Traffic acquisition and reads their top sources together |
| 45-70 min | Key events and comparisons: mark an event, compare two periods, read the change |
| 70-90 min | Each learner states one decision from their data; brief next week's capstone |

## Facilitator Notes

Learners without their own website should use Google's GA4 demo account so everyone has real data to explore. The most common confusion is treating sessions as success; keep steering them back to engaged sessions and key events. Many will struggle to find the Compare toggle, so demonstrate it live. The real skill to assess is not navigation but whether each learner turns a number into one specific, sensible decision.

## Can you do these now?

1. Which report shows where your visitors come from, and why do engaged sessions matter more than raw visits?
2. What is a key event, and why is it more important than total sessions?
3. Why is comparing this period to the last one more useful than a single number on its own?
