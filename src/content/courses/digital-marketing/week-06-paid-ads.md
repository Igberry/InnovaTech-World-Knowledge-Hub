**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have a complete ad built in DRAFT that you could launch with one click, plus a written set of rules that keep you from wasting money. You learn by doing, not reading. You will not spend any money this week.

## What you'll be able to do

- Pick one clear objective for an ad and explain why
- Build a basic Meta or Google ad in draft, without spending
- Define a target audience and set a tiny, safe budget
- Write ad copy and choose a creative that match the objective
- Read cost-per-result and judge if an ad is working
- Apply simple rules that stop beginners from wasting money

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open an ad account. Use [Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/tools/ads-manager) if you sell through Facebook or Instagram, or [Google Ads](https://ads.google.com) if people search for what you offer.
- Make sure you are in draft mode and that no payment method is set to charge automatically. We stay in draft all week.
- Warm-up: in a document, write one sentence on what you want more of (messages, website visits, calls, or sales). That is your objective in plain words.

## Try it 1 - Pick one objective

**Why it matters:** an ad that tries to do everything does nothing. The platform optimises for the one objective you choose, so choosing well is most of the job.

**Do this now:**
1. Write down what success looks like in one line, for example "more WhatsApp messages about my catering service".
2. Match it to a platform objective: Engagement or Leads for messages, Traffic for website visits, Sales for purchases.
3. Pick exactly one and write why in one sentence.

**You've got it when:** you have one objective written down and can say what single result you want from the ad.

## Try it 2 - Start the ad in draft

**Why it matters:** building the real thing in draft teaches you the actual screens without risking a single naira. Most beginners learn faster this way than from any tutorial.

**Do this now:**
1. In your ad tool, click to create a new campaign and choose the objective from Try it 1.
2. Name the campaign clearly, for example "Catering - Messages - Nov".
3. Stop at the point where it asks you to confirm or publish. Do not publish.
4. Confirm the status shows Draft or In review, not Active.

**You've got it when:** you have a named campaign sitting in draft with your chosen objective and nothing is live.

## Try it 3 - Define your audience

**Why it matters:** showing your ad to the wrong people wastes your whole budget. A tight, sensible audience is how a small budget still works.

**Do this now:**
1. Set the location to where you actually serve, for example Yenagoa and nearby, not the whole country.
2. Set an age range that fits your real customers.
3. Add one or two interests or search terms that match what you sell.
4. Write your audience in one line in your document, for example "Adults 25-45 in Bayelsa interested in events and catering".

**You've got it when:** your audience is specific enough that you could describe a single real person it would reach.

## Try it 4 - Set a tiny budget

**Why it matters:** you learn with small money. A tiny daily budget lets you test, read the numbers, and stop before any mistake gets expensive.

**Do this now:**
1. Choose a daily budget you would not mind losing entirely, for example a small fixed amount per day.
2. Set a start and end date just a few days apart so the test cannot run forever.
3. Check the estimated reach the tool shows for that budget and audience.
4. Note in your document the budget, the dates, and the estimated reach.

**You've got it when:** your draft has a small daily budget and a fixed short end date, so it can never overspend.

## Try it 5 - Write copy and choose a creative

**Why it matters:** the audience decides in under two seconds. A clear image and a short, specific message beat a clever one almost every time.

**Do this now:**
1. Write a one-line headline that names the benefit, for example "Fresh party trays, delivered in Yenagoa".
2. Write two short body lines: what you offer and one clear call to action ("Message us to book").
3. Choose one creative: a clear photo of the real product or a simple graphic made in [Canva](https://www.canva.com).
4. Add the creative and copy to your draft ad and preview how it looks on a phone.

**You've got it when:** your draft shows a readable phone preview with a clear benefit and one call to action.

## Try it 6 - Read cost-per-result

**Why it matters:** cost-per-result is the one number that tells you if an ad is worth it. Without it, you are guessing.

**Do this now:**
1. In your document, write the formula: cost-per-result equals amount spent divided by number of results.
2. Work a practice example: if an ad spends a small amount and brings 10 messages, what is the cost per message?
3. Decide your own ceiling: the most you would pay for one result and still make a profit.
4. Write one sentence on what you would do if the real cost-per-result came back double your ceiling.

**You've got it when:** you can calculate cost-per-result from spend and results, and you have named your own ceiling.

## Weekly Assignment

Produce one complete, launch-ready ad in draft plus a money-safety plan.

Steps:
1. Build a draft ad with one objective, a specific audience, a tiny budget, short copy, and one creative.
2. Capture screenshots of the draft showing the objective, the audience, the budget, and the phone preview.
3. Calculate the cost-per-result for a worked example and state your profit ceiling.
4. Write a list of at least six rules to avoid wasting money (for example: start small, one objective per ad, kill ads above the ceiling, never publish without a preview, check the audience is not too broad, set an end date).
5. Confirm in writing that nothing was published and no money was spent.

Done looks like: draft screenshots, a cost-per-result calculation with a ceiling, a six-rule money-safety list, and a clear statement that the ad stayed in draft.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Why most ad spend is wasted; the one-objective rule |
| 15-45 min | Live build: everyone creates a draft campaign and defines an audience together |
| 45-70 min | Copy and creative clinic: review ads on a phone preview, cut anything unclear |
| 70-90 min | Work cost-per-result examples; set personal ceilings; brief next week on analytics |

## Facilitator Notes

The biggest risk this week is a learner accidentally publishing a live ad, so check everyone's status stays in draft and that no auto-charging payment method is active. Beginners pick audiences that are far too broad; push them toward a specific place and interest. Many will struggle with cost-per-result maths, so work several examples slowly as a group. Reassure learners that spending real money is not required to pass; a well-built draft and a clear reading of the numbers is the goal.

## Can you do these now?

1. Why should an ad have only one objective, and how does that choice change what the platform does?
2. How do you calculate cost-per-result, and why is it the number that decides if an ad is worth keeping?
3. Name three rules that stop a beginner from wasting money on ads.
