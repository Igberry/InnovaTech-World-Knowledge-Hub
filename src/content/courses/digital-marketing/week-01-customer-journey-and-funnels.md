**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have a written customer journey for one real product, a drawn funnel with numbers at each stage, a marked leak point, and a one-action plan for each stage. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Map the awareness, consideration, and decision stages for a real product
- Draw a simple funnel and put a rough number on each stage
- Spot the stage where you lose the most people
- Match one concrete marketing action to each stage

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your offer sheet from the setup week and a blank Google Doc titled "Week 1 Journey".
- Warm-up: think of the last thing you bought online and write the three or four steps you went through, from first hearing about it to paying. That is a customer journey, and you just did one from memory.

## Try it 1 - Map one real journey in three stages

**Why it matters:** strangers do not buy on first sight. They move through awareness (they notice a problem), consideration (they compare options), and decision (they choose). If you know the stage someone is in, you know what to say to them.

**Do this now, for the product on your offer sheet:**
1. Write a heading "Awareness" and note how a stranger first realises they have the problem your offer solves.
2. Write "Consideration" and list two or three things they do to compare their options (search Google, ask friends, read reviews).
3. Write "Decision" and note the last thing that makes them choose: a price, a guarantee, a testimonial, a quick reply to a message.

**You've got it when:** your doc shows three named stages, each with at least one real sentence about what the person is thinking or doing.

## Try it 2 - Draw the funnel with numbers

**Why it matters:** a funnel shows that more people enter at the top than reach the bottom. Putting numbers on it turns a vague feeling into something you can improve.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, search "flowchart" or just stack four boxes top to bottom labelled Visitors, Leads, Customers, Repeat customers.
2. Put a rough monthly number in each box. If you have no data, estimate: for example 1,000 visitors, 100 leads, 10 customers, 3 repeat.
3. Make the top box wide and each lower box narrower, so it looks like a funnel.

**You've got it when:** you have a four-stage funnel image with a number in every box, widest at the top.

## Try it 3 - Work out the drop at each step

**Why it matters:** the percentage that moves from one stage to the next is your conversion rate. The worst one is where you are losing the most money.

**Do this now:**
1. Divide Leads by Visitors and write it as a percent (100 / 1,000 = 10%).
2. Divide Customers by Leads (10 / 100 = 10%).
3. Divide Repeat by Customers (3 / 10 = 30%).
4. Write all three percentages next to the arrows on your funnel.

**You've got it when:** every arrow on your funnel has a conversion percentage next to it.

## Try it 4 - Find the leak

**Why it matters:** fixing your biggest leak gives the fastest result. Chasing more visitors when your leak is further down just wastes money.

**Do this now:**
1. Look at your three percentages and circle the lowest one.
2. Write one sentence about why people might be dropping out there. For example, "Only 10% of leads buy because the price is not clear and nobody follows up."
3. Write one thing you could test to fix it.

**You've got it when:** you have circled the weakest step and written a plausible reason plus one fix to test.

## Try it 5 - Match one action to each stage

**Why it matters:** different stages need different marketing. An advert that works for awareness is wasted on someone ready to decide. Matching the action to the stage is the heart of digital marketing.

**Do this now, add an "Actions" column next to your journey:**
1. **Awareness:** pick one action that helps strangers notice you (a helpful social post, an SEO blog, a short video).
2. **Consideration:** pick one that helps them compare and trust you (a comparison page, reviews, a free sample or demo).
3. **Decision:** pick one that removes the final doubt (a clear call to action, a guarantee, an easy way to pay or book).

**You've got it when:** each of your three stages has exactly one real marketing action written next to it.

## Try it 6 - Write the one-line story

**Why it matters:** if you can say the whole journey in one sentence, you understand it. This sentence guides every decision in later weeks.

**Do this now:**
1. Fill in the blanks: "A [who] who feels [problem] finds us through [awareness action], decides we are right because of [consideration action], and buys when we offer [decision action]."
2. Read it aloud. Fix anything that sounds unlikely.

**You've got it when:** you have one clear sentence that tells the whole journey from stranger to customer.

## Weekly Assignment

Produce a one-page "Customer Journey and Funnel" brief for your real offer and submit it. It must contain:

1. The three-stage journey (awareness, consideration, decision) with a real sentence under each.
2. Your funnel image with a number in every stage and a conversion percentage on every arrow.
3. The circled leak, a one-sentence reason, and one fix you would test.
4. One marketing action matched to each of the three stages.
5. Your one-line journey story.

Done looks like: a single page (or one doc plus the Canva funnel image) that a stranger could read in two minutes and understand exactly how you turn a visitor into a customer and where you currently lose people.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Welcome; each learner says their offer in one sentence |
| 15-45 min | Build journeys together: map awareness, consideration, decision for two real offers on the board |
| 45-75 min | Draw funnels with numbers; calculate conversion rates; hunt for each learner's leak |
| 75-90 min | Match actions to stages; set the weekly assignment and brief Week 2 |

## Facilitator Notes

Learners often confuse the funnel stages, treating "leads" and "customers" as the same thing, so define each one with a concrete action (a lead gave you their contact, a customer paid). Many have no real numbers yet; let them estimate and stress that a rough funnel beats no funnel. The most common mistake is wanting more traffic when the real leak is lower down, so push them to fix the weakest conversion first. Keep every example tied to a real local offer so the journey feels concrete.

## Can you do these now?

1. What are the three stages of a customer journey, and what is the person thinking in each?
2. How do you calculate the conversion rate between two funnel stages?
3. Why is fixing your biggest leak usually better than finding more visitors?
