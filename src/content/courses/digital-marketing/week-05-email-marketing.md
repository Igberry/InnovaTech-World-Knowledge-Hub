**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have a working email list, a live signup form, a welcome email, a 3-email sequence, and five tested subject lines. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Set up a free email list and a signup form that collects consent
- Write a welcome email that actually gets opened and read
- Draft a simple 3-email sequence that moves a contact toward a sale
- Write and compare subject lines, and read open and click rates
- Follow the basic permission and anti-spam rules so you stay out of trouble

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open a free email tool. We use [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com) in this lesson because the free plan is generous, but Brevo or Mailchimp work the same way.
- Create your free account and confirm your email address.
- Warm-up: in a document, write one sentence describing the business you will send emails for. It can be real (your shop, a side hustle) or a practice one like a Yenagoa catering service. You will use this all week.

## Try it 1 - Create your list and a signup form

**Why it matters:** email marketing starts with a list people chose to join. Buying contacts or adding people without asking gets you blocked and breaks the law in many places.

**Do this now:**
1. In your email tool, create a new group or list and name it after your business.
2. Build a signup form: ask only for first name and email address, nothing else yet.
3. Add one short line of consent text, for example "Yes, send me tips and offers by email. You can unsubscribe anytime."
4. Publish the form and copy its link. Open the link in your phone browser to see what a subscriber sees.

**You've got it when:** you can open your own signup form on your phone, enter a test email, and see that address appear in your list.

## Try it 2 - Write a welcome email

**Why it matters:** the welcome email gets the highest open rate you will ever get. It sets the tone and tells people what they just signed up for.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a new email in your tool called "Welcome".
2. Write a short greeting that uses the first name, for example "Hi {$name}, welcome."
3. In three short sentences, say who you are, what they will get, and how often.
4. Add one clear next step, such as "Reply and tell me what you are working on" or a link to your best page.
5. Send a test to yourself and read it on your phone.

**You've got it when:** you have a welcome email under 120 words that reads well on a phone and has one clear next step.

## Try it 3 - Draft a 3-email sequence

**Why it matters:** one email rarely sells. A short sequence builds trust step by step, so the ask at the end does not feel pushy.

**Do this now, write three short drafts in your document:**
1. Email 1 (day 0): the welcome. Deliver what you promised and set expectations.
2. Email 2 (day 2): be useful. Share one helpful tip, story, or short guide with no hard selling.
3. Email 3 (day 4): make one clear offer. Name the product or service, the price or next step, and a reason to act now.
4. For each email, write the subject line and the first line only once the body is done.

**You've got it when:** you can read all three in order and each one has a single clear purpose, ending with one real offer.

## Try it 4 - Write five subject lines and pick the best

**Why it matters:** if the subject line fails, nothing else matters because the email is never opened.

**Do this now:**
1. For your Email 3 offer, write five different subject lines in your document.
2. Make them different on purpose: one short and plain, one a question, one with a number, one that names a benefit, one with light curiosity.
3. Keep each under 50 characters so it is not cut off on a phone.
4. Read them aloud and delete any that sound like spam ("FREE!!!", "ACT NOW").
5. Circle the one you would open and write one sentence on why.

**You've got it when:** you have five honest subject lines and a clear, reasoned pick for the best one.

## Try it 5 - Understand open rate and click rate

**Why it matters:** these two numbers tell you if people are opening (subject line working) and acting (content and offer working). You cannot improve what you do not measure.

**Do this now:**
1. Send your welcome email as a real campaign to your own test list of 2 to 3 addresses (ask a friend or use a second email).
2. Wait a few hours, then open the campaign report in your tool.
3. Find the open rate (opened divided by delivered) and the click rate (clicked divided by delivered).
4. In your document, write one sentence on what a low open rate would tell you, and one on what a low click rate would tell you.

**You've got it when:** you can point to the open rate and click rate in your report and explain what each one measures.

## Try it 6 - Pass the permission and anti-spam check

**Why it matters:** sending to people who did not ask, or hiding the unsubscribe link, gets you marked as spam and can be illegal. Clean habits protect your business.

**Do this now, confirm each item for your emails:**
1. Everyone on the list gave clear consent through your form.
2. Every email shows a real sender name and a reply address that works.
3. Every email has a visible, one-click unsubscribe link (your tool adds this automatically, so check it is on).
4. Your emails include your business name or a contact, so people know who is writing.
5. You never bought a list or added people from your phone contacts without asking.

**You've got it when:** you can tick all five items honestly for your own sequence.

## Weekly Assignment

Build and submit a complete starter email campaign for one business.

Steps:
1. Set up a live signup form with consent text and share the link.
2. Write a welcome email and a 3-email sequence (welcome, useful, offer), all under 150 words each.
3. For the offer email, include five subject lines and mark your chosen one with a one-sentence reason.
4. Send the sequence to a small test list (2 to 3 addresses) and capture a screenshot of the report showing open and click rates.
5. Write three sentences on what the numbers tell you and one change you would make.

Done looks like: a shared signup form link, four written emails, five subject lines with a pick, one report screenshot, and your three-sentence read of the results.

## Facilitator Notes

Beginners often want to write long, salesy emails; push them toward short and useful, with only one ask per email. The most common mistake is adding contacts without consent, so stress permission early and often. Many learners cannot send a real test because they have only one email address; have them pair up to test each other's sequences. If a tool's free-plan limits block sending, a detailed screenshot of the drafted campaign is an acceptable substitute.

## Can you do these now?

1. Why must every contact on your list have given consent, and what two things must every email include by law or good practice?
2. What is the single job of a welcome email, and what should its one next step be?
3. What do open rate and click rate each measure, and what would a high open rate but low click rate suggest?
