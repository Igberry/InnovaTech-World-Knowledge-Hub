**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have run one small end-to-end campaign for real, one goal, one audience, two channels, a tiny budget, measured over a week, and presented the results. You learn by doing, not reading. This is where everything from Weeks 1 to 7 comes together.

## What you'll be able to do

- Set one clear campaign goal and define the audience
- Choose two channels and create the assets for each
- Set a tiny budget and run the campaign for a week
- Measure results with the numbers that matter
- Present the plan, the work, and the results as a portfolio piece

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open the tools you will reuse: [Canva](https://www.canva.com) for assets, your email tool from Week 5, and [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com) from Week 7.
- Gather your best work from earlier weeks: a page, a subject line, an audience definition, an ad draft.
- Warm-up: in a document, write the one real result you want from this campaign in a single sentence.

## Try it 1 - Pick one goal and audience

**Why it matters:** a small campaign with one goal and a clear audience can actually succeed in a week. A big vague one cannot.

**Do this now:**
1. Write one measurable goal, for example "get 20 new email signups" or "get 15 WhatsApp enquiries".
2. Describe the audience in one line: who they are, where, and what they want.
3. Set a start and end date exactly one week apart.
4. Check the goal is realistic for a week on a tiny budget.

**You've got it when:** you can state your goal, audience, and one-week dates in three short lines.

## Try it 2 - Choose two channels

**Why it matters:** two channels working together beat one channel shouting alone. One brings attention, the other captures and follows up.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick one channel to reach people: a social post, a small ad, or outreach in a group.
2. Pick one channel to capture and nurture: an email signup form or a landing page.
3. Write in one line how the two connect, for example "social post sends people to the signup form, then the welcome email follows up".
4. Confirm you can actually publish on both this week.

**You've got it when:** you have two named channels and a clear path from one to the other.

## Try it 3 - Create the assets

**Why it matters:** a campaign is only as good as the thing people see. This is where your copy, design, and email skills pay off.

**Do this now:**
1. Make the attention asset: a social post or ad creative in Canva with a clear benefit and one call to action.
2. Make the capture asset: a signup form or a simple landing page with consent text.
3. Write the follow-up email that goes out when someone signs up.
4. Add UTM tags to your links so Analytics can show where signups came from (search "Google Campaign URL Builder" for the free tool).
5. Preview everything on a phone.

**You've got it when:** you have a post or ad, a capture page or form, and a follow-up email, all phone-ready with tracked links.

## Try it 4 - Set a tiny budget and launch

**Why it matters:** running it for real, even tiny, teaches more than any plan on paper. You see what people actually do.

**Do this now:**
1. Decide your spend: it can be zero (organic post plus email) or a very small ad budget with a fixed end date.
2. Publish the attention asset and make sure its link points to your capture page.
3. Confirm the follow-up email sends automatically to new signups.
4. Do a full test yourself: click the post link, sign up, and check the email arrives.

**You've got it when:** your campaign is live and your own test signup triggered the follow-up email correctly.

## Try it 5 - Measure over the week

**Why it matters:** the numbers tell you if it worked and what to do next time. Measuring is the difference between marketing and guessing.

**Do this now:**
1. Each day, note two or three key numbers: reach or visits, signups or enquiries, and spend.
2. In Analytics, check Traffic acquisition to see which source your signups came from.
3. At the end of the week, calculate your result against the goal and your cost-per-result if you spent money.
4. Write one sentence on what worked and one on what did not.

**You've got it when:** you have a week of numbers, your result versus the goal, and a clear read of what worked.

## Try it 6 - Present the results

**Why it matters:** being able to show the work and the numbers is what makes you employable. A finished, measured campaign is a strong portfolio piece.

**Do this now, build a short presentation covering:**
1. The goal and audience.
2. The two channels and why you chose them.
3. The assets (show them).
4. The results: numbers, cost-per-result, goal hit or missed.
5. An honest reflection: what you would change next time.

**You've got it when:** you have a presentation someone could follow in five minutes, with real screenshots and honest numbers.

## Weekly Assignment

This is your capstone: a complete mini campaign, run and presented.

Steps:
1. Write a one-page campaign plan: goal, audience, two channels, dates, and budget.
2. Build all assets: the attention post or ad, the capture form or page, and the follow-up email, with tracked links.
3. Run the campaign live for one full week, after testing it yourself end to end.
4. Collect the results: daily numbers, source of signups from Analytics, result versus goal, and cost-per-result if any.
5. Deliver a short presentation (slides or a document) covering the plan, the assets with screenshots, the results, and an honest reflection.

Done looks like: a one-page plan, the built assets, a week of real results tied to the goal, and a clear presentation with honest numbers and one lesson for next time.

## Facilitator Notes

Judge the capstone on a real, finished, measured campaign and an honest reflection, not on budget size or flashy design. A zero-spend campaign done well should score as high as a paid one. The common failures are a goal too big for a week and skipping the end-to-end test, so a signup never triggers the email; check both early. Reward learners who report a missed goal honestly and explain why, because that is the real skill. Point them to the Remote Ready course for hosting a simple portfolio.

## Can you do these now?

1. Why does a one-week campaign need one clear goal and a defined audience rather than a broad aim?
2. How should your two channels work together, and why is that stronger than using one alone?
3. What numbers prove whether your campaign worked, and why is an honest reflection part of a good portfolio piece?
