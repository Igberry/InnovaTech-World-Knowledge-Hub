**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have three content pillars tied to your offer, a full outline of one helpful piece, that piece repurposed into a social post and an email, and a two-week content plan on a calendar. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Choose three content pillars that connect to what you sell
- Outline one genuinely helpful article or post
- Repurpose one piece of content into several formats
- Build a simple, realistic two-week content plan

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your offer sheet, your Week 1 journey, and your Week 3 keyword list.
- Warm-up: write down the five questions customers ask you most often (or would ask). Each one is a future piece of content. Keep this list.

## Try it 1 - Choose three content pillars

**Why it matters:** pillars are the three or four topics you post about again and again. They keep your content focused and tied to your offer, instead of random posts that attract the wrong people.

**Do this now:**
1. Look at your offer and the problem it solves. Name three broad topics a good customer cares about. For a meal-delivery offer: "quick family meals", "eating well on a budget", "behind our kitchen".
2. Check each pillar connects back to buying your offer, not just to general interest.
3. Write one sentence under each pillar on why your ideal customer cares about it.

**You've got it when:** you have three named pillars, each tied clearly to your offer and your customer.

## Try it 2 - Turn questions into titles

**Why it matters:** the fastest way to be helpful is to answer real questions. Your warm-up list and Week 3 keywords are a content goldmine.

**Do this now:**
1. Sort your five customer questions under the three pillars.
2. Turn each question into a clear title, for example "What does a week of family dinners cost if we order in?".
3. Star the one title that would help the most customers and fits a pillar best. This is the piece you build this week.

**You've got it when:** you have at least five titles sorted under pillars, with one starred to write.

## Try it 3 - Outline one genuinely helpful piece

**Why it matters:** helpful content earns trust and attracts people at the awareness stage. An outline means you write faster and stay useful instead of rambling.

**Do this now, for your starred title:**
1. Write the promise: one line on what the reader will know or be able to do after reading.
2. List three to five sub-points, each a step or a key idea, in a sensible order.
3. Under each sub-point, jot one real example, number, or tip from your own knowledge.
4. End with one soft line pointing to your offer, not a hard sell.

**You've got it when:** you have a complete outline with a promise, ordered sub-points, real detail under each, and a gentle call to action.

## Try it 4 - Write the piece

**Why it matters:** an outline is not content until it is written. Writing it proves it is genuinely helpful and gives you something to repurpose.

**Do this now:**
1. Expand each sub-point of your outline into two or three plain sentences. Write like you talk.
2. Keep it honest and specific; real numbers and local examples beat vague advice.
3. Add a clear title and one simple image made in Canva if it suits.

**You've got it when:** you have a finished helpful piece of around 300 to 600 words that a customer would thank you for.

## Try it 5 - Repurpose into a social post and an email

**Why it matters:** one good piece should feed many channels. Repurposing saves hours and reaches people who will never read the full article.

**Do this now:**
1. **Social post:** pull the single most useful tip from your piece, write it as a short standalone post (3 to 5 lines), and design a simple Canva graphic for it.
2. **Email:** write a short email that gives one helpful point from the piece and links or points to the rest, ending with your soft call to action.
3. Keep the voice and the core idea the same across all three.

**You've got it when:** from one piece you have produced a social post with a graphic and a short email, all carrying the same core idea.

## Try it 6 - Build a two-week content plan

**Why it matters:** consistency beats bursts. A simple calendar tells you what to post and when, so content actually happens.

**Do this now:**
1. Make a table with columns: Date, Pillar, Title/idea, Format, Channel.
2. Fill in six to eight rows across the next two weeks, rotating your three pillars.
3. Be realistic: three short posts a week you will actually make beats a plan you abandon.

**You've got it when:** you have a filled two-week calendar with a date, pillar, idea, format, and channel on every row.

## Weekly Assignment

Submit a "Content Starter Kit" built from one real piece. Submit:

1. Your three content pillars, each with a one-line reason your customer cares.
2. Your five-plus titles sorted under the pillars, with the one you wrote starred.
3. The finished helpful piece (300 to 600 words) with a title and, if suitable, a graphic.
4. The repurposed social post (with its Canva graphic) and the repurposed email.
5. Your two-week content plan table.

Done looks like: proof that from one genuinely helpful piece you produced content for three channels and have a realistic plan to keep going, all tied back to your offer.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Recap; where content sits in the funnel (mostly awareness and consideration) |
| 15-40 min | Pillar workshop: define three pillars for two offers and stress-test the link to the sale |
| 40-65 min | Outline and write a short helpful piece together, live |
| 65-90 min | Repurpose the piece into a post and an email; build a two-week plan; brief Week 5 |

## Facilitator Notes

The common failure is content that is interesting but does not lead anywhere near the offer, so keep pulling learners back to pillars that a buyer, not just a browser, would care about. Many write far too long; hold them to one promise and a tight outline. Repurposing is the lightbulb moment, so make sure every learner turns one piece into at least two formats and feels the time saved. Emphasise consistency over volume; a plan they can actually keep is worth more than an ambitious one they drop.

## Can you do these now?

1. What is a content pillar, and how do you check it ties to your offer?
2. Why is repurposing one piece into several formats worth doing?
3. Why does a realistic two-week plan beat an ambitious plan you cannot keep?
