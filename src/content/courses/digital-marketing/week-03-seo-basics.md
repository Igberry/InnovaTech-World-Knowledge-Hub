**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have a keyword your customers actually search, a written title and meta description, a page with correct on-page basics, a free SEO check report, and a live or improved Google Business Profile. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Find keywords real customers type, using free tools
- Write a page title and meta description that earn clicks
- Apply on-page basics: one H1, clear headings, image alt text
- Check a page in a free SEO checker and act on it
- Set up or improve a Google Business Profile for local search

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your landing page from Week 2 and a blank doc titled "Week 3 SEO".
- Warm-up: type the start of your offer into Google and watch the suggestions drop down, for example "home cooked meals in". Those suggestions are real searches. Write down the three most relevant ones.

## Try it 1 - Do free keyword research

**Why it matters:** SEO starts with the words customers actually use, which are often different from your own. Free tools reveal them so you write for real demand.

**Do this now:**
1. In Google, type your offer plus a location and note the autocomplete suggestions.
2. Search one of them, scroll to the bottom, and copy the "People also search for" and "Related searches".
3. Open https://answerthepublic.com or https://trends.google.com and search your topic to see questions and interest.
4. Pick one main keyword (clear buying intent, plain words) and three supporting ones.

**You've got it when:** your doc lists one main keyword plus three supporting keywords, all taken from real searches.

## Try it 2 - Write the title tag and meta description

**Why it matters:** the title and meta description are the blue link and grey text people see in Google results. They decide whether anyone clicks you instead of a competitor.

**Do this now:**
1. Write a title under about 60 characters that includes your main keyword, for example "Home-Cooked Meal Delivery in Lagos | FreshPlate".
2. Write a meta description under about 155 characters that names the benefit and ends with a reason to click.
3. Count the characters (type them into a doc and check) so neither gets cut off.

**You've got it when:** you have a title under ~60 characters and a description under ~155, both using your main keyword naturally.

## Try it 3 - Apply the on-page basics

**Why it matters:** Google reads your page structure to understand it. One clear H1, descriptive sub-headings, and alt text on images make the topic obvious to both Google and people using screen readers.

**Do this now, on your Week 2 page or a copy of it:**
1. Make sure there is exactly one H1 (the main heading), and that it contains your keyword naturally.
2. Give sub-sections descriptive headings, not "Section 1" but "How delivery works".
3. Add alt text to every image that plainly describes it, for example "Packed lunch box of jollof rice and chicken".

**You've got it when:** your page has one H1, descriptive sub-headings, and alt text on each image.

## Try it 4 - Check the page in a free SEO tool

**Why it matters:** a free checker catches problems you cannot see, such as a missing description, a slow page, or no headings, before customers do.

**Do this now:**
1. Open a free checker such as https://seositecheckup.com or https://pagespeed.web.dev.
2. Paste your page link and run the report.
3. Write down the top three issues it flags and fix any you can (often a missing meta description or oversized image).

**You've got it when:** you have a saved report and have fixed at least one issue it raised.

## Try it 5 - Set up or improve a Google Business Profile

**Why it matters:** for most local businesses, the Google Business Profile brings more customers than the website, because it shows on Maps and in the local pack with reviews and a phone number.

**Do this now:**
1. Go to https://business.google.com and search for your business. Claim it if it exists, or create it.
2. Fill in the name, category, area served, hours, phone, and a short description with your main keyword.
3. Add at least three real photos and your website link.

**You've got it when:** your profile has a correct category, description, hours, photos, and a link, and is submitted for verification.

## Try it 6 - Write one search-answering section

**Why it matters:** pages that directly answer a searched question rank well and build trust. You already have the questions from Try it 1.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick one question from your keyword research, for example "How much does meal delivery cost in Lagos?".
2. Write a short, honest three-to-four sentence answer and add it to your page under a heading that matches the question.
3. Use the exact words people search in the heading.

**You've got it when:** your page has a clearly headed section that answers one real customer question in plain words.

## Weekly Assignment

Submit an "SEO one-pager" plus your updated page and profile. Submit:

1. Your keyword list: one main keyword and three supporting, with a note on where each came from.
2. Your final title tag and meta description with character counts.
3. The link to your page showing one H1, descriptive headings, image alt text, and the question-answering section.
4. Your free SEO checker report and the issue you fixed.
5. A screenshot or link showing your Google Business Profile set up or improved.

Done looks like: a page a stranger could find by searching your main keyword, structured so Google understands it, backed by a tool report, plus a live Business Profile for local customers.

## Facilitator Notes

Beginners often pick keywords that are too broad or that only they would type, so steer them toward specific, local, buying-intent phrases pulled from real autocomplete. Watch for pages with several H1s or none; fixing heading structure is a quick, high-value win. The Google Business Profile step matters most for local learners, so prioritise it for anyone with a physical or service-area business, and warn them verification can take a few days. Keep keyword stuffing out; natural language ranks better and reads better.
