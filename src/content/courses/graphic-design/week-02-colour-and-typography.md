**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have built a 3-colour palette you can defend, chosen a heading-and-body font pairing, run a readability test, used colour to lead the eye, and repaired a design ruined by poor type. You learn by building and testing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do
- Build a 3-colour palette and explain why each colour earns its place
- Pick a heading and body font pairing using simple, repeatable rules
- Test any design for readability and fix what fails
- Use one accent colour to guide the eye to the action
- Spot and fix common typography mistakes

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open [Canva](https://www.canva.com) and your practice folder. Have your Brand Kit palette from setup open too.
- Warm-up: open any two brands you like on Instagram. Count how many colours each really uses on a single post. Usually two or three. Good brands stay disciplined.

## Try it 1 - Build a 3-colour palette and defend it
**Why it matters:** too many colours looks chaotic. A tight palette looks designed and deliberate.
**Do this now:**
1. Choose a **dominant** colour (used most), a **secondary** colour (support and backgrounds), and one **accent** (used rarely, for the thing you want clicked).
2. A safe recipe: pick one main colour you like, use a near-white or near-black as the secondary, and pick an accent that clearly stands apart from the main colour.
3. In Canva, make three large rectangles, fill each with one colour, and label it dominant, secondary or accent.
4. Under each, write one sentence on its job (for example "accent red: used only on the button").
**You've got it when:** you have three swatches, each with a clear named job, and the accent genuinely stands out from the other two.

## Try it 2 - Pick a font pairing by the rules
**Why it matters:** two fonts that clash make a design feel broken. A few rules make pairing reliable.
**Do this now:**
1. Rule one: contrast, do not match. Pair a bold heading font with a simpler body font, not two similar ones.
2. Rule two: never more than two fonts in one design. Weight and size give variety, not extra fonts.
3. Rule three: body text must be a clean, plain font. Save decorative fonts for short headings only.
4. In Canva, set up two options: heading **Montserrat** with body **Open Sans**, and heading **Poppins** with body **Lato**. Type the same sentence in each and keep the pair you prefer.
**You've got it when:** you have one heading-plus-body pair that clearly contrasts, with a plain, readable body font.

## Try it 3 - Run a readability test
**Why it matters:** a design nobody can read has failed, however pretty it looks.
**Do this now:**
1. Put a paragraph of body text on a coloured background from your palette.
2. Check size: body text on a social post should feel comfortable on a phone. If you squint, make it bigger.
3. Check contrast: dark text on light, or light text on dark. Pale grey on white fails. Use Canva's colour picker to pick a text colour far from the background in brightness.
4. Step back from the screen about two metres. If the body text turns to mush, fix size or contrast until it holds.
**You've got it when:** you can read every line easily from two metres and on a phone preview.

## Try it 4 - Use colour to guide the eye
**Why it matters:** your accent colour is a pointing finger. Used sparingly, it sends the eye exactly where you want.
**Do this now:**
1. Make a simple post with a headline, a line of detail, and a button or call to action ("Register now").
2. Keep the whole design in your dominant and secondary colours only.
3. Now apply the accent colour to one thing only: the button.
4. Notice your eye jumps straight to it. Try adding the accent to three things instead and watch the effect disappear.
**You've got it when:** the accent appears on just one element and your eye lands there first.

## Try it 5 - Fix a design with bad type
**Why it matters:** diagnosing type problems is a job skill. Clients send you exactly these messes to fix.
**Do this now:**
1. Make or find a design that breaks the rules on purpose: three or four fonts, tiny low-contrast text, all-capitals body text, cramped line spacing.
2. Fix it one problem at a time: cut to two fonts, raise the body size and contrast, use sentence case for body text, open up the line spacing (Canva: **Spacing**, then **Line spacing**).
3. Keep headings short; move long detail into body text at a readable size.
4. Save a before and after so you can see the jump.
**You've got it when:** the fixed version uses two fonts, reads cleanly, and feels calm instead of noisy.

## Weekly Assignment
Create a one-page **brand style sheet** for a small business you invent (a tailor, a food vendor, a tutor) and submit it as one image or PDF:

1. Your 3-colour palette with each colour's hex code and its named job.
2. Your heading-and-body font pairing, shown with a sample headline and paragraph.
3. One sample social post that uses the palette and fonts correctly, with the accent on a single call to action.
4. Two short "rules for this brand" lines (for example "accent only on buttons", "never more than two fonts").

Done looks like: a tidy sheet where the palette is limited and purposeful, the fonts contrast and stay readable, and the sample post passes the two-metre readability test. Export as PNG or PDF into your practice folder.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}

|---|---|

| 15-40 min | Build palettes together; mentor checks each accent truly stands apart |
| 40-65 min | Font pairing clinic: test pairs live, kill anything with three or more fonts |
| 65-85 min | Readability test on real designs; fix size and contrast on the spot |
| 85-90 min | Assign the style sheet; brief Week 3 on social media graphics |

## Facilitator Notes
The hardest habit to build is restraint; most learners want more colours and more fonts, so make "cut it down" the refrain of the session. The two-metre readability test and the squint test catch almost every contrast and size problem, so run them aloud repeatedly. Watch for pale grey text on white, a very common and silent failure. Encourage reusing the Brand Kit so good choices carry forward instead of being remade each week.

## Can you do these now?
1. What are the three roles in a simple palette, and what does the accent colour do?
2. Name two rules for pairing fonts and the maximum number of fonts in one design.
3. How do you test whether your body text is readable before you publish?
