**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have designed an event flyer, a poster, and a one-page CV or proposal, understood print sizes, margins and bleed in plain terms, and exported a print-ready PDF. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do
- Design a clear event flyer that tells people what, when and where in seconds
- Design a poster that reads from a distance
- Lay out a tidy one-page CV or proposal
- Understand print sizes, margins and bleed without the jargon
- Export a print-ready PDF a printer will accept

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open Canva at https://www.canva.com and sign in.
- Warm-up: think of a real event you could make a flyer for (a church programme, a small business opening, a weekend football match) and jot down its name, date, time and venue on paper. You will use these details all week.

## Try it 1 - Design an event flyer

**Why it matters:** a flyer has one job, make a reader grab the what, when and where in a glance. Most homemade flyers fail because they cram in too much.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva search "Flyer" and open a blank A5 or A4 flyer, or start from a simple template.
2. Put the event name as the biggest text at the top.
3. Add the date, time and venue in a clear block that stands out, each on its own line.
4. Add one strong image or a solid brand colour background. Keep text off busy parts of a photo.
5. Add a contact line at the bottom (phone number or handle) and nothing else.

**You've got it when:** a friend can glance at your flyer for three seconds and tell you the event name, date and venue.

## Try it 2 - Design a poster that works from a distance

**Why it matters:** a poster is read from across a room or a street, so the order of importance has to be ruthless. Small print is wasted.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a new design at A3 size (search "Poster" or set a custom size of 297 by 420 mm).
2. Decide the single most important line (often the event name or a short headline) and make it fill much of the top third.
3. Use only three text sizes: a big headline, a medium subhead, and small details.
4. Leave generous empty space. Do not fill every corner.
5. Step back from your screen, or shrink the preview, and check the headline still reads.

**You've got it when:** you can read the main message of the poster from across the room with the preview shrunk small.

## Try it 3 - Lay out a one-page CV or proposal

**Why it matters:** a clean CV or proposal shows you can organise information, which is half of graphic design. Clients judge tidiness instantly.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a new A4 design.
2. Put a clear name and contact line at the top for a CV, or a project title and client name for a proposal.
3. Use clear section headings (for a CV: Profile, Experience, Skills, Education) in your brand heading font.
4. Keep body text in one plain font, left-aligned, with consistent spacing between sections.
5. Use one accent colour only for the headings. Black text everywhere else.

**You've got it when:** the page has clear headings, even spacing, and no more than one accent colour.

## Try it 4 - Understand sizes, margins and bleed

**Why it matters:** get the size or margins wrong and the printer cuts off your text or leaves white edges. This is the number one cause of wasted print money.

**Do this now:**
1. Note the common sizes: A4 is a full sheet, A5 is half of that, A3 is a large poster.
2. Margin in plain terms: keep all important text and the logo at least 5 to 10 mm away from every edge, so nothing gets cut.
3. Bleed in plain terms: if your background colour or photo goes right to the edge, stretch it about 3 mm past the edge so a slightly off cut still has no white line.
4. In your flyer, drag the background so it runs fully off all four edges, and pull all text inward from the edges.

**You've got it when:** your flyer background runs off every edge and no text sits within about 10 mm of any edge.

*Tip: printers cut a stack of sheets at once, so the blade is never perfectly on the line. Bleed is your safety margin for that.*

## Try it 5 - Turn on bleed and crop marks in Canva

**Why it matters:** a printer expects a PDF with bleed and crop marks. Canva adds both for you once you know where the switch is.

**Do this now:**
1. In your flyer design, open **File**, then **View settings**, and tick **Show print bleed**. A dotted line appears near the edges.
2. Make sure your background fills past that dotted bleed line.
3. Check that nothing important crosses the dotted line into the trim zone.
4. You will turn on crop marks at export in the next step.

**You've got it when:** the dotted bleed line is visible and your background colour reaches past it on all sides.

## Try it 6 - Export a print-ready PDF

**Why it matters:** the right export settings are the difference between a clean print and a blurry, wrongly cut one.

**Do this now:**
1. Click **Share**, then **Download**.
2. Choose **PDF Print** as the file type.
3. Tick **Crop marks and bleed**.
4. If offered, set colour profile to CMYK (this is the print colour system; screens use RGB).
5. Download the file and open it to confirm you can see the crop marks at the corners.

**You've got it when:** you have a PDF Print file with visible crop marks and bleed, ready to send to any printer.

## Weekly Assignment
Produce a three-piece print pack for your chosen event and submit it as print-ready PDFs.

Steps:
1. Finish the event flyer with correct bleed and a background that runs off all edges.
2. Finish the A3 poster version of the same event, reusing the colours and fonts.
3. Finish a one-page CV or a one-page proposal using the same brand style.
4. Export all three as PDF Print with crop marks and bleed.
5. Write two or three sentences explaining the margin and bleed choices you made.

Done looks like: three separate PDF Print files (flyer, poster, CV or proposal) that all share one colour and font style, each with crop marks and bleed, plus your short written note on margins and bleed.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Flyer gallery: each student shows their flyer and the group names what, when, where in one glance |
| 15-45 min | Live fix: shrink each poster preview and ruthlessly cut anything the group cannot read from a distance |
| 45-75 min | Walk through bleed and crop marks together, then everyone exports one correct PDF Print file |
| 75-90 min | Check CVs for spacing and single accent colour, and brief the capstone week |

## Facilitator Notes
The hardest idea to land is bleed, so demonstrate it with a real example of a print that got cut with a white edge. Learners overcrowd flyers and posters, so push the "three text sizes and lots of empty space" rule hard. Many students forget to switch from PDF Standard to PDF Print at export, so check each file has crop marks before accepting it. CMYK versus RGB can wait as a short aside; the practical win is correct bleed and the right PDF type.
