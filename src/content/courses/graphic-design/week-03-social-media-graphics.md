**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have made an Instagram post and story in the right sizes, designed a 3-slide carousel, turned a design into a reusable template, exported correctly, and produced a 3-post set that clearly reads as one brand. You learn by making share-ready graphics, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do
- Design in the correct size for a feed post and a story
- Build a multi-slide carousel that flows from slide to slide
- Turn any design into a template you can refill in minutes
- Export at the right size and file format for social media
- Make several posts that look like one consistent brand

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open [Canva](https://www.canva.com), your practice folder, and the brand style sheet you made in Week 2.
- Warm-up: scroll your feed and screenshot one post that made you stop. Name the one thing that caught you. You will aim for that same stopping power.

## Try it 1 - Make a post and a story in the correct sizes
**Why it matters:** wrong sizes get cropped, blurred or rejected. Correct sizes look sharp and professional.
**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, create an **Instagram Post (Square)**, which is 1080 by 1080 pixels. Build a simple post from your brand style sheet: one headline, one line of detail, your accent on one element.
2. Now create an **Instagram Story**, which is 1080 by 1920 pixels (tall). Rebuild the same message to fit the tall shape.
3. On the story, keep important text in the middle. The top and bottom get covered by profile icons and buttons.
4. Compare the two. Notice how the same idea has to be re-laid-out, not just stretched, for each shape.
**You've got it when:** you have one 1080x1080 post and one 1080x1920 story, both using your brand palette and fonts, with story text kept clear of the edges.

## Try it 2 - Design a 3-slide carousel
**Why it matters:** carousels hold attention longer and are shared and saved more, because each slide pulls the reader to the next.
**Do this now:**
1. Create an **Instagram Post (Square)** and add three pages (the **+ Add page** button).
2. Plan the flow: slide 1 is the hook (a bold promise or question), slide 2 is the value (the main point or steps), slide 3 is the call to action ("Follow for more", "Register now").
3. Keep the same background, fonts and colours on all three so they feel like one set.
4. Add a small "swipe" or arrow cue on slide 1 so people know to keep going.
**You've got it when:** three slides that share one look, start with a hook, and end with a clear next step.

## Try it 3 - Turn a design into a reusable template
**Why it matters:** templates let you make next week's posts in minutes instead of starting from zero every time.
**Do this now:**
1. Open your best post so far. Replace the specific words with placeholder labels like "HEADLINE HERE" and "detail line here".
2. Keep the colours, fonts, spacing and element positions exactly in place. The template is the skeleton.
3. Rename the file clearly, for example "TEMPLATE - square announcement", and keep it in your practice folder.
4. Make a copy (**... menu**, then **Make a copy**) and fill the copy with a brand-new message to prove the template works.
**You've got it when:** you have a labelled template and a filled copy made from it in under five minutes.

## Try it 4 - Export at the right size and format
**Why it matters:** the wrong export setting makes a crisp design look blurry or heavy online.
**Do this now:**
1. Open a finished post. Click **Share**, then **Download**.
2. Choose **PNG** for graphics with text and flat colour (sharpest). Choose **JPG** only when the file must be smaller and it is mostly photo.
3. Leave the size at the design's native 1080 pixels. Do not shrink it.
4. For a multi-page carousel, download as **PNG** and Canva gives you one image per slide, correctly numbered.
**You've got it when:** you have sharp PNG files, one per slide for the carousel, saved and ready to post.

## Try it 5 - Produce a 3-post set that looks like one brand
**Why it matters:** consistency is what makes a page look trustworthy and recognisable. This is the real deliverable clients pay for.
**Do this now:**
1. Pick one invented brand (reuse your Week 2 style sheet). Plan three different posts: an announcement, a tip, and a testimonial or quote.
2. Design all three using the same palette, the same fonts, and the same layout rules. Only the content changes.
3. Place the same small brand mark (name or simple logo text) in the same spot on each.
4. Lay all three side by side in one view. They should obviously belong together.
**You've got it when:** three different posts clearly read as one brand at a glance.

## Try it 6 - Pressure-test for the feed
**Why it matters:** a design that looks good full-screen can fail at thumbnail size in a real feed.
**Do this now:**
1. Export your three posts, then view them small on a phone, or shrink the preview on screen.
2. Check the headline is still readable at small size. If not, make it bigger and bolder.
3. Check the accent still draws the eye and the brand mark is still visible.
4. Fix the weakest post until all three hold up small.
**You've got it when:** all three posts still communicate clearly when viewed at thumbnail size.

## Weekly Assignment
Produce a **3-post brand set plus one reusable template** and submit it as one PDF or a set of images:

1. Three square posts (announcement, tip, testimonial or quote) that share one palette, one font pairing and one layout system.
2. One matching Instagram Story version of any one post, sized 1080x1920.
3. One labelled reusable template the set was built from.
4. A one-line note of which brand this is and who the posts are for.

Done looks like: all pieces obviously belong to the same brand, each is exported sharp at the correct size, headlines read at thumbnail size, and the accent colour guides the eye on every post. Save everything in your practice folder.

## Facilitator Notes
The most common mistake is stretching one design across shapes instead of re-laying it out, so show a cropped, broken example early. Enforce the thumbnail test, since many learners only judge their work full-screen. Push consistency hard on the 3-post set: same spot for the brand mark, same fonts, same accent behaviour. Templates often fail because learners change positions when refilling, so remind them the skeleton stays fixed and only the content changes.
