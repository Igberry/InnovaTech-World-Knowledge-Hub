**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have five rough logo sketches on paper, one clean wordmark logo built in Canva, a one-page brand kit (logo, colours, fonts, usage rules), and a mock-up of your logo on a profile picture and a header. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do
- Sketch and judge rough logo ideas quickly on paper
- Build a clean, readable wordmark logo in Canva
- Pick a small, sensible colour and font set for a brand
- Write simple, clear rules for how a logo should and should not be used
- Show a logo in real places (profile picture, header) so a client can picture it

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open Canva at https://www.canva.com and sign in to your free account.
- Grab a pen and any paper. A notebook page is perfect.
- Warm-up: write down the name of a small business you know in your area (a tailor, a food vendor, a barber). You will design a logo for this made-up or real brand all week, so pick one now.

## Try it 1 - Sketch five rough logo ideas on paper

**Why it matters:** paper is faster than any app. Getting five ideas out quickly stops you falling in love with the first weak one.

**Do this now:**
1. At the top of your page, write the brand name you chose.
2. Draw five small boxes, each about the size of a matchbox.
3. In each box, sketch one different logo idea in under two minutes: try the name in a bold style, the name with a tiny simple shape, just the first letter, the name inside a circle, and the name with a short tagline under it.
4. Do not erase. Rough is the point. Keep going until all five boxes are full.

**You've got it when:** you have five clearly different sketches and can point to the two you like most.

## Try it 2 - Build a clean wordmark in Canva

**Why it matters:** a wordmark (the name set in nice type) is the most useful logo for a beginner. It is clear, flexible, and hard to get wrong.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, click **Create a design**, choose **Custom size**, and enter 1000 by 1000 pixels.
2. Click **Text**, add a heading, and type your brand name.
3. Try three or four fonts from the font list. Pick one that is easy to read and matches the mood (a tailor can feel elegant, a food vendor can feel friendly and round).
4. Make the name large and centred. If the name has two words, try putting the second word on a new line or in a lighter weight.
5. Keep it to one or two colours for now. Simple wins.

**You've got it when:** your brand name reads clearly from across the room and uses only one font and one or two colours.

## Try it 3 - Choose your colours and fonts

**Why it matters:** a brand that reuses the same few colours and fonts looks professional. Random colours on every post look amateur.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, open your design and click a text element, then the colour tile, then **Add a new colour**.
2. Pick one main colour that fits the brand (deep green for a farm stall, warm red for a food brand).
3. Pick one dark colour for text (a near-black or deep grey) and keep white for backgrounds.
4. Note the colour codes. Click a colour in the picker and copy the six-character hex code (for example `0b3b66`). Write all your codes on paper.
5. Pick one heading font and one plain body font. Two fonts maximum.

**You've got it when:** you have written down two or three hex codes and the names of your two fonts.

*Tip: three colours is plenty for a whole brand, one main colour, one dark for text, and white. More than that gets messy fast.*

## Try it 4 - Assemble a one-page brand kit

**Why it matters:** a brand kit is the single page a client or teammate checks so everything stays consistent. It is the deliverable people actually pay for.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, create a new design at A4 size (search "A4 Document").
2. At the top, place your finished logo.
3. Below it, add a row of coloured rectangles, one per brand colour, and label each with its hex code.
4. Add a "Fonts" section showing your heading font and body font with their names.
5. Add a short "Usage rules" list, for example: keep clear space around the logo, do not stretch it, do not add a drop shadow, use the dark logo on light backgrounds and the white logo on dark.

**You've got it when:** one page shows the logo, the colours with codes, the fonts, and at least three plain usage rules.

## Try it 5 - Make a white version of the logo

**Why it matters:** a logo often sits on a dark header or photo. A white (reversed) version keeps it readable everywhere.

**Do this now:**
1. Duplicate your logo design in Canva (click the three dots on the design, then duplicate).
2. Change the background to your dark brand colour.
3. Change the logo text colour to white.
4. Check it still reads clearly. If a thin font disappears, make it bolder.

**You've got it when:** you have two logo versions, a dark one for light backgrounds and a white one for dark backgrounds.

## Try it 6 - Mock the logo on a profile picture and header

**Why it matters:** seeing the logo in a real place is how a client decides yes. A flat logo on a white page does not sell it.

**Do this now:**
1. New Canva design, custom size 500 by 500 pixels, for a profile picture. Fill the background with a brand colour and centre a simple version of the logo (often just the first letter or the short name).
2. New design sized 1640 by 624 pixels for a Facebook cover or page header.
3. Place the full logo on the header with plenty of clear space, and leave room so no text is hidden by the profile picture circle.
4. Download both as PNG (**Share**, then **Download**, then **PNG**).

**You've got it when:** you can open the two PNG files and the logo looks at home in both the small circle and the wide header.

## Weekly Assignment
Build a complete mini brand identity for the business you chose in the warm-up and submit it as one PDF.

Steps:
1. Include a photo or scan of your five paper sketches.
2. Include your final wordmark logo, in both the dark and white versions.
3. Include the one-page brand kit (logo, colours with hex codes, fonts, at least three usage rules).
4. Include the profile picture mock-up and the header mock-up.
5. Write two or three sentences at the end explaining why you chose these colours and fonts for this brand.

Done looks like: a single PDF with the sketches, both logo versions, the brand kit page, the two mock-ups, and your short reasoning. To export, open your brand kit design in Canva, add each item on its own page, then **Share**, **Download**, **PDF Standard**.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Each student holds up their five paper sketches and names their favourite in one sentence |
| 15-45 min | Live build: turn one sketch into a clean wordmark in Canva together, fixing spacing and font choice |
| 45-75 min | Pairs review each other's brand kits against a checklist: two fonts max, codes written, clear usage rules |
| 75-90 min | Quick mock-up gallery and brief for next week on flyers and print |

## Facilitator Notes
Beginners rush to the computer and skip the paper step, so insist on five sketches before anyone opens Canva. The most common mistake is too many fonts and too many colours, so repeat the "two fonts, two colours" rule often. Watch for logos that disappear on dark backgrounds, which is why the white version matters. If a learner is stuck on ideas, suggest they start from a wordmark in a strong font before trying any icon.
