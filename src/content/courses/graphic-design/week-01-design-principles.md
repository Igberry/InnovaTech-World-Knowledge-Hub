**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have fixed a messy layout, made one message stand out, built a clear visual order, used whitespace on purpose, and rebuilt a bad flyer from scratch. You learn by redesigning real things, not reading theory.

## What you'll be able to do
- Line up elements so a design looks tidy and intentional
- Use contrast so the most important message is seen first
- Build hierarchy so the eye moves first, second, third in the order you want
- Use whitespace to make a crowded design breathe
- Apply all four principles to rescue a bad flyer

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open [Canva](https://www.canva.com) and your **Graphic Design Practice** folder from setup.
- Warm-up: look at any two flyers on your phone or around you. For each, say out loud what you notice first. That first thing is the design working (or failing). You are now thinking like a designer.

## Try it 1 - Straighten a messy layout with alignment
**Why it matters:** most "amateur" designs are just badly aligned. Lining things up is the fastest way to look professional.
**Do this now:**
1. Create a blank **Instagram Post (Square)**. Add a heading, two lines of body text, and a small shape, and scatter them so nothing lines up.
2. Select all of them (drag a box around them). Click **Position** at the top, then **Align to page**, then **Left**. Everything snaps to the same left edge.
3. Now drag items so their left edges all sit on the same purple guide line Canva shows. Keep equal gaps between each row.
4. Turn on a guide: top menu **File**, then **Settings**, tick **Show rulers and guides**, and drag a guide from the ruler to mark your left margin.
**You've got it when:** every element shares a clean left edge and the spacing between rows looks even.

## Try it 2 - Make the main message pop with contrast
**Why it matters:** contrast is difference. Without it, nothing stands out and the reader does not know where to look.
**Do this now:**
1. On a new blank square, type one short headline, for example "Free Class Saturday".
2. Make the headline large and bold. Make everything else (date, location) much smaller.
3. Give the background a dark colour from your palette and the headline a light colour, or the reverse. Large light text on a dark block reads from far away.
4. Add one small accent-colour element near the headline (a line or dot) to pull the eye to it.
**You've got it when:** from across the room, your headline is the first and clearest thing you see.

## Try it 3 - Build visual hierarchy (first, second, third)
**Why it matters:** hierarchy decides the order people read. You control it with size, weight and position, not luck.
**Do this now:**
1. Reuse the design from Try it 2. Decide the reading order you want: 1) what the event is, 2) when it is, 3) how to join.
2. Make the "what" the biggest. Make the "when" medium. Make the "how to join" smallest but still readable.
3. Place them top to bottom in that order. Our eyes read big-to-small and top-to-bottom, so size and position should agree.
4. Squint at the screen until it blurs. The blurry shapes should still read in the right order.
**You've got it when:** a friend glances at it for two seconds and can repeat the three pieces of information in the right order.

## Try it 4 - Let it breathe with whitespace
**Why it matters:** empty space is not wasted space. It separates ideas and makes the important thing feel important.
**Do this now:**
1. Take a crowded design (use Try it 3, or paste in lots of text). Notice how tight it feels.
2. Delete anything the message does not truly need. Less content, more impact.
3. Pull the remaining elements apart so there is clear, even space around the headline and around the whole group. Leave a consistent margin from every edge, say the width of one capital letter.
4. Do not fill the empty space. Let it sit there and do its job.
**You've got it when:** the design feels calm, the headline has room around it, and nothing touches the edges.

## Try it 5 - Redesign a bad flyer using all four
**Why it matters:** this is where the principles stop being separate tricks and become one skill.
**Do this now:**
1. Find a genuinely bad flyer (a crowded WhatsApp promo, a cramped notice board sheet). Screenshot it for reference, do not copy it.
2. Create a new blank design the same shape. Rebuild the same information using your own palette and fonts.
3. Apply each principle in order: align everything to a grid, add strong contrast to the main offer, set a clear first-second-third hierarchy, and open up whitespace.
4. Put your version and the original side by side and list three things yours does better.
**You've got it when:** your redesign carries the same information but is clearly easier and faster to read than the original.

## Weekly Assignment
Produce a **before-and-after flyer redesign** and submit it as one image or PDF:

1. The original bad flyer you chose (screenshot) on the left or top.
2. Your rebuilt version on the right or bottom, using your saved palette and font pairing.
3. A short note (3 to 5 bullet points) naming exactly how you used alignment, contrast, hierarchy and whitespace.

Done looks like: one clean comparison file where your version is tidier, the main message is obvious within two seconds, the reading order is clear, and nothing touches the edges. Export it as a PNG or PDF and save it in your practice folder.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Warm-up: everyone shows a design and says what they notice first; name the principle at work |
| 15-45 min | Live fix: mentor shares a messy layout; the group aligns it, then adds contrast, together |
| 45-75 min | Each learner redesigns their chosen bad flyer; mentor circulates and nudges hierarchy and whitespace |
| 75-90 min | Three quick before-and-after shares; brief Week 2 on colour and type |

## Facilitator Notes
Learners most often confuse "contrast" with "colour" and forget it also means size and weight; show a black-and-white example to prove contrast works without colour. The squint test is the single most useful habit to drill for hierarchy, so make everyone do it out loud. Watch for over-decoration: push them to remove elements rather than add. Keep flyer examples local and familiar so the redesign feels relevant.

## Can you do these now?
1. What is the fastest single change that makes a design look more professional, and why?
2. Name three ways to create contrast that do not involve changing colour.
3. How do you check your visual hierarchy in two seconds without reading every word?
