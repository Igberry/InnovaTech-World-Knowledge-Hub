**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have picked your best five pieces, written a short case note for each, assembled a shareable portfolio, and designed a full brand pack for a fictional local client, then presented it. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do
- Choose and order your strongest work like a professional
- Write a short case note that explains the brief and what you did
- Assemble a clean portfolio you can share as a link or PDF
- Design a complete brand pack: logo, colours, fonts, social templates and a flyer
- Present your work clearly and take feedback

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open Canva at https://www.canva.com and gather everything you have made in weeks one to five into one place.
- Warm-up: on paper, invent a fictional local client in one line, for example "Mama Ngozi's Kitchen, a small jollof and small-chops caterer in Lagos". You will build their full brand this week.

## Try it 1 - Choose your best five pieces

**Why it matters:** a portfolio is judged on its weakest item, so curating hard matters more than including everything.

**Do this now:**
1. Lay out every design you have made from the earlier weeks.
2. Rate each one good, okay or weak, honestly.
3. Pick the five strongest, aiming for variety: a logo, a flyer, a poster, a social post, a document.
4. Set aside anything rushed or cluttered. Fewer, stronger pieces win.

**You've got it when:** you have exactly five pieces chosen and can say in one sentence why each earned its place.

## Try it 2 - Write a short case note for each piece

**Why it matters:** clients want to know how you think, not just what it looks like. A short case note shows your reasoning.

**Do this now:**
1. For each of the five pieces, write three short lines: the brief (what was needed), what you did (the key design choices), and the result or intended effect.
2. Keep each case note under 60 words. Plain English, no jargon.
3. Mention one specific choice per piece, for example "used a warm red to feel appetising" or "kept one font so it reads fast".

**You've got it when:** each of your five pieces has a three-line case note that a non-designer would understand.

## Try it 3 - Assemble a shareable portfolio

**Why it matters:** a tidy portfolio you can send as one link or file is what turns practice into paid work.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, create a new design (a Presentation or a Doc works well).
2. Add a cover page with your name and the words "Graphic Design Portfolio".
3. Give each of the five pieces its own page: the design large, with its three-line case note beside or below it.
4. Keep every page in the same layout and colours so it feels like one body of work.
5. Add a final page with your contact details and a note that you trained with Innovatech World.

**You've got it when:** you can scroll through a single, consistent portfolio of five pieces each with its case note.

## Try it 4 - Build the client's logo and brand kit

**Why it matters:** the brand pack is your capstone. It proves you can deliver a full identity, not just one graphic.

**Do this now:**
1. Using the skills from week four, sketch and then build a clean wordmark logo for your fictional client.
2. Make a dark version and a white version.
3. Choose two or three brand colours and write down their hex codes.
4. Choose one heading font and one body font.
5. Put the logo, colours with codes, fonts, and three usage rules on a single brand kit page.

**You've got it when:** one page shows the client's logo (both versions), colours with codes, fonts, and usage rules.

## Try it 5 - Design three social templates and one flyer

**Why it matters:** real clients need reusable templates, not one-off posts. Templates are what keep a brand consistent.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a square 1080 by 1080 pixel design for a social post using the client's colours and fonts, and leave a clear space for a changing headline.
2. Duplicate it twice and vary the layout so you have three different templates (a quote style, a promo style, a "now open" style).
3. Design one event or promotion flyer for the client, reusing the same colours and fonts, with correct bleed.
4. Keep the logo in a consistent position across all four pieces.

**You've got it when:** you have three matching social templates and one on-brand flyer that clearly belong to the same client.

## Try it 6 - Prepare your three-minute presentation

**Why it matters:** being able to walk a client through your work is a real, paid skill. Silent designs sell themselves poorly.

**Do this now:**
1. Write five short talking points: who the client is, the logo idea, the colours and why, the templates, and the flyer.
2. Practise saying it out loud once, timing yourself to about three minutes.
3. Prepare for one likely question, for example "why these colours for a food brand?"

**You've got it when:** you can present your brand pack out loud in about three minutes without reading a script word for word.

## Weekly Assignment
This is your capstone. Deliver a complete brand pack for your fictional local client, plus your five-piece portfolio, and present it.

Steps:
1. Assemble the brand pack: logo (dark and white), brand kit page (colours with hex codes, fonts, usage rules), three social templates, and one flyer, all on brand.
2. Finish the five-piece portfolio with a case note on every piece.
3. Export both the brand pack and the portfolio as PDFs (PDF Print for the flyer so it keeps bleed).
4. Record or deliver a three-minute presentation walking through the brand pack.
5. Submit the two PDFs and your presentation (a recording link or a live session with your mentor).

Done looks like: a brand pack PDF with every item on brand, a portfolio PDF of five pieces each with a case note, and a clear three-minute presentation of the brand pack. This is the job-ready piece you can show to anyone who asks what you can do.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Each student states their fictional client and shows the logo in one minute |
| 15-50 min | Live critique of brand packs: is it consistent, are the templates reusable, does the flyer match |
| 50-80 min | Three-minute presentations in pairs, each giving one piece of kind, specific feedback |
| 80-90 min | Next steps: how to find a first real client and keep the portfolio growing |

## Facilitator Notes
The biggest risk is inconsistency, so push students to reuse the same logo position, colours and fonts across every capstone piece. Many learners undervalue the case notes, yet these are what separate a designer from someone who just uses Canva, so insist on them. For presentations, coach confidence and brevity rather than polish. Remind students never to promise clients guaranteed income from design; the honest pitch is job-ready skill and a real portfolio.

## Can you do these now?
1. Why is it better to show five strong pieces than ten mixed ones?
2. What three things does a good case note cover?
3. What makes a set of social templates feel like one brand?
