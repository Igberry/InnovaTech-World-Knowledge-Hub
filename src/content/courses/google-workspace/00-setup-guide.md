**Goal:** Prepare a safe, organised workspace before practising the Google apps.

## Choose an account and device

Use a personal Google Account for this course unless your employer or school has explicitly provided a training account. A managed work or school account may restrict sharing, Forms, Sites, offline access or security settings. Ask its administrator before changing policies. A personal account's Gmail, Drive and Google Workspace apps are not a paid Google Workspace business subscription; the available features and terms differ.

A laptop with a keyboard is strongly preferred. Most assignments involve arranging rows and columns, editing a presentation, reviewing sharing settings or building a site. A phone is useful for reading, quick capture and joining meetings, but small screens make those tasks harder. Check that the browser is current, that you can sign in to the account you intend to use, and that you have a safe place to save downloads. If you share a computer, use a separate browser profile and sign out when you finish. Do not save your password on a public or borrowed device.

If you install Gmail, Drive, Calendar or Docs on a phone, use the app store on your device and check the publisher before installing. Mobile apps are useful for reviewing and quick capture; use the laptop browser for the full course exercises. Do not install a configuration profile or grant device-wide access just because an unfamiliar page requests it.

## Protect account access

Visit [Google Account security](https://myaccount.google.com/security) and review the recovery methods you control. Turn on **2-Step Verification** if you can do so safely, and store recovery information somewhere you control. Google may offer prompts, passkeys, security keys or codes; read the screen and choose methods that you can recover if your phone is lost. Never tell a mentor a password, one-time code or recovery code. A person claiming to help with this course does not need them.

Google's official [2-Step Verification guide](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185839) explains the current options. Work or school administrators may control which options are available. If you see a sign-in prompt you did not initiate, deny it, secure your account and do not continue the lesson on that device until you understand what happened.

## Create a course workspace

Download the folder template and adapt it inside **your own Drive**. Keep the starter files separate from completed work. Use short names that sort predictably, such as `2026-11-12_FreshBites_WeeklyReport_v01`. Do not add a student's, customer's or colleague's real name to a practice filename.

Create one working copy of each download. Keep a second local copy of important work where possible. Cloud synchronisation is useful, but it is not an independent backup: deletion or an unwanted edit can also sync. Before sharing a file, check its owner, audience and role. Start with **Restricted** access and add only the practice account or mentor who needs access. Viewer, Commenter and Editor permissions are different; use the least access that lets the person complete the exercise.

## Prepare for offline work and unreliable connectivity

Google's [offline instructions](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/6388102) currently require an internet connection to set up offline access and specify Chrome or Microsoft Edge, a non-private browser window and the Google Docs Offline extension. The setting is made in Drive, Docs, Sheets or Slides; only files made available offline can be relied on without a connection. An organisation may disable the feature. Interface names and requirements can change, so check the current help page and test one unimportant file before relying on it.

Set up offline access while connected and open the practice file before you travel or lose power. Save a local working copy where the app allows it. After reconnecting, check the file status and confirm the latest version is visible before closing the browser or deleting a local copy. Offline access is not a backup or a guarantee that every change will sync.

## Watch shared storage

As checked on 12 November 2026, Google's personal-account storage help says storage is shared across Gmail, Drive and Photos. It also notes that accounts created from 9 March 2026 start with 5 GB and can unlock an additional 10 GB by verifying a valid phone number. Offers and account eligibility may change. Check [your storage](https://drive.google.com/settings/storage) before uploading large files; do not assume there is room for video or photo backups. This course's text and CSV starters are small.

## Verify the workspace

- [ ] I can sign in to the intended account and reach Gmail, Drive and Docs.
- [ ] I know whether the account is personal or managed by a school or organisation.
- [ ] I reviewed recovery options and enabled a second sign-in step where appropriate.
- [ ] I downloaded and opened the folder and shortcut templates.
- [ ] I know where downloaded files and local working copies are saved.
- [ ] I checked available storage and my browser's offline support.
- [ ] I have not put real customer, student, financial or private information in the practice folder.
- [ ] I can identify the **Restricted**, Viewer, Commenter and Editor sharing choices.

If a feature is unavailable, record the account type and error message without sharing private details, then use the labelled alternative in the lesson. Do not switch to a paid plan just to finish a course assignment.
