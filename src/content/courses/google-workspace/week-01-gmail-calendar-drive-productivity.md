**Goal:** This week you'll clean up a real-feeling inbox, plan a week in Calendar in the Yenagoa time zone, and share a Drive file the safe way. You learn it by clicking through it, not by reading about it.

## What you'll be able to do

- Sort a busy inbox with labels, filters and search operators that actually find things
- Build reusable replies and run a fast, calm inbox triage
- Plan a weekly Calendar routine with the right time zone and a clean invite
- Share Drive files with the least access that does the job
- Capture work in Contacts, Tasks and Keep without hoarding private data
- Spot a phishing cue and check your account safely

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open the [30 fictional inbox messages](/downloads/google-workspace/mock-inbox-emails.md) and the [folder template](/downloads/google-workspace/workspace-folder-structure-template.md) in a practice account you control. The inbox uses invented roles and `.example` addresses only.
- Keep the [search operators cheat sheet](/downloads/google-workspace/search-operators-cheat-sheet.md) open in a tab.
- Warm-up: skim the 30 messages for 3 minutes and tag each in your head as handle, schedule, store or ignore. You'll do it for real below.

## Try it 1 - Make Gmail find anything in seconds

**Why it matters:** an inbox is a queue, not a filing cabinet. Search and labels beat scrolling every time.

**Do this now:**
1. Load the fictional messages into your practice inbox.
2. Create a small label set: `Action`, `Waiting`, `Orders`, `Team`, `Reference`. Colour them, but don't make colour the only signal.
3. Run a search: `from:orders@freshbites.example subject:reservation`. Then try `is:unread`, `has:attachment` and `older_than:7d`.
4. If a search returns nothing, drop one condition at a time until it works.

**You've got it when:** you can find any message with search instead of scrolling, and each label has a one-line purpose.

> Quick win: give one message two labels at once (say `Orders` and `Action`). A Gmail label is not a folder - one message can wear several.

## Try it 2 - Set up one safe filter

**Why it matters:** a good filter does the sorting for you; a careless one buries an account alert.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a filter that labels only one known fictional sender.
2. Review the search condition and the actions before you save.
3. Keep it gentle: no auto-delete, no forwarding outside your control, never auto-archive security notices.

**You've got it when:** a new message from that sender lands with the right label on its own, and nothing important gets hidden.

## Try it 3 - Build replies and run a 15-minute triage

**Why it matters:** repeated replies waste your day, and a calm triage beats a frantic inbox-zero sprint.

**Do this now:**
1. Draft three reusable templates: an acknowledgement, a "please send the missing details", and a meeting follow-up. Keep them short and neutral - no invented price, date or promise. Do not send them.
2. Set a 15-minute timer and work the queue: **Scan** for anything urgent or security-related, **Do** one-minute jobs, **Defer** longer work to a dated task, **File** reference with a label, and only **Delete or report** what you recognise.
3. Try Schedule send on one draft so it goes out at a sensible hour.

**You've got it when:** you've cleared or parked every fictional message in 15 minutes, with unresolved items noted rather than rushed.

> **Warning:** An email asking for a password, code, payment or urgent "account recovery" is not safe just because the logo looks right. Open account security yourself in a new tab - never follow the link.

## Try it 4 - Plan a real week in Calendar

**Why it matters:** a calendar is a plan you can keep, not a pile of promises.

**Do this now:**
1. Set your Calendar time zone to `Africa/Lagos` (the fictional Fresh Bites team is in Yenagoa).
2. Block study, family, travel and rest first, then add one 30-minute team event with a clear title, agenda and the right local time.
3. Add a video link only if everyone agreed to meet online, and check guest permissions before sending.

**You've got it when:** your week shows realistic blocks and one tidy invite in the correct time zone.

## Try it 5 - Capture work with Contacts, Tasks and Keep

**Why it matters:** the right tool for each scrap of work stops things falling through the cracks.

**Do this now:**
1. In Tasks, add "Review Fresh Bites practice proposal" with a due date.
2. In Keep, make a checklist of your 15-minute triage steps.
3. Keep each tool in its lane:

| Tool | Good fit | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts | A consented work contact you need to reach | Importing every address with no purpose |
| Tasks | A concrete next action with a due date | Vague goals like "fix everything" |
| Keep | A quick note or reusable checklist | Passwords, codes or confidential data |

**You've got it when:** you can say which of the three you'd reach for next time, and why.

## Try it 6 - Share a Drive file the safe way

**Why it matters:** "Anyone with the link" is the easiest way to leak a file. The safe habit is Restricted plus least access.

**Do this now:**
1. Rebuild the folder template in Drive and save one copy of each Week 1 starter. Use dated, clear names - never `final-final-latest`.
2. Share one file with a test account you control as **Viewer** only.
3. Open it from that account to confirm, then remove the access.
4. Notice: "Anyone with the link" means the link can be forwarded to anyone at all.

**You've got it when:** your practice file is Restricted, view-only, holds only fictional data, and you unshared it after testing.

### Watch out for

- Making dozens of labels and filters before you understand each one.
- Auto-archiving or deleting messages that might be account alerts.
- Treating a calendar invite as permission to share a private calendar.
- Choosing "anyone with the link" because it's quicker.
- Saving a password, recovery code or real customer detail in Keep.

## This week's build: your "How I Manage My Inbox" kit

Put it all together into something you'd actually show an employer:

1. Screenshots of five labels, three tested filters and three unsent templates in your practice account.
2. A weekly calendar routine plus one event sent only to a test address you control.
3. A Drive folder system from the template, with a view-only file tested from a second account and then unshared.
4. A one-page guide titled **How I Manage My Inbox** describing your triage and security habits.

*Tip: write the one-pager as if a new teammate will follow it on day one.*

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Setup and organisation | 40% |
| Correct, least-privilege sharing | 25% |
| Useful routine and guide | 25% |
| Security habits | 10% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] Each label and filter has a clear purpose; filters were tested before saving.
- [ ] Templates contain no invented price or promise and were not sent to real people.
- [ ] The event shows the correct time zone and has a useful title.
- [ ] The shared file is view-only, holds fictional data and was unshared after testing.
- [ ] The guide explains how to handle suspicious messages and protect account access.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Beat your own time: run the 15-minute triage again and finish in 10.
- Build a filter that stars anything from the fictional `Team` sender.
- Add a second display time zone and schedule a mock call with a teammate "abroad".

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Check account access and review the practice folder; do not collect passwords or codes |
| 10–25 min | Learners explain their labels and inbox triage choices |
| 25–40 min | Demonstrate a search operator, then have learners test a filter condition |
| 40–55 min | Build a weekly Calendar event and check the time zone and guest permissions |
| 55–70 min | Review Drive role choices and discuss why link scope matters |
| 70–82 min | Practise responding to a fictional phishing or unexpected sign-in scenario |
| 82–90 min | Review assignment evidence and set the Week 2 due date |

## Facilitator Notes

Ask learners to use their own practice account only. Never ask them to share a password, code, recovery key, inbox screenshot containing real mail or access to a private folder. If a student's organisation account blocks a feature, accept a written permission plan as an alternative. Do not ask the learner to change a managed-account policy. Keep the 15-minute triage exercise focused on safe decision-making rather than inbox-zero speed.
