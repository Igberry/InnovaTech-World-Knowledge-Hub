**Goal:** Bring the course tools together in a secure, documented Remote Team Workspace for a fictional small business.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you can:

- Schedule and join a Google Meet session with suitable audio, video and screen-sharing choices.
- Choose between email, Chat and a shared document based on the task and audience.
- Audit Drive links and permissions using least privilege.
- Review account security and recognise phishing and unsafe OAuth permission requests.
- Explain the difference between using apps as a learner and administering a Workspace organisation.
- Present a documented Remote Team Workspace without exposing personal or customer data.

## Tools / Accounts Needed This Week

Google Meet, Chat, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets and Forms in an account you control, plus the capstone brief and role matrix. Recording, breakout rooms, shared drives and administration features are optional and can depend on edition, administrator settings and region.

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Meet for low-bandwidth, inclusive collaboration

Schedule a meeting from Calendar when the group needs a clear time, invite list and agenda. Use a specific title and add the meeting link through the supported Calendar or Meet workflow. Confirm the time zone, duration and guests. In the invitation, state what participants should prepare and whether they should join with audio, video or a document ready. As checked on 12 November 2026, Meet supports core scheduling and joining flows, while recording, breakout rooms and some other controls can depend on the organiser's plan or administrator settings; verify current availability before promising a feature.

Before joining, check your microphone and camera. If data or power is limited, turn off video, use audio when available and share only the window or tab that is needed. Close private tabs and notifications first. A screen share can expose the whole desktop, so choose the smallest safe surface and stop presenting when finished. If audio is unstable, pause video, use one speaker at a time and summarise decisions in a shared agenda afterward.

Captions can make a meeting easier to follow, but language support and controls can vary. Use hand-raise when it is available to take turns without interrupting. Google's current Workspace pricing and Help pages list plan-dependent meeting features; check them before promising that recording, breakout rooms or another advanced feature is available. These options are not required for the capstone.

Do not record a meeting without explaining why, obtaining appropriate consent and following the organisation's retention policy. Recordings can include faces, voices, chat and shared information. For this course, a written walkthrough or a short recording made only by the learner with fictional content is sufficient. Never record a real customer call or a private class session as assignment evidence.

### Lesson 2: Chat, Spaces and choosing the right channel

Use email when a message needs a durable summary, a clear subject or a response from someone who may not be online at the same time. Use Chat for a short coordination question that benefits from a quick exchange. A shared document or task is better for work that needs a stable record, multiple edits or a due date.

Spaces can group a team conversation around an ongoing topic. Before joining or creating one, inspect its membership and history settings. Avoid posting credentials, customer details or account recovery information in Chat. A message can be forwarded, screenshotted or retained according to the organisation's settings. The default should be to share the minimum information needed.

Use a clear message structure: context, question or action, owner and due date. For example: "The fictional weekly report is ready for review. Please leave a comment by Friday; the owner will approve the final copy." This is easier to act on than "Please check." Do not use chat to make a sensitive decision that should follow a formal process.

Set notifications so that you can notice genuine requests without expecting to respond instantly to every message. A weekly review can help the team close completed actions, update owners and due dates, archive old drafts and check the next week's calendar. Use a shared calendar only for team events that members need to see; keep private appointments on a calendar with appropriate access.

### Lesson 3: Audit links, roles and ownership

Use the access matrix to list which role needs each folder and file. Give the rider access only to the assigned route sheet; do not share the full order tracker if it contains unrelated details. Give a cook access to the kitchen procedure, and a counter role access to the order form and status fields. A broad parent-folder permission can flow to its children, so inspect folder inheritance before making a one-file exception.

Google Drive roles such as Viewer, Commenter and Editor determine what a person can do. An Editor may be able to change or delete content and, depending on settings, share it further. Start with the narrowest role that supports the task. Re-check any "Anyone with the link" access; it can be forwarded beyond the intended audience. Protected ranges in Sheets can reduce accidental edits but do not hide the data from someone who can view the file.

File ownership and transfer options can depend on account type, organisation and policy. Do not assume that a personal file can be transferred to any work account. For this capstone, show ownership and access as a written plan or use only test accounts you control. Do not create real Workspace users, groups or sharing policies. Remove temporary practice access after review.

Complete the audit checklist with generic descriptions. Do not include an actual recipient's name, email address, file content or account screenshot in the course submission. A safe finding might say, "A practice folder inherited Editor access from its parent; moved the fictional draft to a restricted folder and checked the new role."

### Lesson 4: Account security and permission requests

Open account security directly from Google's account settings. Review 2-Step Verification, recovery options, passkeys if available, recent security activity and connected third-party apps. Remove only an app you recognise and no longer use; if you do not recognise an app, first check its publisher and what access it has. Do not approve an unexpected sign-in or permission request to make a lesson work.

OAuth is a standard way for an app to ask permission to connect to an account. A legitimate-looking permission screen can still request more access than a tool needs. Read the app name and each permission. If the request is unrelated to the task, cancel it and ask a trusted administrator. Never hand a third party your password or one-time code to "connect" a service.

Phishing can arrive by email, Chat, document comment or calendar invite. Be cautious with unexpected files, requests to change payment details, shortened links and urgency. Navigate to a known service directly rather than clicking an unverified login link. If you think an account is compromised, stop using the suspicious session, use Google's official recovery process and notify the responsible administrator through a known channel.

Review recovery methods before travelling or changing phones. A phone number can be helpful, but it should not be the only recovery option if you may lose access to it. Keep backup codes in a safe, private location. Never upload recovery codes to Drive or include them in a capstone file.

### Lesson 5: Workspace administration is a separate responsibility

A Google Workspace administrator manages an organisation's users, groups, services, sharing rules and security controls. The Admin console and policy options are for an authorised administrator, not for a student to experiment with on someone else's domain. A business Workspace plan and admin role may be required. Read current plan and administrator documentation before applying any setting.

For the capstone, describe an admin policy instead of configuring one: who may create users, how 2-Step Verification is encouraged or enforced, who can share externally, how staff leave access, and where the owner reviews security events. Use fictional roles and assume a qualified administrator will approve any real policy. Shared drives may help organisations keep team-owned files when members leave, but access and availability are edition- and policy-dependent. A personal account can still practise folder structure and least-privilege planning.

The optional [Associate Google Workspace Administrator certification](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/associate-google-workspace-administrator) is a separate Google Cloud exam. As checked on 12 November 2026, Google lists a two-hour exam and recommends six months of hands-on Super Admin experience. This course does not provide that experience or exam preparation. It is reasonable to learn the concepts first and decide later whether administration is a goal.

### Lesson 6: A safe workflow glimpse

The course has already shown how a Form can save responses to a Sheet. In a real process, an approved automation might notify a team when a new response arrives. Before automating, confirm the purpose, permissions, error handling, retention and a human review step. Do not send a customer confirmation from the course account or create an Apps Script or third-party integration for this assignment.

For more, continue to [Automation](/courses/automation/), which introduces Forms-to-Sheets workflows and automation. For account recovery, phishing and cloud security, review [Cybersecurity Foundations](/courses/cybersecurity/). These cross-links are for further study, not prerequisites for finishing the capstone.

### Lesson 7: Describe the work honestly

Workspace skills can support work such as virtual assistance, office administration and remote support, but course completion does not qualify someone automatically for a role. Describe what you actually built: "Created a fictional order tracker with validated inputs, a checked summary and role-based sharing plan." Include the tool, your contribution and evidence you can show safely. Do not claim to have administered a real organisation if you only completed a practice plan.

For service positioning and client communication, continue with [VA Launch](/courses/virtual-assistant/). For a portfolio, CV and remote-work routine, continue with [Remote Ready](/courses/remote-ready/). ## Common mistakes

- Recording a meeting or sharing a screen without checking consent and exposed content.
- Treating a Chat Space as a secure place for passwords or sensitive customer records.
- Giving every role Editor access because permission planning seems tedious.
- Assuming a protected Sheet range prevents someone from viewing or copying its values.
- Granting OAuth permissions without checking the app and scope.
- Editing organisation-wide Admin console settings without explicit authority.
- Describing a fictional team setup as a real business deployment.

## Practice Exercises

1. Draft a Calendar invitation with a time zone, short agenda and clear participation instructions.
2. Practise muting, captions and sharing one safe browser tab in a test meeting; do not record anyone.
3. Choose whether four sample messages belong in email, Chat, a task or a shared document.
4. Use the role matrix to give each fictional role the minimum file access it needs.
5. Complete a generic sharing audit and write one corrective action.
6. Review a mock OAuth permission screen; list what you would check before approving it.
7. Write a one-page admin policy outline without changing any account setting.

## Weekly Assignment: Remote Team Workspace Capstone

For the fictional six-role Fresh Bites team, submit:

1. A Drive folder map and role matrix with access levels for owner, two kitchen roles, two counter roles and the rider.
2. Three reusable templates: an order SOP, a weekly report and an order tracker.
3. A fictional Form-to-Sheet order or reservation workflow with a summary dashboard.
4. A shared team calendar plan with one recurring weekly meeting and the linked agenda template.
5. A completed security and sharing audit checklist using generic findings only.
6. A 10-minute walkthrough, live or recorded by the learner alone. No customer data or unconsented people may appear.

**Rubric:** organisation and sharing (25%); correct tool use (25%); collaboration design (20%); security (15%); documentation and walkthrough (15%).

**Definition of done:**

- [ ] The role matrix gives each role only the access needed for its task.
- [ ] The tracker and Form use fictional information and avoid unnecessary personal fields.
- [ ] The response Sheet and Drive folders have separate, checked permissions.
- [ ] The recurring meeting has a time zone, agenda and review owner.
- [ ] The audit includes at least one risk and a practical corrective action.
- [ ] The walkthrough distinguishes a practice design from an actual deployed Workspace.

## Facilitator Notes

Do not connect to, create or modify any learner, owner or organisation Google account. The rubric assesses planning and practice artifacts, not access to paid Workspace administration. Do not request account screenshots that expose real email, calendars, membership or security settings. A learner may provide a written configuration plan instead of using external sharing or recording. Confirm all participants consent before any live walkthrough.
