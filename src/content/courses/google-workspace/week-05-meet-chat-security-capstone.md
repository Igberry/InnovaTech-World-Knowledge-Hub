**Goal:** This week you'll pull the whole course together: run a low-bandwidth meeting, audit who can see what, and build a documented, secure Remote Team Workspace for a fictional business. Mostly building and testing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Schedule and run a Google Meet with sensible audio, video and screen-share choices
- Choose between email, Chat and a shared doc for a given task
- Audit Drive links and permissions using least privilege
- Review account security and spot phishing and risky OAuth requests
- Tell the difference between using Workspace and administering it
- Present a documented workspace without exposing personal data

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open the [capstone brief](/downloads/google-workspace/capstone-brief.md), the [sharing audit checklist](/downloads/google-workspace/sharing-audit-checklist.md), the [meeting agenda](/downloads/google-workspace/meeting-agenda-template.md) and the [six-role access matrix](/downloads/google-workspace/team-roles-matrix.csv).
- Warm-up: for each role in the matrix, write one line on which files it should and should not see.

## Try it 1 - Run a low-bandwidth, inclusive meeting

**Why it matters:** meetings in Yenagoa often happen on patchy data and power. Running one well is a real, hireable skill.

**Do this now:**
1. Schedule a meeting from Calendar with a clear title, time zone, agenda and who should prepare what.
2. Before joining, test your mic and camera. If data is tight, turn off video and go audio-only.
3. Share only the one tab or window you need - close private tabs and notifications first - then stop presenting when done.
4. Turn on captions and use hand-raise to take turns.

**You've got it when:** you can run the meeting audio-only if the connection drops, and you only ever shared the safe, needed window.

> Never record a meeting without a clear reason, consent and a retention plan. For the capstone, a written walkthrough or a solo recording of fictional content is enough.

## Try it 2 - Pick the right channel

**Why it matters:** choosing email vs Chat vs a shared doc keeps a team coordinated instead of drowning in pings.

**Do this now:**
1. Take four sample messages and decide: email (durable summary), Chat (quick coordination), task (a due date) or shared doc (needs a stable record).
2. Write one Chat message the clear way: context, the action, the owner, the due date. Example: "The fictional weekly report is ready for review. Please comment by Friday; the owner approves the final copy."
3. Check a Space's membership and history before posting - and never post credentials or customer details there.

**You've got it when:** each of the four messages has a channel and a reason, and your Chat message says who does what by when.

## Try it 3 - Audit links, roles and ownership

**Why it matters:** this is the heart of workspace security. Give each role only what its job needs.

**Do this now:**
1. Use the access matrix to map each folder and file to a role. Give the rider only the route sheet, the cook only the kitchen procedure, the counter role only the order form and status.
2. Check folder inheritance: a broad parent permission flows to its children, so inspect before making a one-file exception.
3. Re-check any "Anyone with the link" access and tighten it. Start every share at the narrowest role.
4. Fill in the audit checklist with generic findings only - no real names, emails or screenshots. For example: "A practice folder inherited Editor access from its parent; moved the draft to a restricted folder and checked the new role."

**You've got it when:** each fictional role can reach exactly what it needs and nothing more, and your audit notes one fixed risk.

## Try it 4 - Harden the account and vet permission requests

**Why it matters:** one approved rogue app or phishing link can undo every other control.

**Do this now:**
1. Open account security directly and review 2-Step Verification, recovery options, recent activity and connected third-party apps.
2. Remove one app you recognise and no longer use. If you don't recognise an app, check its publisher and access first.
3. Look at a mock OAuth consent screen and list what you'd check: the app name, each permission, and whether the access even fits the task. If it asks for more than it needs, cancel.
4. Store backup codes somewhere private - never on Drive or in a capstone file.

**You've got it when:** you can walk through an OAuth screen and say exactly why you would approve or cancel it.

> Phishing arrives by email, Chat, a doc comment or a calendar invite. Navigate to a service yourself rather than clicking an unverified login link, and never hand over a password or one-time code to "connect" a tool.

## Try it 5 - Describe admin, don't configure it

**Why it matters:** administering a real organisation is a separate, authorised role. Knowing the boundary keeps you and the business safe.

**Do this now:**
1. Write a one-page admin policy outline for the fictional team: who creates users, how 2-Step Verification is encouraged, who can share externally, how leavers lose access, who reviews security events.
2. Use fictional roles and assume a qualified administrator approves anything real.
3. Do not touch a real Admin console or create real users, groups or sharing policies.

**You've got it when:** you have a clear written policy and did not change a single live account setting.

### Watch out for

- Recording or screen-sharing without checking consent and what's exposed.
- Treating a Chat Space as a safe place for passwords or customer records.
- Giving every role Editor "to save time".
- Assuming a protected Sheet range hides data from a viewer.
- Granting OAuth permissions without checking the app and scope.
- Editing organisation-wide Admin settings without authority.
- Describing a fictional setup as a real deployment.

## This week's build: the Remote Team Workspace capstone

For the fictional six-role Fresh Bites team, build and document a workspace that proves job-ready skills:

1. A Drive folder map and role matrix with access levels for owner, two kitchen roles, two counter roles and the rider.
2. Three reusable templates: an order SOP, a weekly report and an order tracker.
3. A fictional Form-to-Sheet order or reservation workflow with a summary dashboard.
4. A shared team calendar plan with one recurring weekly meeting and the linked agenda template.
5. A completed security and sharing audit using generic findings only.
6. A 10-minute walkthrough, live or recorded by you alone - no customer data or unconsented people on screen.

*Tip: describe honestly what you built, for example "Created a fictional order tracker with validated inputs, a checked summary and a role-based sharing plan." Completing the course builds skills; it does not by itself qualify you for a specific role.*

### Definition of done

- [ ] The role matrix gives each role only the access its task needs.
- [ ] The tracker and Form use fictional data and avoid unnecessary personal fields.
- [ ] The response Sheet and Drive folders have separate, checked permissions.
- [ ] The recurring meeting has a time zone, agenda and review owner.
- [ ] The audit includes at least one risk and a practical corrective action.
- [ ] The walkthrough makes clear this is a practice design, not a deployed Workspace.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Agree on safe meeting and screen-sharing boundaries |
| 10–25 min | Review the role matrix and capstone folder map |
| 25–40 min | Conduct a sample permissions audit and discuss inherited access |
| 40–55 min | Review a fictional Form-to-Sheet flow and human check |
| 55–70 min | Practise a short Meet walkthrough with captions or audio-only fallback |
| 70–82 min | Learner presents one security risk and its mitigation |
| 82–90 min | Give feedback, confirm artifacts and plan next steps |

## Facilitator Notes

Do not connect to, create or modify any learner, owner or organisation Google account. The rubric assesses planning and practice artifacts, not access to paid Workspace administration. Do not request account screenshots that expose real email, calendars, membership or security settings. A learner may provide a written configuration plan instead of using external sharing or recording. Confirm all participants consent before any live walkthrough.
