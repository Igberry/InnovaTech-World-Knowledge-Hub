**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have chosen one strong post, set a clear objective for it, defined a simple target audience, set a small daily budget you can afford, worked out what one result costs, and written your own rules for not wasting money. You learn by doing, not reading. Note: you can set up and review a boost without ever pressing the final publish button, so you can complete every exercise even with zero budget.

## What you'll be able to do
- Pick a post worth putting money behind.
- Match a boost to a clear objective instead of guessing.
- Describe a target audience in plain words and set it up.
- Read the cost of one result and decide if a boost is worth continuing.

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open a Google Doc titled "Week 5 Boost Plan".
- Open your platform's insights and find the one post from the last month with the most saves, shares, or comments.
- Warm-up: write that post's link and its numbers at the top of your doc. This is your candidate for boosting.

## Try it 1 - Pick a post and set one clear objective
**Why it matters:** Boosting a weak post wastes money. Boosting your best post, with one clear goal, multiplies something that already works.
**Do this now:**
1. Look at your three best-performing posts. Choose the one that already got real engagement from strangers, not just friends.
2. Decide the single thing you want this boost to do. Pick one only: more profile visits, more messages, or more link clicks.
3. Write it as a sentence in your doc: "I am boosting [post] so that more people [your one objective]."
4. Open the boost or promote screen on that post and select the objective that matches your sentence.
**You've got it when:** Your doc names one post and one objective, and the boost screen shows that same objective selected.

## Try it 2 - Define a simple target audience
**Why it matters:** Showing your boost to the right people costs far less per result than showing it to everyone. A tight audience spends your money where it counts.
**Do this now:**
1. In your doc, describe your ideal viewer in one line: their location, their age range, and one interest. Example: "Lagos and Abuja, ages 24 to 40, interested in small business."
2. On the boost screen, choose "Create your own audience" rather than the automatic option.
3. Enter the location, age range, and one or two interests that match your line.
4. Check the estimated audience size the tool shows. If it is in the millions, it is too broad; add another interest to narrow it.
**You've got it when:** Your custom audience is saved on the boost screen and its estimated size is specific, not the whole country.

## Try it 3 - Set a small daily budget you can afford
**Why it matters:** Ads can quietly drain money. Deciding your limit before you start keeps you in control.
**Do this now:**
1. Decide the total you are willing to lose this week if the boost teaches you nothing. Be honest; use a number that would not hurt.
2. Divide that total by the number of days you will run it to get a daily budget. For example, a 5,000 Naira test over 5 days is 1,000 Naira a day.
3. Enter the daily budget and the end date on the boost screen.
4. Record both the daily and total figures in your doc under "My Budget".
**You've got it when:** Your boost screen shows a daily budget and a fixed end date that match the affordable total written in your doc.

## Try it 4 - Read what one result costs
**Why it matters:** The only number that tells you if a boost works is the cost per result. Without it you are guessing.
**Do this now:**
1. If you have a past boost, open its results. If not, use this practice example: 1,000 Naira spent produced 20 profile visits.
2. Divide the money spent by the number of results. In the example, 1,000 divided by 20 equals 50 Naira per profile visit.
3. Ask yourself: is one new interested visitor worth that price to me? Write your yes or no answer and why.
4. Save the calculation and your verdict in your doc under "Cost Per Result".
**You've got it when:** Your doc shows a cost-per-result figure you worked out yourself and a one-line decision on whether it is worth it.

## Try it 5 - Write your rules to avoid wasting money
**Why it matters:** Clear rules protect you when excitement or impatience tempts you to overspend.
**Do this now:**
1. In your doc, start a list titled "My Ad Safety Rules".
2. Write at least five rules in your own words. Strong starting ones: never boost a post that flopped organically; always set an end date; check cost per result before adding money; stop a boost that costs more than my chosen limit per result; never enter card details on a link sent by a stranger.
3. Add one rule that fits your own weakness, for example "I will not boost late at night when I am tempted to raise the budget."
4. Keep this list somewhere you will see it before every future boost.
**You've got it when:** You have at least five clear, personal safety rules saved in your doc.

## Weekly Assignment
Produce a complete "Boost Plan" for one real post, set up but not necessarily published. Deliverable: one Google Doc containing (1) the chosen post and its objective sentence, (2) your custom audience line and its estimated size, (3) your daily and total budget, (4) a worked cost-per-result calculation with your verdict, and (5) your five-plus ad safety rules. Add a screenshot of your boost setup screen showing the objective, audience, and budget filled in. Done looks like a plan a cautious friend could follow without overspending, submitted through your account. You decide whether to press publish; the learning is in the plan, not the spend.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Each learner states the post they chose and their one objective. Mentor flags anyone boosting a post that only friends engaged with. |
| 15-45 min | Audience workshop. Learners build a custom audience live and compare estimated sizes; the group narrows any that are too broad by adding interests. |
| 45-75 min | Budget and maths drill. Work through two or three cost-per-result examples together until everyone can divide spend by results confidently and judge the price. |
| 75-90 min | Share and combine everyone's safety rules into one class list, then brief next week: analytics and the one-week capstone campaign. |

## Facilitator Notes
The biggest risk this week is real money lost, so stress that every exercise can be completed in setup mode without publishing. Many learners confuse reach with results; keep returning to cost per result as the only honest scorecard. Watch for audiences left on the automatic, country-wide setting, which burns budget fast.
