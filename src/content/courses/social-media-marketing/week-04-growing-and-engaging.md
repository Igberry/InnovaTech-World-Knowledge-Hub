**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have built a saved set of hashtags and keywords, written five genuine replies, run a 20-minute daily engagement routine for at least three days, sent one collaboration message, and replied to a DM using your own template. You learn by doing, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do
- Find the hashtags and keywords your audience actually searches for.
- Engage daily in a way that pulls in new followers without feeling fake.
- Reply to comments and DMs in a warm, professional voice.
- Reach out to another creator or page for a simple collaboration.

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open a fresh Google Doc and title it "Week 4 Growth Log". This is where every exercise below gets saved.
- Open the social platform you have been posting on since Week 1.
- Warm-up: scroll your feed for two minutes and screenshot one post from someone in your niche that got lots of comments. Paste it into your doc. You will study it later.

## Try it 1 - Build a saved research set
**Why it matters:** People find new accounts through search and hashtags. If your posts carry the words your audience types, strangers discover you for free.
**Do this now:**
1. In your platform's search bar, type one word that describes your niche, for example "ankara styles" or "lagos food". Watch the suggestions that drop down. Those are real searches.
2. Write the top eight suggestions into your doc under a heading "Keywords".
3. Now tap the hashtag tab and search the same word. Note how many posts each hashtag has. Pick five small tags (under 100k posts) and five medium tags (100k to 1 million).
4. Save these ten under a heading "My Hashtag Set". Small tags help you rank; medium tags give reach.
**You've got it when:** Your doc has eight keywords and ten hashtags split into small and medium, ready to paste into your next caption.

## Try it 2 - Write five genuine replies
**Why it matters:** A thoughtful reply on a bigger account puts you in front of their audience. Generic comments like "Nice post" get ignored. Specific ones get clicks.
**Do this now:**
1. Open five posts from accounts in your niche that have more followers than you.
2. For each, read the caption fully, then write a reply that adds something: a tip, a question, or a short honest reaction to a detail in the post.
3. Keep each reply to one or two sentences. No links, no "check my page".
4. Post all five, then paste the text of each into your doc under "My Five Replies".
**You've got it when:** You have posted five replies that each mention a specific detail from the post, and none of them say only "nice" or "great".

## Try it 3 - Run the 20-minute daily routine
**Why it matters:** Consistent small effort beats one big burst. A fixed routine means growth happens even on busy days.
**Do this now:**
1. Set a timer for 20 minutes. Spend the first 5 replying to every comment and DM you already have.
2. Spend the next 10 commenting on posts under three or four of the hashtags from Try it 1.
3. Spend the last 5 following five accounts that are active in your niche and liking their recent posts.
4. In your doc, log the date and a one-line note on what you did. Repeat this on at least three days this week.
**You've got it when:** Your doc shows three dated routine logs, each one taking about 20 minutes.

## Try it 4 - Send one collaboration message
**Why it matters:** A shoutout, a shared post, or a simple feature exposes you to a whole new audience at once. Most people never ask, so asking already puts you ahead.
**Do this now:**
1. Pick one account close to your size (roughly the same follower count) that serves the same audience but is not a direct rival.
2. Draft a short, warm DM: greet them by name, say one true thing you like about their work, then propose one small idea, for example "Want to each share one of the other's posts this week?"
3. Keep it under four sentences. Send it.
4. Paste your message into your doc under "Collab Outreach" and note the date.
**You've got it when:** You have sent one real, specific collaboration DM and saved a copy of it.

## Try it 5 - Reply to DMs with a simple template
**Why it matters:** DMs are where interest turns into trust. A calm, professional reply makes a stranger feel safe to keep talking.
**Do this now:**
1. In your doc, write a reusable three-part template: a greeting, a helpful answer line, and a friendly next step. Example: "Hi, thanks for reaching out. [Answer their question here.] Would it help if I shared a quick example?"
2. Find one real DM in your inbox (or ask a friend to send you a test question).
3. Reply using your template, filling the middle with a genuine answer. Never copy-paste the brackets; always write the real words.
4. Save the final reply in your doc under "DM Template In Action".
**You've got it when:** You have a saved template and one real DM answered with it that reads naturally, with no leftover brackets.

## Weekly Assignment
Build and run your "Growth Kit" for a full week. Deliverable: one Google Doc that contains (1) your saved keyword and hashtag set from Try it 1, (2) five posted replies, (3) at least three dated 20-minute routine logs, (4) one collaboration DM you sent, and (5) your DM template with one real reply. At the top of the doc, write three sentences on what felt easy, what felt awkward, and which single habit you will keep. Done looks like a complete doc with all five parts filled in with your own real activity, not samples, submitted through your account.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Each learner shares their hashtag set and explains why they split small versus medium tags. Mentor corrects any that chose only huge tags. |
| 15-45 min | Live reply practice. Learners open a real post and write a reply out loud; the group rates each as generic or specific and rewrites the weak ones together. |
| 45-75 min | DM and collaboration role-play in pairs. One plays a cautious stranger, the other answers with their template. Swap, then draft one collab message each and get peer feedback. |
| 75-90 min | Review routine logs, troubleshoot who skipped days and why, and brief next week: we move from free growth to a small paid boost. |

## Facilitator Notes
Learners most often get stuck writing replies that still sound like spam; push them to quote a specific detail from the post before they comment. Watch for anyone chasing only million-plus hashtags, which buries their posts instantly. The collaboration DM causes the most hesitation, so normalise rejection early and celebrate the act of asking regardless of the answer.
