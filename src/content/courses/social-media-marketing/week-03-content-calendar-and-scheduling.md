**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have three to four content pillars, a filled two-week content calendar, six finished posts batched in Canva, and a scheduled queue in Meta Business Suite. You learn by planning and scheduling real posts, not by reading about consistency.

## What you'll be able to do

- Define content pillars so you never run out of ideas
- Build a two-week calendar that mixes value, trust and selling
- Batch-make several posts at once using a reusable template
- Schedule posts in advance and set a rhythm you can actually keep

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open Canva, Meta Business Suite, and a blank Google Sheet, plus your Week 1 and Week 2 work.
- Warm-up: list every post idea in your head right now, even messy ones. You will sort them into pillars next.

## Try it 1 - Define three to four content pillars

**Why it matters:** pillars are repeating themes. With four of them you always know what to post, and your page feels focused instead of random.

**Do this now:**
1. Look at your warm-up list and group the ideas into themes.
2. Name three to four pillars. A common mix is: Teach (tips), Trust (behind the scenes, results), Connect (stories, questions), and Sell (offers).
3. Write one example post under each pillar.

**You've got it when:** you have three to four named pillars, each with one example post.

## Try it 2 - Build a two-week content calendar

**Why it matters:** a calendar turns "I should post" into a plan you can follow without deciding from scratch every day.

**Do this now:**
1. In Google Sheets, make columns: Date, Platform, Pillar, Hook, Format, Caption status.
2. Fill two weeks at your chosen rhythm (for example three posts a week is six rows). Rotate your pillars so no two posts in a row are the same type.
3. Write the hook for each row now, reusing and expanding your Week 2 hooks.

**You've got it when:** you have a two-week grid where every row has a date, pillar, format and a hook.

## Try it 3 - Make a reusable Canva template

**Why it matters:** a template means every post matches your brand sheet and you design faster each time.

**Do this now:**
1. In Canva, open an Instagram post and apply your brand sheet colours and fonts from the Setup Guide.
2. Set a text box for the hook, a space for an image, and a small logo or handle in the corner.
3. Save it, then use "Make a copy" so the original stays clean as your master template.

**You've got it when:** you have one on-brand master template you can copy for every post.

## Try it 4 - Batch-make six posts

**Why it matters:** making six posts in one sitting is far faster than making one post six times, and it keeps your look consistent.

**Do this now:**
1. Copy your template six times, one per post from your calendar.
2. Drop in each hook and swap the image or colours to fit that post.
3. Write or paste the caption and call to action for each (reuse your Week 2 style).

**You've got it when:** six finished posts sit in Canva, each matching your brand and tied to a calendar row.

## Try it 5 - Schedule them in Meta Business Suite

**Why it matters:** scheduling means your page stays active even on busy days, which is what builds an audience over time.

**Do this now:**
1. In Meta Business Suite, open the Planner and click Create post.
2. Upload a post, paste its caption, choose Instagram and Facebook, then set the date and time from your calendar.
3. Repeat for all six. Space them out across the two weeks instead of all at once.

**You've got it when:** six posts show as Scheduled in the Meta Business Suite Planner on your chosen dates.

## Try it 6 - Set a rhythm you can keep

**Why it matters:** posting three times a week forever beats posting daily for one week and then vanishing. Consistency is what the algorithm and your audience reward.

**Do this now:**
1. Decide an honest number of posts per week you can sustain (three is a solid start).
2. Block one weekly "batch day" in your phone calendar for making next week's posts.
3. Write one sentence rule for yourself, for example "I batch every Sunday and schedule for the week."

**You've got it when:** you have a weekly posting number, a batch day booked, and a one-sentence rule.

## Weekly Assignment

Produce and submit a "Two-Week Content System". It must include:

1. Your three to four named content pillars with an example each (Try it 1).
2. A completed two-week calendar in Google Sheets (Try it 2).
3. Six finished posts, on-brand and batched in Canva (Try it 4).
4. A screenshot of the six posts Scheduled in Meta Business Suite (Try it 5).
5. Your posting rhythm and batch-day rule (Try it 6).

Done looks like: a real queue of six on-brand posts scheduled across two weeks, planned from pillars, that would keep a page active without daily effort.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Review Week 2 content packs; each learner names their likely pillars |
| 15-45 min | Build the two-week calendar together; check the pillar mix is balanced |
| 45-75 min | Batch posts from one shared template; troubleshoot Canva and brand consistency |
| 75-90 min | Schedule live in Meta Business Suite; agree posting rhythms; brief next steps |

## Facilitator Notes

Learners often set an unrealistic rhythm like daily posting and burn out by week two, so steer them to a number they can keep forever. The template step is where consistency is won or lost; make sure everyone builds one master and copies it rather than redesigning each post. Some will hit account-connection snags in Meta Business Suite, so leave time to fix logins and permissions live.

## Can you do these now?

1. What are your content pillars, and why do they stop you running out of ideas?
2. How does batching and a template make you post more consistently?
3. How do you schedule a post in Meta Business Suite ahead of time?
