**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have mapped where your target audience really hangs out, written a clear one-paragraph persona, studied three accounts that already serve a similar audience, and chosen the two platforms you will focus on. You learn by looking at real accounts, not by reading theory.

## What you'll be able to do

- Describe your target audience as one specific person, not "everyone"
- Say which platforms your audience actually uses and why
- Learn from accounts that already reach the people you want
- Commit to two platforms so your effort is focused, not spread thin

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, and a blank doc for notes.
- Warm-up: think of one real person you have helped or sold to before, and write their first name at the top of your doc. That person is your starting point.

## Try it 1 - Map where your audience actually spends time

**Why it matters:** posting on the wrong platform is wasted effort. You want to be where your people already scroll.

**Do this now:**
1. In your doc, list the platforms: Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp, X, LinkedIn.
2. For the person you named, mark each platform High, Medium or Low for how likely they are to be there. A market trader in Onitsha may be High on Facebook and WhatsApp; a 22-year-old student may be High on TikTok and Instagram.
3. Circle the two platforms you marked High.

**You've got it when:** you have two circled platforms backed by a reason about who your audience is, not a guess.

## Try it 2 - Write a one-paragraph audience persona

**Why it matters:** when you write to one clear person, your posts sound human. "Everyone" leads to posts that reach no one.

**Do this now, write one paragraph covering:**
1. Name, age range, and location (for example "Ada, 28 to 35, Port Harcourt").
2. What they do and what they worry about or want.
3. What would make them stop scrolling and follow you.

**You've got it when:** you have a paragraph specific enough that a stranger could picture the person and name one thing they care about.

## Try it 3 - Study three accounts that serve a similar audience

**Why it matters:** you do not need to guess what works. Accounts ahead of you have already tested it.

**Do this now:**
1. On your two platforms, search your topic and find three accounts that reach the audience you want (local is fine).
2. For each one, note in your doc: their three most-liked post types, how often they post, and the first line they use to hook people.
3. Write one thing from each account you could borrow (an idea, not a copy).

**You've got it when:** you have notes on three accounts and three specific ideas you could try yourself.

## Try it 4 - Note what your audience responds to

**Why it matters:** the comments on other accounts are free research into what your audience likes and dislikes.

**Do this now:**
1. On the three accounts, read 10 to 15 comments across their popular posts.
2. In your doc, list three things people ask about, praise, or complain about.
3. Turn each into a post idea you could make (for example a complaint about high prices becomes a "how to choose on a budget" post).

**You've got it when:** you have three post ideas that come straight from what real people said in comments.

## Try it 5 - Choose your two focus platforms and write why

**Why it matters:** two platforms done well beats five done badly. A clear choice protects your time.

**Do this now:**
1. From Try it 1, confirm your two platforms.
2. Write one sentence per platform: why your audience is there and what kind of content fits it (for example "TikTok for short how-to videos, because my audience is young and watches a lot of video").
3. Decide which of the two is your main platform and which is secondary.

**You've got it when:** you can name your two platforms, a reason for each, and which one is primary.

## Weekly Assignment

Produce a one-page "Audience and Platform Plan" that you will reuse all course. It must include:

1. Your one-paragraph audience persona (Try it 2).
2. Your two chosen platforms, with one reason each and which is primary (Try it 5).
3. A short study of three accounts: post types, posting frequency, and hook style (Try it 3).
4. Five post ideas drawn from real comments and account research (Try it 2 to 4).

Save it as a PDF or Canva page. Done looks like: a single, tidy page a stranger could read and understand exactly who you serve, where, and what you will post.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Welcome; each learner says who they want to reach in one sentence |
| 15-45 min | Build personas together; sharpen vague ones into specific people |
| 45-75 min | Study example accounts live on screen; pull out hooks, formats and posting rhythm |
| 75-90 min | Each learner commits to two platforms out loud; brief the assignment and Week 2 |

## Facilitator Notes

The most common stumble is a persona that is still "everyone", so push hard for a single named person with an age range and a location. Learners often want to be on every platform; hold them to two so they can actually keep up. When they study other accounts, redirect any instinct to copy captions word for word toward borrowing the idea and making it their own.

## Can you do these now?

1. Who is your audience, described as one specific person?
2. Which two platforms will you focus on, and why each?
3. What did you learn from studying three accounts that serve a similar audience?
