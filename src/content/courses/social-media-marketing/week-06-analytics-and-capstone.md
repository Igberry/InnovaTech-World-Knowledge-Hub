**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have read reach, engagement, and saves in your native insights, found your single best post and explained why it worked, set one measurable goal, and planned and run a one-week content campaign that you present with a short results write-up. You learn by doing, not reading. This is your capstone: everything from Weeks 1 to 5 comes together into one real campaign.

## What you'll be able to do
- Read the three numbers that matter most in native insights.
- Spot your best post and explain the reason it performed.
- Turn a vague wish into one measurable goal.
- Plan, run, and report on a one-week campaign end to end.

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)
- Open a Google Doc titled "Week 6 Capstone".
- Open your platform's native insights (Professional dashboard on Instagram, Insights on Facebook, Analytics on TikTok or X).
- Warm-up: write down today's follower count and the number of posts you made over the last 30 days. This is your starting line.

## Try it 1 - Read reach, engagement, and saves
**Why it matters:** These three numbers tell you how many people you reached, how many cared, and how many found a post worth keeping. Everything else is noise at this stage.
**Do this now:**
1. In insights, find your reach for the last 30 days and write it in your doc.
2. Find total engagement (likes, comments, shares together) and write that down.
3. Open your three most recent posts and note the saves on each. Saves are the strongest signal of real value.
4. Under a heading "My Three Numbers", record reach, engagement, and your highest save count.
**You've got it when:** Your doc clearly shows reach, engagement, and saves, each pulled from your own insights, not guessed.

## Try it 2 - Find your best post and say why it won
**Why it matters:** Your best post is a free template. Understanding why it worked lets you repeat it on purpose instead of by luck.
**Do this now:**
1. Sort or scan your last month of posts and pick the one with the most saves or shares.
2. Study it like a detective. Note the hook in the first line, the format (reel, carousel, single image), the topic, and when you posted it.
3. In your doc, write three reasons it likely won, each tied to something you can see, for example "the first line asked a question" or "it solved one clear problem".
4. Write one sentence on how you will reuse this pattern in your campaign.
**You've got it when:** Your doc names your best post and lists three evidence-based reasons it worked, plus one reuse idea.

## Try it 3 - Set one measurable goal
**Why it matters:** "Grow my page" cannot be measured, so it cannot be achieved on purpose. A specific number and date make success obvious.
**Do this now:**
1. Pick one metric from Try it 1 to move: reach, engagement, saves, profile visits, or new followers.
2. Write a goal with a number and a deadline. Example: "Reach 2,000 accounts over the next 7 days" or "Get 15 new followers this week".
3. Check your goal is realistic by comparing it to last week's numbers; aim for a stretch, not a fantasy.
4. Save it in your doc under "My One Goal" and write the exact date you will check it.
**You've got it when:** Your doc holds one goal with a specific number and a specific date, grounded in your real recent numbers.

## Try it 4 - Plan a one-week campaign
**Why it matters:** A plan turns scattered posting into a focused push toward your goal. Planning the week in advance removes the daily "what do I post?" panic.
**Do this now:**
1. In a simple table in Google Docs or Sheets, list the seven days of the coming week.
2. For each day, decide one action: a post, a reel, a story, or an engagement-only day. Reuse the winning pattern from Try it 2 for at least two posts.
3. Write the hook or topic for each content day in the table, and apply your saved hashtag set from Week 4.
4. Add the 20-minute daily routine from Week 4 to every day so growth and posting work together.
**You've got it when:** You have a seven-day table filled with a specific action and topic for each day, aimed at your one goal.

## Try it 5 - Run and track the campaign
**Why it matters:** A plan only teaches you something once you run it and watch the numbers move. Tracking daily lets you adjust instead of waiting until it is too late.
**Do this now:**
1. Start executing your plan from day one. Post what the table says and run the daily routine.
2. Each evening, add one row to a tracking section in your doc: the date, what you posted, and that post's early reach and engagement.
3. On day four, check progress against your goal. If you are far behind, boost your strongest post using the safe method from Week 5 or double your engagement time.
4. Keep the daily log going until day seven.
**You've got it when:** Your doc shows a daily tracking log across the campaign week, with a mid-week check and any adjustment you made.

## Weekly Assignment
This is your capstone: run a real one-week mini-campaign from start to finish and present it. Deliverable: one Google Doc that contains (1) your three starting numbers, (2) your best-post analysis, (3) your one measurable goal, (4) your seven-day campaign plan, (5) your daily tracking log, and (6) a short results write-up of about 200 words. The write-up must state whether you hit your goal, show the before and after numbers, name the one post that performed best and why, and list one thing you will do differently next time. Done looks like a complete campaign a stranger could read and understand exactly what you set out to do, what happened, and what you learned, submitted through your account. This capstone proves you can plan, run, and measure social media marketing on your own, which is the job-ready skill this course builds toward.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Each learner reads their three numbers and their one goal aloud. Mentor checks every goal has a number and a date. |
| 15-45 min | Best-post clinic. Learners present their winning post and their three reasons; the group pressure-tests whether the reasons are evidence or guesses. |
| 45-75 min | Campaign presentations. Each learner walks through their seven-day plan and tracking log, and the group suggests one adjustment for anyone behind their goal. |
| 75-90 min | Reflection and close. Learners share their biggest lesson from the six weeks and name the one habit they will keep going. Mentor points to next steps for continued practice. |

## Facilitator Notes
This capstone week reveals who internalised the earlier weeks and who posted randomly; use the best-post analysis to surface that. Learners commonly set unmeasurable goals, so hold the line on a number and a date before they plan anything. Encourage honest write-ups even when a goal is missed, since a clear-eyed miss teaches more than a vague win. This section is for staff only and is stripped from the published site.
