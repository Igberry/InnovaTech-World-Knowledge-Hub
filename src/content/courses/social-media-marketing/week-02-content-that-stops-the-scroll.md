**Goal:** By the end of this week you will have written five hooks for one topic, turned a single idea into three different post formats, written captions that end with a clear call to action, and filmed a short video that earns attention in the first three seconds. You learn by making posts, not by reading about them.

## What you'll be able to do

- Write hooks that make people stop scrolling
- Stretch one idea into a single image, a carousel, and a short video
- Write captions that lead to a clear next step
- Grab attention in the first three seconds of a video

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open Canva, your phone camera, and your Audience and Platform Plan from Week 1.
- Warm-up: pick one topic you could teach your audience in under a minute. Write it at the top of your notes. Everything this week uses that one topic.

## Try it 1 - Write five scroll-stopping hooks

**Why it matters:** the first line decides whether anyone reads the rest. A weak hook wastes a good post.

**Do this now:**
1. Take your one topic. Write five different opening lines using these starters: a question, a bold claim, a mistake people make, a number ("3 things..."), and a before-and-after.
2. Read each one out loud. Cut any that need more than six words to make sense.
3. Star the two hooks that made you most curious.

**You've got it when:** you have five hooks for one topic and two starred favourites.

## Try it 2 - Turn one idea into three formats

**Why it matters:** one idea can become a week of content. This is how creators post often without running dry.

**Do this now:**
1. **Single image:** in Canva, make one post where your starred hook is the biggest text on the image.
2. **Carousel:** make a 3 to 5 slide carousel that breaks the same idea into steps, one point per slide.
3. **Short video:** plan a 20 to 30 second video saying the same idea out loud, with your hook as the opening line.

**You've got it when:** the same idea exists as an image, a carousel, and a planned video.

## Try it 3 - Write captions that end with a call to action

**Why it matters:** a post without a next step gets a like and nothing more. The call to action turns watchers into followers, DMs, or customers.

**Do this now:**
1. Write a caption for your single image: hook line, two to three short lines of value, then one clear ask.
2. Make the ask specific: "Save this for later", "Comment YES if this helped", or "DM 'START' to begin".
3. Add three to five relevant hashtags your audience actually searches.

**You've got it when:** your caption ends with one clear, specific action, not a vague "follow for more".

## Try it 4 - Practise the first-3-seconds rule

**Why it matters:** on video, people decide in three seconds whether to keep watching. The opening is everything.

**Do this now:**
1. Film three different 5-second openings for your video idea: one where you say the hook straight to camera, one that starts mid-action, and one that shows the end result first.
2. Watch all three back. Which one makes you want to keep watching?
3. Keep the winner as the opening of your full short video.

**You've got it when:** you have a 5-second opening that makes you want to see what happens next.

## Try it 5 - Record and review your short video

**Why it matters:** the only way to get comfortable on camera is to film, watch, and film again.

**Do this now:**
1. Using your winning opening, film the full 20 to 30 second video in good light, phone steady or propped.
2. Keep sentences short. Speak a little slower and clearer than feels natural.
3. Watch it back once. Note one thing to improve and refilm if you have time.

**You've got it when:** you have a finished short video that opens strong and makes one clear point.

## Weekly Assignment

Create a "Content Pack" for one topic and submit it. It must include:

1. Five hooks with your two favourites starred (Try it 1).
2. The same idea in three formats: a single image, a carousel, and a short video (Try it 2, 4, 5).
3. A finished caption with a specific call to action and relevant hashtags (Try it 3).

Save the images and caption as a PDF or Canva page, and keep the video file. Done looks like: one idea, fully produced three ways, each with a strong first line and a clear next step.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes) {advanced}
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-15 min | Warm-up; each learner reads one hook aloud, group says stop or scroll |
| 15-45 min | Build the three formats in Canva together from one shared idea |
| 45-75 min | Film 5-second openings on phones; watch back and compare the three styles |
| 75-90 min | Workshop captions and calls to action; brief the assignment and Week 3 |

## Facilitator Notes

Learners write captions before hooks and then wonder why nothing lands, so insist the hook comes first every time. On video, the biggest blocker is self-consciousness; normalise that the first take always feels awkward and that watching it back is the skill. Watch for vague calls to action like "follow for more" and push for one specific, single ask per post.

## Can you do these now?

1. What makes a hook stop the scroll, and can you write three?
2. How do you turn one idea into three different formats?
3. Why does every post need a clear call to action, and what is a strong one?
