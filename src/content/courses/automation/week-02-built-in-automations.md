**Goal:** Get real time savings *this week* using automations already built into the tools you have - email
rules, templates, calendar features, phone shortcuts. No new tools, very little risk, and you will feel the
difference the same day. You learn by switching them on.

## What you'll be able to do

- Create email rules (filters) that sort and handle mail for you
- Save and reuse templates and signatures
- Use calendar automations: scheduling, reminders, recurring events
- Use simple, safe phone automations
- Judge which built-in automations are safe to trust quickly

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your email, calendar, and phone, plus the
  [email rules cheat-sheet](/downloads/automation/email-rules-cheatsheet.md).
- Warm-up: search "[your email] create a filter" and keep the help page open in a tab. That is all the
  reading you need today - the rest is clicking.

## Try it 1 - Make an email rule (the easiest safe win)

**Why it matters:** your email already automates with rules (Gmail calls them filters). It only touches
your own inbox, so it is the safest place to start: match, then act.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick one sender you get a lot (a bank, a newsletter, a receipts sender).
2. Create a rule: WHEN mail comes from that sender, DO label it and archive it.
3. Make two more from the cheat-sheet, for example: WHEN it contains "invoice", DO label "To pay"; WHEN from your top client, DO star it.
4. Prefer **archive** or **label** over delete, and never auto-forward mail outside your organisation.

**You've got it when:** three rules are live and you can see what each one catches.

## Try it 2 - Stop retyping with templates and signatures

**Why it matters:** typing the same message again and again is a task to automate, and it is free and instant.

**Do this now:**
1. Find a reply you send often (a "we received this", a booking confirmation).
2. Save it as a **template / canned response** and insert it once to test.
3. Set a professional **signature** so you never retype your name and contact details.

**You've got it when:** you can drop in a saved reply and your signature in one click.

## Try it 3 - Let your calendar do the remembering

**Why it matters:** your calendar automates more than most people use, and it protects your focus.

**Do this now:**
1. Create a **recurring event** (a weekly study block or team check-in) so it repeats on its own.
2. Add a **reminder** before a deadline so you get nudged automatically.
3. If you take bookings, set up a **scheduling link** so people pick a slot without the back-and-forth.

**You've got it when:** one repeating event and one reminder are set and will fire without you.

## Try it 4 - Trim small frictions on your phone

**Why it matters:** tiny repeated frictions add up. Phone shortcuts remove them safely if you keep them simple.

**Do this now:**
1. Set a **text-expansion shortcut**: type a short code to insert your address or a long phrase.
2. Set a **Do Not Disturb schedule** for your focus hours so notifications go quiet automatically.
3. Keep it simple - the goal is to remove friction, not build something fragile.

**You've got it when:** a shortcut expands your text and your focus hours go quiet on their own.

### Watch out for

- Auto-deleting with a weak rule and losing something important
- Auto-forwarding mail outside the organisation (a privacy leak)
- Making so many labels and rules that the system gets confusing
- Never reviewing rules, so they catch the wrong things over time
- Building fragile phone automations that break and waste more time than they save

## This week's build: your "hour back" setup

Set up and document **three working built-in automations** that genuinely save you time:

1. At least **three automations** across email, calendar, and phone (for example two email rules + one template, plus a recurring reminder), each with a line on what it does
2. A short note on **what each one saves** you (time and hassle)
3. One **safety check** you applied (what you chose not to auto-delete or auto-forward, and why)
4. A two-day **review note**: did any rule catch the wrong thing, and what did you adjust?

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Add a filter that sends newsletters straight to a "Read later" label, out of your inbox.
- Save a second template for the message you dread typing most.
- Set one recurring reminder for a bill or renewal you always forget.

## Facilitator Notes

This week should produce immediate, motivating wins with zero new tools and almost no risk, build that
confidence. Do the email rules live; they are the clearest example of trigger-and-action. Stress the two
safety habits: prefer archive/label over delete, and never auto-forward externally without care. Keep
phone automations simple so nobody builds something fragile. Praise time saved, measured honestly.
