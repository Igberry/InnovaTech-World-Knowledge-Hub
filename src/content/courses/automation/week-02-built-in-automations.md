**Goal:** Get real time savings this week using automations already built into the tools you have, email
rules, templates, calendar features, and phone shortcuts, with no new tools and very little risk.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Create email rules (filters) that sort and handle mail automatically
- Save and reuse templates and signatures
- Use calendar automations: scheduling, reminders, and recurring events
- Use simple phone and app automations safely
- Judge which built-in automations are safe to trust

## Tools / Accounts Needed This Week

- Your email, calendar, and phone
- The [email rules cheat-sheet](/downloads/automation/email-rules-cheatsheet.md)

- Read: search "[your email] create a filter" or "create a rule" and read the official help page.

## Lesson 1: Email rules, the easiest safe win

Your email already automates with **rules** (Gmail calls them filters). A rule says: WHEN an email matches
something, DO an action, automatically. This is the safest automation to start with: it only touches your
own inbox, and nothing leaves it.

Useful recipes (see the [cheat-sheet](/downloads/automation/email-rules-cheatsheet.md)):

- WHEN from a receipts sender, DO label "Finance" and archive
- WHEN it contains "invoice", DO label "To pay"
- WHEN from your boss or top client, DO star / mark important
- WHEN it is a newsletter, DO skip the inbox and label "Read later"

The exact menu names differ by provider, so search "[your email] create a filter" for the clicks. The
thinking is the same everywhere: match, then act.

> **Warning:** Prefer "archive" or "label" over "delete" in rules. A weak rule that auto-deletes can lose
> something important. And avoid auto-forwarding mail outside your organisation; it can leak private
> information.

## Lesson 2: Templates and signatures

Typing the same thing again and again is a task to automate:

- **Templates / canned responses:** save replies you send often (a confirmation, a "we received this", a
  scheduling message) and insert them in one click.
- **Signatures:** set a professional signature once so you never retype your name and contact details.
- **Snippets:** some tools let you expand a short code into a longer block of text.

These are simple, safe, and save a surprising amount of time for anyone who answers similar messages daily.

## Lesson 3: Calendar automations

Your calendar automates more than you might use:

- **Recurring events:** set a weekly or monthly event once, and it repeats.
- **Reminders / notifications:** get nudged before a meeting or a deadline.
- **Scheduling / booking links:** let people book free slots without the back-and-forth (you met this in
  the Virtual Assistant course).
- **Buffers and working hours:** protect focus time and stop out-of-hours bookings.

Together these remove a lot of manual scheduling and remembering.

## Lesson 4: Phone and app shortcuts

Your phone has safe, handy automations too:

- **Text expansion / keyboard shortcuts:** type a short code to insert a long phrase or address.
- **Do Not Disturb schedules:** silence notifications automatically at set times.
- **Alarms and reminders** tied to a time or, on some phones, a place.
- **Built-in shortcut apps:** some phones let you chain simple actions (this is optional and more advanced).

Keep these simple. The goal is to remove small repeated frictions, not to build something fragile.

## Lesson 5: Which built-in automations to trust

Built-in automations are mostly low-risk because they stay within one tool you control. Still, apply light
judgement:

- Safe to trust quickly: labels, templates, signatures, reminders, recurring events.
- Use care with: anything that **deletes**, **forwards externally**, or **sends on your behalf** without
  you seeing it first.
- Review your rules occasionally; senders and needs change.

> **Tip:** Built-in automations alone can save a busy person an hour a day, with almost no risk. Master
> these before reaching for a separate automation tool in Week 4.

### Common mistakes

- Auto-deleting with a weak rule and losing something
- Auto-forwarding mail outside the organisation (privacy leak)
- Making so many labels/rules that the system gets confusing
- Never reviewing rules, so they catch the wrong things over time
- Building fragile phone automations that break and waste more time than they save

## Practice Exercises

1. Create three email rules from the cheat-sheet and confirm what each catches.
2. Save two reply templates you would reuse, and insert each once.
3. Set a professional email signature.
4. Create one recurring calendar event with a reminder.
5. Set a text-expansion shortcut on your phone or email.
6. Set a Do Not Disturb schedule for your focus hours.
7. Review your new rules after two days and note anything they caught wrongly.

## Facilitator Notes

This week should produce immediate, motivating wins with zero new tools and almost no risk, build that
confidence. Do the email rules live; they are the clearest example of trigger-and-action. Stress the two
safety habits: prefer archive/label over delete, and never auto-forward externally without care. Keep
phone automations simple so nobody builds something fragile. Praise time saved, measured honestly.
