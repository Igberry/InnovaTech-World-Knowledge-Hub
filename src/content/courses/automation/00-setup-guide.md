**Goal:** Set up the free accounts and the no-code automation tool you will use, and learn the safety
basics that keep an automation from causing harm before you connect anything real.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this setup you can:

- Create the free accounts an automation beginner needs
- Explain what a no-code automation tool does, in plain words
- List the safety basics: testing, rollback, privacy, and account security
- Start a task-audit log to find tasks worth automating

## 1. What you will use

Automation connects the tools you already use. You do not need to code. Here is what each piece is for.
Tools and free tiers change, so **check the current details on each tool's own website**.

| Tool | What it is for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Email + documents | Where many tasks live (Gmail/Outlook, Docs/Sheets) | You likely have this already |
| Spreadsheet app | Automating with formulas and validation (Week 3) | Google Sheets or Excel |
| A no-code automation tool | Connects apps: "when X, do Y" (Weeks 4 to 6) | Pick one with a free tier |

> **Note:** A **no-code automation tool** lets you link apps together with clicks instead of
> programming. You choose a trigger (when something happens) and an action (do something), and the tool
> runs it for you. You will choose one in Week 4; for now just create a free account to explore.

## 2. Create your accounts

- Make sure you have a working **email and spreadsheet** account (Google or Microsoft).
- Create a free account on **one no-code automation tool** (you will use it from Week 4). Do not sign up
  for several; one is enough to learn on.
- Turn on strong passwords and **two-step verification** everywhere, this matters more once tools are
  connected.

## 3. Safety basics (read before you connect anything)

Automation is powerful because it repeats work quickly, which also means it can repeat a **mistake**
quickly. Four rules keep you safe:

1. **Test first.** Never point a new automation at real customers, real money, or the only copy of
   important data. Test with sample data.
2. **Keep a rollback.** Always know how to switch an automation off and undo what it did. If you cannot,
   do not automate it yet.
3. **Protect privacy.** Do not route passwords, bank details, or private information through a tool that
   should not have them. Give connected accounts the least access they need.
4. **Respect the rules.** Do not automate spam, deception, or anything against a platform's terms.

> **Warning:** A bad rule can send hundreds of wrong messages before you notice. Start small, watch
> closely, and keep the off switch within reach. You will use the
> [test and rollback checklist](/downloads/automation/test-and-rollback-checklist.md) all course.

## 4. Account security (because automations hold the keys)

When you connect an app to an automation tool, you give it access to that account. So:

- Use **strong, unique passwords** (a password manager helps)
- Turn on **two-step verification**
- Review which tools have access to your accounts, and remove any you no longer use
- Never share your automation tool login

## 5. Start a task-audit log

The best way to find what to automate is to notice what you repeat. Open the
[task-audit log](/downloads/automation/task-audit-log.csv) and start recording repetitive tasks this
week, how often and how long. You will use it in Week 1.

## 6. Setup checklist

- [ ] Email and spreadsheet accounts working
- [ ] One no-code automation tool account created
- [ ] Two-step verification on key accounts
- [ ] I understand the four safety rules
- [ ] Task-audit log started

2. What are the four safety rules before connecting anything real?
3. Why does account security matter more once you start automating?
