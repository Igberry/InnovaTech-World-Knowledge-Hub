**Goal:** In one short session, get your accounts and one no-code tool ready, make your first tiny
automation with your own hands, and learn the four safety habits that keep automations from causing harm.
Almost no reading - you set things up by doing.

## What you'll be able to do

- Create the free accounts an automation beginner needs
- Say, in plain words, what a no-code automation tool does - after poking one yourself
- Use the four safety habits: test, rollback, privacy, account security
- Start a task-audit log that feeds straight into Week 1

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Have a working **email + spreadsheet** account ready (Google or Microsoft). You likely already do.
- Warm-up: on a piece of paper, jot three tasks you did twice this week. One of them is probably your
  first automation. Keep this note.

## Try it 1 - Get your accounts ready

**Why it matters:** automation connects the tools you already use, so you need the accounts first and
nothing more.

**Do this now:**
1. Confirm you can log into your **email and spreadsheet** (Gmail/Sheets or Outlook/Excel).
2. Pick **one** no-code automation tool with a free tier and create an account. Just one - not several.
3. Turn on **two-step verification** on your email and your new tool account.

**You've got it when:** you are logged into one no-code tool and your key accounts have two-step verification.

## Try it 2 - See what a no-code tool actually is

**Why it matters:** a no-code tool lets you link apps with clicks instead of code: you pick a trigger
("when X happens") and an action ("do Y"). Seeing the menu once removes the mystery.

**Do this now:**
1. Open your tool and start creating a new automation (look for "Create", "New Zap", "New flow", or similar).
2. Browse the list of **triggers** - notice options like "new email", "new form response", "new row".
3. Browse the list of **actions** - notice "send email", "add row", "create task".
4. Do not finish it yet. Just close it. You have seen the whole idea: pick a trigger, pick an action.

**You've got it when:** you can say in one line, "a no-code tool runs an action when a trigger happens, no code needed."

## Try it 3 - Learn the four safety habits

**Why it matters:** automation repeats work fast, which means it can repeat a mistake fast. A bad rule can
send hundreds of wrong messages before you notice.

**Do this now - read each habit and write one real example of your own:**
1. **Test first.** Never point a new automation at real customers, real money, or the only copy of data. Use sample data.
2. **Keep a rollback.** Always know how to switch it off and undo it. If you cannot, do not automate it yet.
3. **Protect privacy.** Do not route passwords, bank details, or private information through a tool that should not have them. Give each connection the least access it needs.
4. **Respect the rules.** No spam, no deception, nothing against a platform's terms.

**You've got it when:** you can recite the four habits and you have the
[test and rollback checklist](/downloads/automation/test-and-rollback-checklist.md) saved to use all course.

## Try it 4 - Start your task-audit log

**Why it matters:** the best way to find what to automate is to notice what you repeat. This log feeds your
Week 1 build directly.

**Do this now:**
1. Open the [task-audit log](/downloads/automation/task-audit-log.csv).
2. Add the three repeated tasks from your warm-up note.
3. For each, jot how often you do it and roughly how long it takes.

**You've got it when:** your log has at least three real tasks with a rough time and frequency.

### Watch out for

- Signing up for several automation tools at once - one is plenty to learn on
- Skipping two-step verification, then connecting accounts that hold real keys
- Auto-deleting or auto-forwarding before you have tested anything
- Treating a shiny demo as "safe" without a rollback

## Setup checklist

- [ ] Email and spreadsheet accounts working
- [ ] One no-code automation tool account created
- [ ] Two-step verification on key accounts
- [ ] I can name the four safety habits
- [ ] Task-audit log started with 3+ tasks
