management, clean data entry, and tidy file organisation, and write them up as SOPs you can follow and

## Lesson 1: Inbox management, the VA bread and butter

Most clients hire a VA because their inbox is out of control. If you can take an inbox from chaos to
calm and keep it there, you will always have work.

The key idea: an inbox is a to-do list other people write for you. Your job is to turn that mess into
clear actions, fast. Use the **four actions** (the 4 Ds) for every email:

| Action | When | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Do** | Under 2 minutes | Handle it now, then file it |
| **Delegate / escalate** | Needs the client or someone else | Forward with a one-line summary and a clear ask |
| **Defer** | Yours, but over 2 minutes | Flag it, add to the task list, schedule time |
| **Delete / archive** | No action needed | File it away so the inbox stays clean |

Work top to bottom, decide once per email, and never leave an email "half-read." A clean inbox at the
end of each pass is the goal, not reading everything twice.

**Labels and folders.** A few clear labels beat many messy ones. A good starter set: *Action needed*,
*Waiting on reply*, *Receipts/finance*, *Reference*, *Done/archived*.

**Templates.** Save replies you send often (confirmations, "we received this", scheduling). You will
answer the same questions again and again; a saved reply turns five minutes into ten seconds.

> **Important:** Agree with every client what you may reply to on your own and what you must check first.
> Routine confirmations: fine. Anything about money, contracts, or their most important people: always
> check. Write this down in the SOP.

## Lesson 2: Calendar management

A calendar a client can trust is worth a lot. The two cardinal sins are **double-booking** and
**wrong time zones**. Avoid both and you are ahead of most.

**Time zones.** When you book across countries, always confirm the time zone of everyone invited. Say
times clearly: "3pm your time (Lagos)" is safer than just "3pm." Most calendar tools show attendees'
time zones; use that.

**A booking checklist** (put this in your SOP):

- Confirm everyone's time zone
- Clear title: who + what ("InnovaTech x Client - intro call")
- Add the video link or location
- Add an agenda or purpose in the notes
- Add a reminder
- Check it does not clash or break a focus block

**Buffers and focus time.** Good VAs protect the client's energy, not just their slots. Add short
buffers between meetings, and protect blocks of focus time where no meetings are booked.

**Scheduling tools.** A booking link (where people pick from your client's free slots) ends the
back-and-forth of "are you free Tuesday?" Learn one such tool; it saves hours.

> **Tip:** When you reschedule, always offer two or three specific new times rather than asking "when are
> you free?" It is faster for everyone and makes you look organised.

## Lesson 3: Data entry and clean-up

VAs often enter or tidy data: contact lists, orders, sign-ups, expenses. Accuracy matters more than
speed; one wrong phone number can cost a client a sale.

Good habits:

- **Be consistent.** Pick one format for dates (e.g., 2026-03-14), phone numbers, and names, and use it
  everywhere.
- **One thing per cell.** First name and last name in separate columns; do not cram.
- **Check as you go.** Spot-check every tenth row. Read numbers back once.
- **Never overwrite the only copy.** Work on a copy, or keep the original safe, until the job is checked.

A few spreadsheet basics that make you faster: freezing the top row, sorting, filtering, and using
find-and-replace to fix a repeated error in one go. You do not need formulas yet; clean, accurate,
consistent data is the goal.

> **Warning:** Client data often includes people's personal details. Treat it as private. Do not copy it
> to your personal accounts, and delete local copies when the job is done. More on this in Week 7.

## Lesson 4: File organisation

A messy drive wastes everyone's time. A good system means anyone can find a file in seconds.

A simple, reliable structure:

- One **main folder per client**
- Inside it, a few clear sub-folders: *Admin*, *Finance*, *Content*, *Reference*
- A consistent **naming pattern**: `YYYY-MM-DD - short description` (e.g., `2026-03-14 - March invoice`)

Dates at the front sort files in order automatically. Clear names mean you never open five files to find
the right one.

> **Rule of thumb:** If a new helper joined tomorrow, could they find any file without asking you? If
> not, your system needs clearer names or folders.

## Lesson 5: Writing an SOP

An **SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)** is a written, step-by-step routine for a task, so it is done
the same good way every time, even on a bad day. SOPs are a VA superpower: they make your work
consistent, they help you train a backup, and they show clients you are a professional.

A good SOP has:

1. The task name and when it is done
2. The steps, in order, numbered
3. Any rules or "always check first" notes
4. What "done" looks like

This week you will fill in the [Inbox and Calendar SOP template](/downloads/virtual-assistant/inbox-calendar-sop.md)
for a made-up client. Keep it short enough that you would actually follow it.

### Common mistakes

- **Reading emails twice.** Decide once, act, move on.
- **Too many labels.** A few clear ones beat a complicated system.
- **Guessing time zones.** Always confirm; never assume.
- **Inconsistent data formats.** Pick one format and stick to it.
- **Vague file names** like "document1" or "final final v2."

## Practice Exercises

1. Set up a practice email with the five starter labels. Sort 15 sample emails using the 4 Ds.
2. Write two reply templates you would reuse (a confirmation and a "we received this").
3. Book three fictional meetings across two time zones with no clashes. Use clear titles.
4. Take a messy sample contact list (make one with 15 rows of fictional data) and clean it: consistent
   formats, one thing per cell, no duplicates.
5. Build a folder structure for one fictional client and add five correctly named files.
6. Rename five badly named files to the `YYYY-MM-DD - description` pattern.
7. Write a 6-step SOP for "processing a new sign-up" for a fictional business.

## Facilitator Notes

This is the most important week for actual VA skill, so keep it hands-on. Run the inbox drill live with
a prepared messy sample (20 to 30 fictional emails); learners learn triage by doing, not reading. Watch
for learners who read every email in full, that is the habit to break. For data, provide a deliberately
messy sample list. Praise consistency and accuracy over speed. Keep all sample data fictional.
