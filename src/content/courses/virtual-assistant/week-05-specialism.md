**Goal:** Understand the main VA specialisms, weigh them honestly against your strengths and the market,
and choose a niche with a simple plan to build the skills it needs.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Describe the main VA specialisms and who each one serves
- Explain why specialising usually earns more than staying general
- Match your strengths and interests to a realistic niche
- Choose a specialism and write a plan to build its skills
- Update your positioning and service list to match

## Tools / Accounts Needed This Week

- A document for your niche decision and skills plan
- Your [service menu worksheet](/downloads/virtual-assistant/service-menu-pricing.md) from Week 1

## Pre-Session Resources (about 2 hours)

- Read: search the web for "virtual assistant niches" and read two current articles. List the niches
  that appear in both.
- Research: search job boards for "virtual assistant" plus a niche word (e.g., "social media", "e-commerce",
  "customer support"). Note which niches have the most posts.
- Reflect: of the tasks you have done so far in this course, which did you enjoy most?

## Lesson 1: Why specialise

In Week 1 you learned the difference between general and specialist VAs. Now you choose a direction.

Specialising usually pays more and competes with fewer people, for three reasons:

1. **You get faster and better** at a narrow set of tasks, so your hour is worth more.
2. **Clients trust a specialist** more than a generalist for their specific problem.
3. **You are easier to refer.** "She's great with e-commerce stores" spreads; "she does everything" does not.

You do not specialise forever, and you can still do general tasks for a client. Specialising just means
you have a clear strength that leads your pitch.

> **Note:** You do not need to have a niche mastered to choose one. You choose a direction, then build
> the skills. Picking the direction is this week's job.

## Lesson 2: The main VA specialisms

Here are common specialisms, what they involve, and the kind of client who needs them. None requires a
degree; all reward reliability and care.

| Specialism | What you do | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **Executive assistant (EA)** | Run a senior person's inbox, calendar, travel, and priorities | Founders, executives, busy professionals |
| **Social media VA** | Schedule posts, reply to comments, basic graphics, simple reporting | Coaches, creators, small brands |
| **E-commerce VA** | Process orders, update listings, answer buyer questions | Online stores, sellers |
| **Customer support VA** | Answer tickets, manage a help desk, write FAQs | SaaS, online services, shops |
| **Bookkeeping-lite VA** | Record invoices and receipts, chase payments, expense tracking | Small businesses, freelancers |
| **Real estate / industry VA** | Industry-specific admin and coordination | Agents, agencies, niche firms |

Each of these connects to other InnovaTech courses. For example, a social media VA benefits from the
[Social Media Marketing](/courses/social-media-marketing/) course; an e-commerce or support VA benefits
from strong communication (Week 3 here). Cross-skilling makes you more valuable.

## Lesson 3: Match the niche to you and the market

A good niche sits where three things overlap:

1. **What you enjoy** (you will do it a lot)
2. **What you are or can become good at** (clients pay for competence)
3. **What the market wants** (there must be clients who need it)

Be honest on all three. A niche you love but no one hires for is a hobby. A niche with lots of demand you
would dread is a fast road to burnout. Look for the overlap.

**How to check demand:** search job boards and freelance sites for the niche. Many posts means real
demand. Few posts means either low demand or a very specific market; dig deeper before committing.

> **Tip:** When unsure between two niches, pick the one with clearer demand and that you would not mind
> doing on a dull Tuesday. Enthusiasm fades; steady interest and real demand carry you.

## Lesson 4: Make a skills plan

Once you pick a niche, write a short plan to build its skills. Keep it concrete:

- **Skills to learn:** the three or four specific skills the niche needs (e.g., for a social media VA:
  scheduling tools, writing captions, basic graphics, simple analytics)
- **How you will learn them:** a course (including InnovaTech ones), free tutorials, and practice projects
- **A practice project:** something you can build without a client, for your portfolio (Week 8)
- **A timeline:** rough weeks for each skill

The plan does not need to be perfect. It needs to turn "I chose social media VA" into "here is exactly
what I will learn and practise next."

### Common mistakes

- **Choosing a niche with no demand** because it sounds nice.
- **Choosing purely for money**, ignoring whether you would tolerate the work.
- **Refusing to choose**, staying a generalist out of fear, and competing with everyone.
- **Thinking you must master the niche first.** You choose, then build; clients grow with you.

## Practice Exercises

1. Write one sentence on what each of the six specialisms involves, in your own words.
2. Score each specialism from 1 to 5 on "enjoy", "good at / can become", and "market demand."
3. Search a job board for three niches and record how many posts each returns.
4. Pick your top two niches and list the pros and cons of each for you.
5. Choose one niche and write why it fits the three-overlap test.
6. List the three or four skills your chosen niche needs.
7. Rewrite your positioning line (from Week 1) to reflect your chosen niche.

## Weekly Assignment (Due Before Week 6)

Write a **niche decision and skills plan**:

1. A short **comparison** of your top two niches (the three-overlap scores and a note on demand you found)
2. Your **chosen niche** and two or three sentences on why it fits you and the market
3. A **skills plan**: the skills to learn, how you will learn them, a practice project idea, and a rough timeline
4. An **updated positioning line** and service list that match the niche

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Honest comparison using the three-overlap test | 25% |
| Clear choice with sound reasoning (fit + demand) | 25% |
| Concrete, realistic skills plan with a practice project | 30% |
| Updated positioning and services match the niche | 20% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] Two niches compared with real demand research
- [ ] A clear choice, justified against fit and demand
- [ ] A skills plan with specific skills, sources, a project, and a timeline
- [ ] Positioning line and services updated to the niche

## Facilitator Notes

Some learners will resist choosing, out of fear of "closing doors." Reassure them: a niche is a lead, not
a cage; they can still take general work and change later. Push back gently on niches chosen for money
alone or with no demand evidence. The strongest outcome is a learner who can say "I'm a [niche] VA, here
is what I'm learning next." Connect choices to other InnovaTech courses where relevant. Keep it realistic
about the local market.
