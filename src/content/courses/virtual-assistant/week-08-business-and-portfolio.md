## Lesson 1: Why a portfolio matters (even with no clients yet)

A client cannot see your past work the way they can see a designer's. So you must **show** that you are
organised, reliable, and safe. A portfolio does that. The good news: you have spent seven weeks creating
exactly the right samples.

A VA portfolio answers one question in the client's mind: "Can I trust this person with my business?"
Everything in it should build that trust.

> **Note:** You do not need a real client to have a portfolio. Your course assignments, done with
> fictional data, are legitimate work samples. Many successful VAs start exactly this way.

## Lesson 2: Build the portfolio

Use the [portfolio outline](/downloads/virtual-assistant/va-portfolio-outline.md). The sections:

1. **Header**: your name, "Virtual Assistant", and your positioning line
2. **Services**: your niche-led service menu (Week 6)
3. **Sample work**: three to four anonymised samples, each with the task, what you did, and the result
4. **Tools I use**: the tools you are comfortable with
5. **About me**: a few honest lines on who you are and what it is like to work with you
6. **A clear call to action**: exactly how to contact or book you

**Best samples to include** (you already built these):

- Your filled-in **SOP** (Week 2)
- A **weekly report** (Week 3)
- A **task board** screenshot (Week 4)
- A **proposal** (Week 6)

> **Privacy:** Every sample must use fictional or fully anonymised data. Never show a real client's
> emails, contacts, numbers, or documents. Remove or blur every identifying detail. When in doubt, leave
> it out or ask permission in writing.

## Lesson 3: Testimonials and references

Social proof builds trust fast. Even early, you can gather it honestly:

- After good work (even a small practice project or a favour for someone's business), ask for a short
  testimonial. Make it easy: offer to draft a sentence they can edit.
- Keep **references** (people who will vouch for you) with their permission.
- Never invent testimonials or use someone's words without consent. Fake proof destroys trust when found,
  and it is dishonest.

> **Warning:** Do not fabricate reviews, clients, or results. One made-up testimonial, discovered,
> undoes everything. Real, modest proof beats impressive fake proof every time.

## Lesson 4: Treat it like a business

Even as a solo VA, a few light business habits keep you steady and professional:

- **Track time and tasks** per client (Week 0 habit) so invoices are accurate.
- **Invoice simply and on schedule**: a clear invoice with your name, the client, the period, hours or
  package, the amount, and how to pay. Send it on the agreed day.
- **Keep records**: income, simple expenses, and who paid what. This helps you see what is working and
  handle any tax obligations (check your local rules).
- **Set boundaries**: working hours, response times, and days off. Burnout helps no one; sustainable
  beats frantic.
- **Keep learning**: add skills (including other InnovaTech courses) so you can raise your rate over time.

> **Tip:** Treating your VA work as a business from day one, even with one client, makes raising rates,
> adding clients, and staying sane far easier later.

## Lesson 5: Your first 90 days

A realistic plan beats a vague hope. A simple shape:

- **Days 1 to 30:** finish your portfolio; tell your network you offer VA services; apply or reach out
  consistently (a set number each week); keep practising skills.
- **Days 31 to 60:** refine your pitch based on responses; aim for discovery calls; send proposals; land
  a first client (paid or a small starter project).
- **Days 61 to 90:** deliver reliably; gather your first real testimonial; review your rate; add a second
  channel for finding work.

Honest reminder: timelines vary a lot. Some land a client in week two; others take months. Consistency
is the one thing you control. Keep showing up.

### Common mistakes

- **Waiting for a "real" client** before building a portfolio (your assignments are enough to start).
- **Showing real client data** in samples (a serious privacy and trust failure).
- **Faking testimonials or results.**
- **No business habits**: lost hours, late invoices, no records.
- **Giving up after a few weeks.** Client-getting is a numbers-and-consistency game.

## Capstone Assignment (Due Week 8 session)

Produce your **complete VA capstone package**:

1. A **portfolio** (document or simple site) with all six sections and **three to four anonymised samples**
   drawn from your course work
2. Your **service menu and starting price** (from Week 6), ready to share
3. A **proposal** and an **onboarding checklist**, polished (Weeks 6 and 7)
4. A **first-90-days plan** with specific weekly actions
5. A short **testimonial-request message** you could send after a job

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Portfolio is complete, professional, and privacy-safe | 35% |
| Service menu and price are clear and defensible | 15% |
| Proposal and onboarding are polished and usable | 20% |
| First-90-days plan is specific and realistic | 20% |
| Testimonial request is honest and well written | 10% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] Portfolio has all six sections and 3 to 4 anonymised samples
- [ ] No real client data anywhere in the portfolio
- [ ] Service menu, price, proposal, and onboarding included and polished
- [ ] 90-day plan has specific weekly actions
- [ ] Testimonial-request message included

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 minutes)

| Time | Activity |

| 0-15 min | Capstone overview; each learner states their niche and offer |
| 15-45 min | Portfolio presentations: each learner shows their portfolio; group feedback |
| 45-65 min | Mock client call: a learner pitches; facilitator plays the client |
| 65-80 min | Privacy and honesty check: scan portfolios for any real data or fake proof |
| 80-90 min | First-90-days commitments; course close and next steps |

## Facilitator Notes

This is the capstone, so make it a real presentation and celebration of eight weeks of work. Run live
portfolio reviews and a mock client call for each learner if time allows; the practice is invaluable.
Check every portfolio for two things: no real client data, and no fabricated testimonials or results,
both are deal-breakers. Point learners to Remote Ready for free portfolio hosting and the wider job
search. End with each learner committing to a concrete first-90-days action. Keep outcomes honest: real
skills and consistent action, not promised income.
