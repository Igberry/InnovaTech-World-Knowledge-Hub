**Goal:** Turn a "yes" into a smooth working relationship: onboard cleanly, handle access and data
safely, deliver reliably, get paid without being scammed, and keep clients coming back.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Onboard a new client cleanly, with scope and terms agreed in writing
- Handle account access and client data safely
- Deliver work reliably with SOPs, reports, and time tracking
- Choose safe ways to get paid and spot payment scams
- Keep clients by being consistent and easy to work with

- A password manager
- A document for your agreement and onboarding notes

## Lesson 1: Onboard cleanly

The first week sets the tone. A calm, organised start tells the client they chose well. Use the
[onboarding checklist](/downloads/virtual-assistant/client-onboarding-checklist.md).

**Agree the basics in writing first** (before any work):

- Scope: exactly what you will and will not do
- Hours or package, rate, and payment schedule
- Working hours, time zone, and response time
- How you will communicate and how often you report
- Who you report to and who can give you tasks

Even a short, simple written agreement protects both sides. It does not need to be a long legal
contract; it needs to be clear and agreed. "In writing" can be a shared document both of you confirm.

> **Important:** Never start real work before scope and payment are agreed in writing. "We'll sort it
> out later" is where disputes and unpaid work come from. A clear start prevents most problems.

## Lesson 2: Handle access and data safely

Clients trust you with access to their business. Protecting it is part of the job, and doing it well
makes you stand out.

- **Access the safe way.** Ask the client to grant access through the tool's own "share" or "invite"
  feature, not by emailing you a password. For shared passwords, use a password manager's secure share.
- **Use a password manager and 2FA** on every account (set up in Week 0).
- **Least access needed.** Ask only for the access your tasks require, nothing more.
- **Keep client data private.** Do not copy it to personal accounts; delete local copies when a job is
  done; never share one client's information with another.

> **Privacy:** If a client insists on emailing you a password, ask them to change it immediately after
> the task. Treat every client's data as if it were your own bank details. Your reputation for safety is
> one of your biggest assets.

## Lesson 3: Deliver reliably

Delivery is where you keep the client. The habits from earlier weeks come together:

- **Run your SOPs** (Week 2) so tasks are done the same good way every time.
- **Use your task board** (Week 4) so nothing is dropped and the client can see progress.
- **Track your time** (Week 0) so your hours and invoices are accurate and honest.
- **Send the weekly report** (Week 3) so your value is visible.
- **Communicate early** when something will be late or is bigger than expected.

Reliability beats brilliance. A VA who does solid work, on time, every week, with no surprises, is worth
more than a flashier one the client cannot depend on.

> **Rule of thumb:** Under-promise and over-deliver. Promise what you can definitely do; then a little
> extra feels like a gift. The reverse, promising big and missing, loses clients fast.

## Lesson 4: Getting paid safely

Getting paid matters as much as doing the work. Two parts: choosing how to get paid, and avoiding scams.

**How to get paid.** Freelancers working with international clients use various methods: freelance
platforms that handle payment, international transfer services, and others. Options, fees, and
availability change and vary by country, so **research current options for your situation and verify them
before you rely on any one.** Agree the method and schedule with the client up front (part of onboarding).

**Payment scams to avoid** (new VAs are targeted):

| Scam | How it works | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| Overpayment | "Client" sends too much, asks you to refund the difference; their payment later bounces | Never refund or forward money for a client |
| Pay-to-work | You must "pay a fee" or "buy equipment" to get the job | Real clients pay you, not the other way round |
| Money handling | You receive funds and forward them "for the business" | Never move money for a client; this can be illegal |
| Fake cheque / gift card | Payment in gift cards, or a cheque you must cash and share | Legitimate clients do not pay in gift cards |

> **Warning:** If getting paid involves you sending, refunding, or forwarding money, stop. That is the
> clearest sign of a scam. A real client simply pays you for your work. Revisit [Remote Ready](/courses/remote-ready/)
> Week 4 for the full scam guide.

## Lesson 5: Keep clients (retention)

It is far easier to keep a client than to find a new one. You keep clients by being consistent, easy,
and proactive:

- Be **predictable**: same reports, reliable hours, no surprises.
- Be **proactive**: suggest small improvements ("I noticed X; shall I set up Y?").
- **Ask for feedback** regularly and act on it.
- **Grow with them**: as trust builds, take on more and raise your rate when you have earned it.

A happy client is also your best source of new clients, through referrals and testimonials (which feed
your portfolio in Week 8).

### Common mistakes

- **Starting work before terms are in writing.**
- **Taking passwords by email** instead of proper sharing.
- **Over-promising** and then missing.
- **Ignoring payment-scam warning signs** because you want the work.
- **Going quiet** between tasks instead of sending steady updates.

## Practice Exercises

1. Fill in the onboarding checklist for a fictional new client, including a short written scope.
2. Write a simple one-page working agreement (scope, hours, rate, schedule, communication).
3. Write the message you would send to request access the safe way (no passwords by email).
4. Research two current payment methods for freelancers in your country; note fees and the date checked.
5. For each of the four payment scams, write the one sentence you would use to decline safely.
6. Write a proactive suggestion message you might send a client in month two.
7. Draft a short, friendly request for feedback and a testimonial.

## Weekly Assignment (Due Before Week 8)

Build an **onboarding and delivery pack** for a fictional client:

1. A completed **onboarding checklist** plus a one-page **written agreement** (scope, hours, rate, schedule)
2. A **safe-access plan**: how you will get and protect access and data (your own words)
3. A short **payment plan**: the method(s) you would use, verified and dated, and how you will avoid scams
4. A **retention note**: two proactive suggestions and a feedback request you would send

## Facilitator Notes

Safety is the theme: data safety and payment safety. Spend real time on the scam table; new VAs lose
money and reputation here, and the overpayment and money-handling scams are common. Insist on "in
writing before work starts." For payments, do not endorse a specific provider; have learners verify
current options themselves and date their findings, since this changes and varies by person. Reinforce
the links to Remote Ready Week 4. Keep all examples fictional.
