**Goal:** Learn to communicate so clients always feel informed and in control, through clear writing,
good async habits, reliable reports, honest expectation-setting, and calm handling of mistakes.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Write clear, professional messages that get a fast, useful reply
- Communicate well asynchronously (async) across time zones
- Send a weekly report that makes your value visible
- Set and manage expectations so clients are never surprised
- Handle a mistake or a missed deadline professionally

## Lesson 1: Clear writing is the whole job

As a remote VA, your client often judges you entirely by your messages. You may never meet in person.
Clear, calm, professional writing is not a nice extra; it is the product.

Three rules carry most of the weight:

1. **Lead with the point.** Put the main message or the ask in the first line. Busy people skim.
2. **One message, one purpose.** Do not bury three questions in a long paragraph. Use short lines or a
   small list.
3. **Make the ask obvious.** If you need something, say exactly what and by when.

Compare:

> **Weak:** "Hi, I was going through things and there were a few items and I wasn't totally sure about
> some of them, let me know your thoughts when you can, thanks."

> **Strong:** "Hi Ada, two quick decisions needed by Thursday: (1) Approve the reply to Supplier X
> (draft below). (2) Pick a time for the team call: Tue 2pm or Wed 10am (your time). Thank you."

The strong version respects the client's time and gets a fast, clear answer. That is what keeps clients.

> **Tip:** Before sending, re-read and ask: "Is my main point in the first line? Is my ask clear? Could
> this be shorter?" Those three checks lift your writing immediately.

## Lesson 2: Async communication across time zones

**Asynchronous (async)** communication means you and the client are not online at the same time. You
send a clear message; they reply hours later when they are awake. For remote work across countries, this
is normal, and doing it well is a real skill.

The golden rule of async: **send complete messages.** A good async message includes everything the
reader needs to act without coming back to you. So instead of "quick question about the invoice", send:
"Question about the March invoice: should I include the delivery fee of N4,500, or is that billed
separately? If separately, I will leave it off."

That one message, with the options spelled out, saves a whole day of back-and-forth. When the client
wakes up, they reply once and you can proceed.

Other async habits:

- **Over-communicate status**, not noise. A short daily note ("done X, waiting on you for Y") keeps
  trust high without meetings.
- **Confirm you received things.** A quick "Got it, will have this done by Thursday" stops the client
  wondering.
- **Respect agreed response times.** If you said "within one business day", meet it, even if the reply
  is "I'm on it, full answer tomorrow."

## Lesson 3: The weekly report

A weekly report is the single easiest way to look professional and prove your value. Clients forget what
you did; a report reminds them, in your words, every week.

Use the [weekly report template](/downloads/virtual-assistant/weekly-client-report.md). It has five parts:

- **Done this week** (lead with results, use numbers)
- **In progress**
- **Waiting on you** (be specific, with deadlines)
- **Notes and suggestions**
- **Hours used**

The most important trick: **report results, not activity.** "Cleared the backlog; inbox at zero" is
worth far more than "spent time on emails." Numbers make invisible admin work visible.

> **Important:** Send the report the same day and time every week (e.g., Friday afternoon). Predictable
> reporting builds trust. A client who always knows where things stand rarely micromanages.

## Lesson 4: Setting and managing expectations

Most client unhappiness comes from surprise, not from the work itself. Set expectations early and keep
them current, and you prevent most problems.

Set these at the start (and write them in the onboarding, Week 7):

- Your working hours and time zone
- Your response time
- What you will and will not do
- How often you report

Then **manage** them as you go:

- If something will be late, say so **early**, with a new time. Early warning is professional; silence is
  not.
- If a task is bigger than expected, flag it before you spend hours on it.
- If you are blocked waiting on the client, say what you need and what is paused until you get it.

> **Rule of thumb:** Never let a client be surprised by bad news. A problem shared early is a shared
> problem; a problem discovered late is your fault in their eyes.

## Lesson 5: Handling mistakes well

You will make mistakes. Everyone does. What separates a professional is how you handle them. A calm,
honest response to a mistake can actually increase a client's trust.

The simple recovery steps:

1. **Own it quickly.** "I made a mistake on X." No excuses, no hiding.
2. **Say the impact.** "This means Y."
3. **Fix it or propose a fix.** "I have already done Z / here is how I will fix it."
4. **Prevent it.** "To stop this happening again, I will add a check to the SOP."

What not to do: go quiet, blame a tool, or over-apologise for a page. One clear, calm message that owns
it and fixes it is what a client wants.

### Common mistakes

- **Burying the ask** in a long paragraph.
- **Vague messages** that force the client to ask follow-ups.
- **Going silent** when something goes wrong or is late.
- **Reporting activity, not results.**
- **Over-apologising** instead of fixing.

## Practice Exercises

1. Rewrite three weak messages (make them up) into strong ones: point first, clear ask, shorter.
2. Write one complete async message that gives the client options so they can reply once and you proceed.
3. Fill in a full weekly report for the fictional client "Ada" using the template (invent the week's work).
4. Write a short "setting expectations" message a VA would send in week one of a new client.
5. Write a "this will be late" message that warns early and offers a new time.
6. Write a mistake-recovery message using the four steps.
7. Take one of your own past messages (or invent one) and cut it by half without losing meaning.

## Weekly Assignment (Due Before Week 4)

Build a **communication pack** for the fictional client Ada:

1. A filled-in **weekly report** (results-led, with numbers and a clear "waiting on you")
2. Three **rewritten messages** (weak to strong), with a one-line note on what you improved
3. One **async message** that offers options for a one-reply decision
4. One **mistake-recovery message** using the four steps

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly report is clear, results-led, specific | 30% |
| Rewrites are genuinely clearer (point first, clear ask) | 25% |
| Async message is complete and decision-ready | 20% |
| Mistake-recovery message owns it, fixes it, prevents it | 25% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] Report uses all five sections and leads with results
- [ ] Each rewrite is shorter and clearer, with the ask obvious
- [ ] Async message lets the client reply once and move on
- [ ] Recovery message follows the four steps, calmly

## Facilitator Notes

Communication is where VAs are made or lost, so make this week interactive. Run live rewrites; learners
improve fast when they see weak-to-strong side by side. Role-play the hard messages (late work, a
mistake) in pairs; practising calm honesty now means they can do it under real pressure. Watch for
over-apologising and for "activity not results" in reports. Keep all examples fictional.
