**Goal:** Bring it all together - build a client-style **capstone**, polish a **portfolio** that proves
your skills, and prepare honestly for **freelance and remote work**.

You've built four projects. This week you build a fifth for a real or realistic client, package your
work professionally, and set up the habits that turn skills into opportunities.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Scope and build a client-style website as a capstone
- Present a polished portfolio: personal site, GitHub profile, pinned projects
- Handle the basics of client work: discovery, scoping, quoting, deposits, handover
- Find work safely and avoid common scams
- Prepare for interviews and a live coding warm-up

## Lesson 1: The capstone

Build one substantial site for a client. Two options:

- **Option A - a real client** (a local business, school, church, clinic, NGO) **with written
  permission** to build and showcase their site. Only proceed with permission in writing.
- **Option B - a realistic case study:** invent a plausible client with a written brief (who they are,
  what they need, their content). Make it believable and complete.

Requirements: multiple pages, responsive, accessible, deployed, with real content, and - where it fits -
a database feature (a contact or booking form saved with Supabase). Write it up with a README and a
short case study (the problem, your solution, screenshots).

## Lesson 2: Client skills

Even one paying client needs process. The essentials:

- **Discovery:** ask questions before quoting - Who is this for? What must it do? What content do you
  have? When do you need it? What's the budget?
- **Scope:** write down exactly what's included (and what's not) so expectations are clear.
- **Pricing:** simple models - a fixed price per project (best for beginners) or an hourly rate. Research
  local and remote rates; price for your level honestly.
- **Deposit:** take a **50% deposit before you start**, the rest on delivery. This protects you and is
  standard.
- **Feedback & changes:** agree a number of revision rounds up front; extra changes are extra work.
- **Handover & maintenance:** hand over the code/accounts, show them how to update content, and offer a
  simple maintenance arrangement if they want one.

> **Important:** Put the scope, price, deposit and timeline in **writing** (even a simple message or
> document) before starting. "In writing" prevents most disputes.

## Lesson 3: Your portfolio

Recruiters and clients judge you on what they can see. Build:

- **A personal portfolio site** (built by you, deployed): a short intro, your best 3–4 projects with
  screenshots and live links, and how to contact you.
- **A polished GitHub profile:** a profile README, **pinned repositories** (your 4 projects + capstone),
  each with a clear README, screenshots and a live link.
- **A professional headline:** on LinkedIn and anywhere else - e.g. "Front-end developer - HTML, CSS,
  JavaScript, React" - with a short, honest "About".

Quality over quantity: four finished, documented, deployed projects beat ten half-built ones.

## Lesson 4: Finding work - safely

Where people find work: **Upwork**, **Fiverr**, **LinkedIn**, **local referrals** (often the best when
starting), and **remote job boards**. Start small, deliver well, collect testimonials and referrals.

> **Warning - avoid scams.** Protect yourself:
> - **Never pay to get a job.** Real employers and clients don't charge you to work.
> - **Beware overpayment/cheque scams:** a "client" overpays and asks you to refund the difference - the
>   original payment is fake. Never refund from your own money.
> - **"Trial projects" that are real work:** a free "test" that is actually a finished deliverable for
>   their business. A tiny, clearly-scoped sample is fine; building their real site for free is not.
> - **Verify** who you're dealing with, use the platform's payment protection, and get a deposit for
>   direct clients.

## Lesson 5: Interview preparation

- **Common questions:** "Tell me about a project you built", "What was hard and how did you solve it?",
  "Explain how you'd centre a div / fetch data / manage state."
- **Explain a project:** the problem, your approach, the tech, a challenge, what you'd improve. Practise
  out loud.
- **Live coding warm-up:** be ready for small logic tasks (FizzBuzz-style) and a DOM/React task. Think
  aloud - interviewers want to see your reasoning, not just the answer.
- **Talking about what you don't know:** say "I haven't used that yet, but here's how I'd find out" -
  honesty plus a learning plan beats bluffing.

## Lesson 6: What next (optional)

After this course, sensible next steps (all optional further learning):

- **TypeScript** - typed JavaScript, widely used in jobs.
- **Node.js / Express** - build your own backend.
- **Testing** - Vitest / React Testing Library.
- **Next.js** - a popular React framework.
- **Deeper accessibility and performance.**

Keep building, keep shipping, keep applying. Consistency is what turns "learning to code" into "working
as a developer".

## Facilitator Notes

Hold the honest-outcomes line: celebrate the real, deployed portfolio and the habit of shipping, and be
clear that job-readiness continues beyond the course. For the capstone, insist on a written brief even
for case studies - scoping is a core professional skill. Make the scam-awareness section non-negotiable;
new developers in Nigeria are actively targeted. Keep any model capstone or solution code in
`_facilitator/`. End on momentum: every student should leave with a concrete "first three places I'll
apply" list.
