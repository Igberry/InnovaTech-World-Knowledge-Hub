**Goal:** Produce a clear 30-second branded advert with legible titles, accurate captions, balanced colour and a clean ending. You design and test on a real phone screen, not on paper.

## What you'll be able to do

- Build a readable title with clear hierarchy and safe margins
- Apply a brand kit so a series looks like it belongs together
- Generate captions, proofread them, and check timing and contrast
- Make restrained colour corrections that match shots
- Review a video for flashing, contrast and accessibility before export

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your Week 3 project, the [brand kit](/downloads/video-editing/fresh-bites-brand-kit.md), the [logo](/downloads/video-editing/fresh-bites-logo.svg) and the [caption style guide](/downloads/video-editing/caption-style-guide.md).
- Warm-up: watch your Week 3 export on a phone and name one text or colour change that would make it clearer. That is your first fix this week.

## Try it 1 - Build a title that reads at a glance

**Why it matters:** hierarchy is what the eye notices first. If everything is bold and huge, nothing stands out.

**Do this now:**
1. Add a short main title, then a smaller supporting line only if it adds information.
2. Pull all text away from the frame edges where device controls sit.
3. Check contrast: white text over a bright plate disappears, dark text over dark fails. Add a solid or semi-opaque backing or move the title.
4. Preview at phone size and read it in one second.

**You've got it when:** the main line is read first and every word is legible on a phone.

## Try it 2 - Apply the brand kit for consistency

**Why it matters:** a small kit (two or three colours, one type choice, a logo rule) makes separate clips feel like one deliberate series.

**Do this now:**
1. Use the same title style, alignment and accent across your titles.
2. Place the fictional Fresh Bites logo so it does not cover the product or the captions.
3. Add a lower third with accurate, fictional, approved text such as "Sample menu". Never invent a real person's title, address or endorsement.
4. Keep a clean version without the logo in case a client wants a clean master.

**You've got it when:** your titles share one look and the logo never blocks key content.

## Try it 3 - Caption it and proofread every line

**Why it matters:** auto-captions mishear names, accents, Nigerian English and pidgin. Published raw, they embarrass the video.

**Do this now:**
1. Generate captions if your app supports it, or type them manually, or add a transcript.
2. Play the video and read each line while listening. Fix spelling, punctuation, timing and who is speaking.
3. Keep each caption short, in sentence case, broken at a natural pause, and off faces and product details.
4. Check reading time and contrast on a phone.

**You've got it when:** every caption matches the audio and is comfortable to read on a phone.

## Try it 4 - Correct colour so shots match

**Why it matters:** mismatched colour makes a cut look accidental. A little correction makes a series look intentional.

**Do this now:**
1. Put two clips side by side. Adjust exposure so important detail is visible.
2. Balance white so a neutral object looks neutral, then make a modest contrast change, then check saturation.
3. If a white shirt looks blue in one clip and orange in another, suspect mixed light before reaching for a preset.
4. Compare before and after at the same brightness. Avoid crushing shadows or over-saturating skin and food.

**You've got it when:** your two clips look like they belong in the same video.

## Try it 5 - Run an accessibility pass

**Why it matters:** a quick review catches flashing, low contrast and missing information that would lock viewers out.

**Do this now:**
1. Watch the export with sound muted: is the message still clear from captions and visuals?
2. Watch with eyes away from the screen: does the sound carry the point?
3. Remove any rapid flashing or high-frequency strobing. Never rely on colour alone to give an instruction; add a label or shape.
4. Keep titles on screen long enough to read without pausing.

**You've got it when:** the message survives both the muted and the eyes-away test, with no flashing.

### Watch out for

- Small text or low-contrast captions crammed with words
- Publishing auto-captions without listening and correcting
- A logo or sticker that was copied or has unclear rights
- Overcorrecting saturation, contrast or skin tone
- Titles near the edges or under app controls
- Treating an app checker as the only accessibility review

## This week's build: a branded 30-second advert

Create a branded 30-second advert for a fictional Fresh Bites sample offer or your own permitted subject.

1. Include a title, a lower third, readable proofread captions, a logo end-card and consistent restrained colour.
2. Make the call to action harmless and honest, such as "See the sample menu". No invented price, location, testimonial or guarantee, and no real contact detail on the end-card.
3. Submit the project or a playable export plus a one-page design-choices note.
4. Also run the 90-minute mid-course checkpoint: take the supplied [raw-clip brief](/downloads/video-editing/checkpoint-raw-clips-brief.md) (or a facilitator-prepared permission-cleared set) and build a 30-second captioned video with a title and balanced audio, time-boxed. Submit the export and a short note with one decision and one remaining limitation.

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Text legibility and captions | 30% |
| Brand consistency | 25% |
| Colour and polish | 25% |
| Call-to-action clarity | 20% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] The advert is about 30 seconds and has a clear, truthful message.
- [ ] Title, lower third, captions and logo end-card are readable on a phone.
- [ ] Captions are proofread; contrast and safe margins have been reviewed.
- [ ] The palette and colour treatment are consistent and restrained.
- [ ] The design note explains the choices and no unlicensed asset is included.
- [ ] Checkpoint export plays from beginning to end and meets the brief.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Make one restrained fade or keyframed title; compare it with a static version.
- Caption a 15-second clip manually, then against an auto-caption draft, and count the fixes.
- Ask someone to watch muted and tell you what the advert is selling.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 min)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 min | Review brand brief, permissions and device capabilities |
| 10-25 min | Demonstrate title hierarchy, lower thirds and safe margins |
| 25-40 min | Caption a sample and proofread names and timing |
| 40-55 min | Compare exposure, white balance and saturation adjustments |
| 55-70 min | Run checkpoint edit: rough cut, title, captions, sound |
| 70-82 min | Export and run an accessibility review |
| 82-90 min | Review assignment rubric and note one next improvement |

## Facilitator Notes

The checkpoint clip set must be created by the facilitator or supplied with documented permission; do not copy video from social media. Verify any editor-specific caption, keyframe or colour feature before promising it is available. If auto-captioning is unavailable, accept manually written captions. No fixed platform safe-zone dimensions are asserted because current specifications could not be verified during preparation.
