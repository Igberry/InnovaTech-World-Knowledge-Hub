**Goal:** Produce a clear 30-second branded ad with legible text, accurate captions, balanced colour and an intentional ending.

## Learning Objectives

- Design a clear title hierarchy and readable lower third with safe margins.
- Apply a consistent fictional brand kit without inventing claims or copying protected logos.
- Generate or enter captions, proofread them, and check their timing and contrast.
- Make restrained exposure, contrast, white-balance and saturation corrections.
- Use a simple keyframe or overlay only when it improves meaning.
- Review a video for flashing effects, contrast and accessibility before delivery.

## Tools / Accounts Needed This Week

Use the Week 3 project and CapCut as the primary editor. The lesson concepts transfer to other editors, but exact caption and colour controls vary by version and plan. Use the supplied fictional Fresh Bites brief, brand kit, logo and caption guide. A phone-sized preview is important. Auto-captioning may require a connection, account, supported language or paid feature; manual captions or a transcript are acceptable. Do not upload sensitive footage to a caption service.

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Titles, hierarchy and legibility

A title gives the viewer orientation. Use a short main phrase, then a smaller supporting line only when it adds information. **Hierarchy** is the visual order in which information is noticed. Size, position, weight, contrast and spacing all contribute. If every line is bold, animated and large, nothing stands out.

Choose a readable typeface available on your device and check its licence for the intended use. Keep text away from the frame edge; different devices and platform controls may cover edge areas. Preview on the target screen and use the current platform preview when available. Do not rely on a universal safe-zone template because interfaces change.

A **lower third** is a short identification or context label positioned in the lower portion of a frame. It can identify a speaker, role or location if the information is accurate and approved. Do not create a real person's job title, business address or endorsement. In the Fresh Bites practice, labels such as "Sample menu" are clearly fictional and safe.

Check text contrast against the footage. White text over a bright plate or shirt may disappear; dark text over a dark background may also fail. Add a solid or semi-opaque backing or reposition the title. Do not use colour as the only way to distinguish one instruction from another.

### Lesson 2: Brand kit and graphic consistency

A small brand kit prevents every clip from looking unrelated. It can define the project voice, audience, two or three colours, a type choice, logo placement and a few usage rules. The supplied kit is for a fictional business. Its simple logo is an original SVG, not an existing brand. It is not a claim that a real Fresh Bites business exists.

Use the same title style across a series: consistent alignment, type treatment, accent and spacing. Restraint makes the content feel deliberate. A logo should not obscure the product or cover captions. Keep a version without a logo in case the client asks for a clean master. If a client supplies a logo, confirm they have authority to use it and keep the original file unchanged.

Do not copy a competitor's design, use a trademark as a decorative element, or download a template with unclear terms. If a graphic is created in the editor, verify that any included stock elements, fonts or stickers are licensed for the intended use. The practice kit avoids these uncertain assets.

### Lesson 3: Captions, subtitles and accessible text

Captions display spoken words and meaningful sound cues. Subtitles may translate speech or show dialogue. In practice, both can help people who watch without sound, people who are deaf or hard of hearing, and viewers processing speech in another language. Captions need to match what was said; they are not a place to rewrite the speaker's meaning.

Automatic captions can save time, but may mishear local names, accents, Nigerian English, pidgin, technical terms and short words. Treat them as a draft. Listen while reading each line. Correct spelling, punctuation, timing and speaker attribution. If auto-captioning is unavailable, create captions manually or provide a transcript and a clear captioned alternative if the assignment calls for on-screen text.

Keep each caption concise, use sentence case, and break at a natural pause. Avoid placing text across a face, product detail or interface control. Check contrast and reading time on a phone. Do not flash captions rapidly or use an unreadably small type size to fit an entire sentence on one line. The caption guide includes a review checklist.

### Lesson 4: Basic colour correction

**Exposure** describes how light or dark the image appears. **Contrast** is the difference between darker and lighter areas. **White balance** helps neutral objects appear neutral under the lighting. **Saturation** describes colour intensity. These adjustments are tools for consistency, not a requirement to apply a dramatic look.

Correct in a simple order: compare clips, adjust exposure so important detail is visible, balance the colour temperature, make a modest contrast adjustment, then check saturation. Use a neutral reference in the scene when available. If a white shirt appears strongly blue in one clip and orange in another, investigate mixed light or automatic camera changes before adding a colour preset.

Avoid clipping highlights until detail disappears, crushing shadows into black, or over-saturating skin and food. A screen's brightness and colour vary, so use a conservative approach. Compare before and after at the same playback conditions. If a tool provides a scopes view, learn it as an optional aid; do not assume every app has the same scopes or controls.

### Lesson 5: Keyframes, overlays and motion

A **keyframe** marks a property at one point in time. Additional keyframes can animate position, scale, opacity or another property, depending on the editor. For example, a title may fade in and remain still. The interface and available properties vary. Use one simple motion only when it helps the viewer notice or understand something.

An **overlay** places one visual element over another, such as a title, logo or picture-in-picture. Keep important information in the main image visible. Check the overlay against the footage at the beginning and end of its duration. A mask can reveal part of a layer, but masks and green-screen tools are optional introductions, not requirements for the assignment.

Stickers, effects and animated elements can distract or reduce professionalism. Use them only when they support the message and are licensed. Decorative effects are not needed. Never use an effect to imply an event, reaction or product result that did not occur.

### Lesson 6: Accessibility, motion and review

Accessible video starts with a clear message and readable content. Use captions, meaningful contrast, restrained motion, and a logical audio description where necessary. Avoid rapid flashing and high-frequency strobing because they can trigger serious reactions in some viewers. If a visual effect is not essential, remove it.

Do not communicate an instruction only by colour, such as "choose the red button." Add a label or shape as a second cue. Keep titles on screen long enough to read. A viewer should not need to pause constantly to understand a short ad. If the clip includes an important visual detail with no spoken explanation, consider a brief descriptive caption or voice-over.

Run the app's accessibility checker if one exists, but do not treat it as proof of accessibility. Watch the full export with sound muted and then with eyes away from the screen to identify missing information. Check on a small display and ask another person whether the message is clear.

### Lesson 7: Mid-course checkpoint

At the end of Week 4, complete the 90-minute practical checkpoint: take a supplied or learner-shot permitted clip set and create a 30-second captioned video with audio levels and a title. The supplied brief explains the expected footage; it contains no copyrighted video. A mentor may prepare a small local set only if permission and licence are documented.

Time-box the work. Spend a few minutes reviewing the brief and clips, then make a rough sequence, add a title and captions, balance the audio, export a review copy and inspect it. Use a checklist rather than spending the full session on animation. Submit the project or export and a short note describing one decision and one remaining limitation.

### Lesson 8: Branded 30-second ad

For the assignment, choose a fictional Fresh Bites sample offer without inventing a price, location, testimonial or guarantee. Include a title, a lower third, captions and the simple logo end-card. Keep colours and typography consistent. Make the call to action a harmless phrase such as "See the sample menu." Use your own footage or a subject you have permission to film.

The end-card should remain on screen long enough to read, but not so long that the video feels unfinished. Make sure it does not contain a real contact detail. Review the picture, captions, sound and logo permissions before export. A polished graphic cannot make an unverified claim appropriate.

## Common Mistakes

- Using small text or low-contrast captions to fit too many words.
- Publishing auto-captions without listening and correcting them.
- Adding a logo or sticker that was copied or has unclear usage rights.
- Overcorrecting saturation, contrast or skin tone.
- Placing all titles near the edges or underneath app controls.
- Making keyframes and motion effects more noticeable than the message.
- Treating an app checker as the only accessibility review.

## Practice Exercises

1. Make a two-level title and test its reading order at phone size.
2. Add a lower third with fictional, verified information and safe margins.
3. Caption a 15-second voice clip manually; compare it with an auto-caption draft if available.
4. Correct two clips so they look consistent under similar light.
5. Create one restrained fade or keyframed title and compare it with a static version.
6. Watch the export muted and describe what information is missing.
7. Remove a flashing or unnecessary effect and explain the change.
8. Run the 90-minute checkpoint using the supplied brief and review checklist.

## Weekly Assignment (Due Before Week 5)

Create a branded 30-second ad for a fictional Fresh Bites sample offer or your own permitted subject. Include a title, lower third, readable captions, logo end-card and consistent restrained colour. Submit the editor project or a playable export plus a one-page design-choices note. Complete the 90-minute checkpoint at the end of the week using the supplied raw-clip brief or a facilitator-prepared permission-cleared set.

**Grading guide:** text legibility and captions 30%, brand consistency 25%, colour and polish 25%, call-to-action clarity 20%.

**Definition of done**

- [ ] The ad is about 30 seconds and has a clear, truthful message.
- [ ] Title, lower third, captions and logo end-card are readable on a phone.
- [ ] Captions are proofread; contrast and safe margins have been reviewed.
- [ ] The palette and colour treatment are consistent and restrained.
- [ ] The design note explains the choices and no unlicensed asset is included.
- [ ] Checkpoint export plays from beginning to end and meets the brief.
