## Who this course is for

This eight-week beginner course is for people who want to make social videos, event highlights and promotional videos for themselves or clients. You will work with a fictional Yenagoa food business, Fresh Bites, and make original practice footage. You do not need a real kitchen: choose a safe, consented subject such as your own meal, a craft, a shop with permission, or a product you own.

The course uses CapCut for Weeks 1–5, then DaVinci Resolve for long-form editing and colour/audio practice in Weeks 6–8. Shotcut is a lightweight alternative when a computer cannot comfortably run the preferred editor. The interfaces, features, free-plan terms, watermarks and system requirements can change and may differ by device or region. Use the official sites listed in Resources, check the current terms before installing or relying on a feature, and never download cracked software.

| Week | Focus | Main deliverable |

| 4 | Titles, graphics, captions and colour | Branded 30-second captioned ad and checkpoint |
| 5 | Short-form content | Five-video series and a seven-day posting plan |

| 8 | Export, delivery, business and capstone | Portfolio package, showreel and presentation |

## Time budget

Plan for about six to eight hours of independent reading and practice each week, plus one 90-minute mentoring session. Allow extra time for transferring and backing up footage; video files can be large. Download the small templates on a reliable connection, keep a local working copy, and avoid uploading large clips when data is limited. Actual export times depend on the device, project and settings.

## Assessment and completion

Weekly assignments for Weeks 1–7 contribute 40% in total. The Week 4, 90-minute mid-course checkpoint contributes 15%; the Week 8 capstone and showreel contribute 30%; attendance and consistency contribute 15%. The capstone rubric is editing craft 35%, story and brief fit 20%, workflow and documents 20%, licensing and ethics 10%, and presentation 15%.

The final package contains a 60–90 second showreel, one two-to-three-minute long-form video, three short-form videos, a project folder with brief and delivery documents, and a ten-minute presentation. The clips should use the learner's own footage or footage they have explicit permission to use.

## Tools and access

A phone with a working camera and enough free storage is assumed. A mid-range phone is a planning assumption, not a guarantee that every setting exists on every device. A laptop is strongly preferred for file organisation and longer edits. CapCut is the main editor for the first five weeks, DaVinci Resolve is the main editor for Weeks 6–8, and Shotcut is an alternative for lower-spec computers. Feature names and availability vary. If an app or feature is unavailable, complete the same editing goal in another editor and note the difference.

The course does not require paid subscriptions, paid media, a gimbal, drone, or new camera. Use a clean phone lens, daylight, a stable surface or homemade support, and a quiet room before buying accessories. Check free-plan terms, export options, watermark behavior, supported operating systems and installation requirements on the relevant official site before beginning; these details were not independently verified while this course was prepared.

## Safe, ethical and accessible practice

Obtain clear permission before filming an identifiable person or private location. For children, obtain guardian permission and follow venue rules; the safest practice exercise is to avoid filming children. Do not film sensitive conversations, private screens, addresses, identity documents, payment details, or people who have not agreed. Never use a drone without checking local rules and permissions. This course includes only a minimal safety note about drones and gimbals.

Use music, graphics, fonts and stock footage only when the terms permit the intended use. Keep a licence log and the original source or proof of permission. A “free download” label does not automatically grant commercial or client-use rights. Do not use commercial songs in client or monetised work unless you have a licence that clearly permits that use. Captions should be readable, timed, proofread and placed away from important screen controls; avoid flashing effects and do not communicate meaning through colour alone.

## Mentoring format

Each week includes a 90-minute screen-based session: check footage and device constraints, demonstrate a workflow, edit together, review a learner cut, and plan the next assignment. Bring a project copy and remove personal or private material first. If connectivity or power is unreliable, charge devices beforehand, work from local copies, save versions, and back up when power is available. Do not upload student, client or event footage to an unapproved cloud service.

## Honest outcomes

Success means planning a shoot, capturing usable footage, editing clear short and long videos, handling sound and captions thoughtfully, and delivering organised practice work with permission and licence records. A showreel can help explain what you have practised; this course does not guarantee clients, jobs, income, views, audience growth, or platform approval.
