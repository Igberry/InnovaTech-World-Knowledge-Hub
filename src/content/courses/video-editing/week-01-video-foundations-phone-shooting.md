**Goal:** By the end of the week you will have shot a varied, stable, well-organised set of phone clips, ready to cut together in Week 2. Almost all of this week is you, a phone and a subject.

## What you'll be able to do

- Pick one camera setting and keep it steady across a shoot
- Capture wide, medium, close-up and detail shots that actually cut together
- Get clean natural sound from a phone
- Shoot three clips in both horizontal and vertical
- Organise originals in a project folder and prove a backup opens

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open the [shot list](/downloads/video-editing/shot-list-10.md), [shooting checklist](/downloads/video-editing/shooting-checklist.md) and [consent one-pager](/downloads/video-editing/consent-and-permission-one-pager.md).
- Pick your subject: fictional Fresh Bites, or your own safe, consented object such as a craft or your own product. No strangers, children or private property.
- Warm-up: record a 10-second test and note its orientation, length, rough file size and sound. This tells you if the setting is right before you commit to the shoot.

## Try it 1 - Lock one camera setting and leave it

**Why it matters:** switching frame rate, resolution or orientation mid-shoot makes clips that fight each other in the edit. Pick once, stay there.

**Do this now:**
1. Open the camera's normal video mode. If you can choose, pick a frame rate your phone supports (24, 25, 30 or 60 are common) and 1080p if storage allows.
2. Turn on the grid. Tap to focus, and lock focus and exposure if your phone offers it.
3. Record a 5-second sample and play it back. Check focus, exposure, sound and file size.

**You've got it when:** you have one setting chosen and you will not change it for the rest of the shoot.

> Resolution is not a cure. A steady, well-lit 1080p clip beats a dark, shaky 4K one every time.

## Try it 2 - Shoot four shot sizes of one thing

**Why it matters:** variety is what lets you cut. Four sizes of the same subject give an editor options.

**Do this now - film the same subject four ways:**
1. **Wide:** the whole scene or arrangement.
2. **Medium:** the activity with some context.
3. **Close-up:** a person, object or action.
4. **Detail:** one small feature, like a texture or a tool.

Start recording a few seconds before the action and hold after it, every time.

**You've got it when:** you can line up four clearly different shots of one subject.

## Try it 3 - Make the light explain the subject

**Why it matters:** a window is a free, large, soft light. Used well it shows shape and texture; used badly it turns your subject into a silhouette.

**Do this now:**
1. Place the subject facing or angled toward a window. Film it.
2. Now put the window behind the subject and film again. Compare how dark it goes.
3. Hold a white sheet of paper opposite the window to bounce light back. See the difference. (Keep paper away from heat, flame and water.)

**You've got it when:** you can point the subject at the light and keep it off a bright background.

## Try it 4 - Get clean sound from a phone

**Why it matters:** viewers forgive a simple picture but not speech they cannot understand.

**Do this now:**
1. Record a voice or natural-sound test close to the source, then again from across the room. Compare with headphones.
2. Pause fans, ask for quiet, and record a few seconds of **room tone** (the location's ordinary sound, no speech).
3. Listen for traffic, a fan, clipping or a distant voice before you commit to a real take.

**You've got it when:** the close recording is clearly the cleaner one, and you know your quietest spot.

## Try it 5 - Shoot both orientations

**Why it matters:** the same story often needs a vertical version for phones and a horizontal version for landscape. A crop cannot invent what was never filmed.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick three of your scenes.
2. Film each one as a separate take in horizontal, then again in vertical.
3. Keep the important subject away from the very edge so both versions stay usable.

**You've got it when:** three scenes exist in both shapes, each a clean separate take.

### Watch out for

- Changing frame rate or orientation without a reason
- Treating resolution as a substitute for focus and light
- Starting or stopping exactly on the action, leaving no handles
- Recording speech from too far away and hoping to fix it later
- Filming people or private places without clear permission
- Deleting the only original after importing

## This week's build: your ten-clip shot set

Shoot the full ten-clip set from the [shot list](/downloads/video-editing/shot-list-10.md) using Fresh Bites or your own safe, consented subject. It gives you a beginning, middle and end: an establishing view, process, details, sound and a closing image.

1. Capture all ten planned clips, each with handles before and after the action.
2. Record three selected scenes in both horizontal and vertical.
3. Name and organise everything in the folder template, keep originals in `01-Raw`, and make a second copy that opens.
4. Write a 150-word shooting note: what worked, and one thing you would change.

Before you leave the location, watch a few clips. If focus or audio failed, reshoot while the setup is still there. That one habit saves whole weeks.

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Variety and coverage | 30% |
| Stability and exposure | 25% |
| Audio clarity | 20% |
| Organisation and backup | 15% |
| Reflection note | 10% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] Ten distinct planned clips are present and reviewable.
- [ ] Three selected scenes have separate horizontal and vertical versions.
- [ ] Permission and safety checks are complete.
- [ ] Originals are named and stored in `01-Raw`; no private information is exposed.
- [ ] A second copy opens, and the 150-word note identifies one improvement.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Explain frame rate, resolution and aspect ratio to a friend in your own words.
- Shoot one "five second" detail you could imagine opening a Fresh Bites advert.
- Try one slow, rehearsed pan on a clear, safe path and see if it stays watchable.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 min)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 min | Confirm safe subject, permission, devices and storage |
| 10-25 min | Demonstrate frame rate, resolution, aspect ratio and test recording |
| 25-40 min | Practise steady wide, medium, close-up and detail shots |
| 40-55 min | Compare window light, background and audio tests |
| 55-70 min | Review the shot list and assemble a folder structure |
| 70-82 min | Learners show a clip and explain what it contributes |
| 82-90 min | Check assignment, backup and permission boundaries |

## Facilitator Notes

Use a safe, non-identifying classroom object if a learner has no permitted subject. Do not require anyone to film their home or upload footage. Demonstrate camera controls on the available devices without implying that every phone has identical features. Keep the mentor's sample footage local and permission-cleared. Allow a storyboard or contact-sheet alternative if a learner cannot safely record.

## Can you do these now?

1. How are frame rate, resolution and aspect ratio different?
2. Why should a clip begin before the action and continue briefly after it?
3. What can you do to make phone audio clearer before editing?
4. Why is synchronised cloud storage not always a second backup?
5. What should you do if a person does not agree to be filmed?
