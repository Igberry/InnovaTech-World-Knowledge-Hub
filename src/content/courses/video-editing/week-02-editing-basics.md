**Goal:** Turn your Week 1 clips into a coherent 30-second rough edit with clean cuts, one speed change, one reframe and an export you have actually watched. This week you learn by cutting, not reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Find your way around any editor: media, viewer, timeline, tracks, inspector
- Import copies without touching your originals
- Trim, split and ripple-delete to remove dead time
- Build a 30-second story with clean cuts and good pace
- Export a draft and catch its problems before anyone else does

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open CapCut on phone or desktop and your Week 1 `01-Raw` folder.
- Skim the [edit review checklist](/downloads/video-editing/edit-review-checklist.md) and the [editing actions reference](/downloads/video-editing/editing-shortcuts-capcut.md).
- Warm-up: import a copy of one Week 1 clip and confirm the original in `01-Raw` is untouched. That is the whole safety habit in one move.

## Try it 1 - Learn the interface by touching it

**Why it matters:** every editor uses the same four or five regions. Spot them once and any app feels familiar.

**Do this now:**
1. Open CapCut and find each: the **media pool** (your clips), the **viewer** (preview), the **timeline** (clips over time), the **tracks** (picture, sound, titles), and the **inspector** (settings for the selected item).
2. Create a project, save it into `04-Projects` with a clear name straight away.
3. Set the project shape to match your intended delivery (vertical or horizontal).

**You've got it when:** you can point to each region and your saved project sits in `04-Projects`.

## Try it 2 - Import copies and pick your best moments

**Why it matters:** the edit is won in selection. The strongest 20 seconds of footage beats a busy minute of everything.

**Do this now:**
1. Import copies of your Week 1 clips. Confirm the originals stay put.
2. Watch each clip and jot the strongest usable section on a quick log:

| Clip | Best moment | Useful detail | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establishing view | | | |
| Process shot | | | |
| Close-up | | | |

3. Drop anything that exposes private info, shows someone without permission, or makes an unverified claim.

**You've got it when:** you have chosen moments that show a beginning, an action and a result.

## Try it 3 - Split, trim and kill a dead pause

**Why it matters:** removing dead time is the single biggest upgrade a beginner edit can get.

**Do this now:**
1. Lay your chosen clips on the timeline in an order that makes sense.
2. To cut a pause: put the playhead at its start and split, move to its end and split, select the middle and delete.
3. Watch whether the gap closes (ripple) or stays. Set the behaviour you want and inspect the result.
4. Play across each cut, not just the first frame, listening for a pop or a black frame.

**You've got it when:** your sequence plays with no awkward dead gaps and no accidental black frames.

## Try it 4 - Cut on action

**Why it matters:** cutting while a movement continues hides the join. It is the trick that makes edits feel smooth and professional.

**Do this now:**
1. Find two clips of the same movement from different sizes or angles (a hand reaching, then placing).
2. Cut from the first to the second while the movement is still going.
3. Match the direction and timing. If it jumps or repeats, cut before the motion starts instead.

**You've got it when:** the cut feels natural and the action flows across it.

## Try it 5 - One speed change and one reframe

**Why it matters:** a small speed change compresses a slow process; a reframe adapts a shot to another shape. Used sparingly they add polish.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick a repetitive process clip and speed it up. Check it still looks smooth.
2. Reframe one shot (crop, scale, reposition) and keep faces, hands and key objects visible on a phone-sized preview.
3. If your device cannot do one of these, document the limitation and swap in a different clip. That is a valid answer.

**You've got it when:** one clip is sped up cleanly and one is reframed without losing the subject.

### Watch out for

- Moving or deleting source clips while the editor is linked to them
- Assuming the timeline is saved because the preview still plays
- Deleting a clip without checking whether the gap closes
- Adding transitions and effects before the rough cut makes sense
- Cropping a vertical version until the subject is lost
- Exporting and never watching the actual file

## This week's build: "A Day at Fresh Bites" in 30 seconds

Edit a 30-second video from your Week 1 clips, for Fresh Bites or your own permitted subject. Think of it as a tiny story: setup, process, result, close.

1. Include at least 12 deliberate cuts, one speed change and one reframed shot.
2. No music yet. Use shot length and movement for rhythm.
3. Keep working versions (`rough-v01`, `review-v02`) in `04-Projects` rather than overwriting.
4. Export at 1080p if your device handles it reliably, otherwise document the constraint and submit the best playable review export. Watch it start to finish, then write a 100-word edit note: the sequence, one decision, one improvement.

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Cut quality | 35% |
| Pacing | 30% |
| Technical export | 15% |
| Story clarity | 15% |
| Edit note | 5% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] The video is approximately 30 seconds and has at least 12 deliberate cuts.
- [ ] One speed change and one reframed shot are visible or a limitation is documented.
- [ ] The sequence has a clear opening, process and ending.
- [ ] No music, unpermitted footage or unsupported claims are included.
- [ ] The export plays to the end and the project and source files remain organised.
- [ ] The edit note is about 100 words and names one next improvement.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Make two versions of the same clips in a different order; decide which is clearer.
- Try a J-cut using natural sound: let the next clip's sound start before its picture.
- Export a second draft and play it on a different device to catch what you missed.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 min)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 min | Check project folder, backup and app version |
| 10-25 min | Identify interface regions and set up a project |
| 25-45 min | Demonstrate importing, splitting, trimming and gap handling |
| 45-60 min | Build a rough cut and discuss pacing and cut on action |
| 60-72 min | Practise one speed change and reframe with a short clip |
| 72-82 min | Export and review a draft against the checklist |
| 82-90 min | Learners explain an edit choice and plan the assignment |

## Facilitator Notes

Do not require a specific commercial plan, account sign-in or cloud upload. Offer a phone workflow and a low-spec alternative. If an app feature is missing, assess the editing concept and ask the learner to document the limitation. Do not request private client footage; use learner-owned or permission-cleared clips.
