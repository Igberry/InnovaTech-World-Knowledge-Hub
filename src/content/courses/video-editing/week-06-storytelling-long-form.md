**Goal:** Plan, shoot and edit a two-to-three-minute story or event highlight with a clear structure, useful B-roll, understandable sound and organised files. You build it in stages this week, not all at once.

## What you'll be able to do

- Shape a story with hook, context, build, payoff and closing
- Write a two-column script that ties sound to picture
- Record a consented interview or voice-over with clean audio and supporting B-roll
- Organise footage in bins and work through rough, fine and final cuts
- Find your way around DaVinci Resolve's main work areas
- Edit on a low-spec machine without giving up

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open the [story script](/downloads/video-editing/story-script-template.md), [storyboard](/downloads/video-editing/storyboard-template.md), [interview questions](/downloads/video-editing/interview-question-bank.md) and [editing log](/downloads/video-editing/editing-log-template.md).
- Check DaVinci Resolve's current system requirements before installing, and test a short project first. Shotcut or CapCut is fine if your hardware cannot run Resolve.
- Warm-up: write your story idea in a single sentence. If you cannot, you are not ready to shoot yet.

## Try it 1 - Lock the story in one sentence

**Why it matters:** footage without a central idea becomes a pretty montage that says nothing.

**Do this now:**
1. Write the one thing the viewer should understand, in a sentence.
2. Map it to hook, context, build, payoff, closing. The hook raises a question or detail; context sets the scene; build develops it; payoff resolves it; closing leaves a useful idea.
3. List which shots are evidence for that story and drop attractive but unrelated footage.

**You've got it when:** you can state the story in one sentence and name the shots that prove it.

## Try it 2 - Write a two-column script and storyboard

**Why it matters:** planning sound beside picture stops you recording a paragraph with nothing to show on screen.

**Do this now:**
1. Put spoken words on the left, the matching picture or B-roll on the right.
2. Sketch a simple six-frame storyboard. Boxes are fine; this is a plan, not art.
3. Write sentences that sound natural aloud. Mark pauses and tricky pronunciations.

**You've got it when:** every spoken line has a planned image next to it.

## Try it 3 - Record a consented interview

**Why it matters:** a clear, consented interview is the backbone of a story. Bad framing or sound sinks it.

**Do this now:**
1. Frame the person near eye level with some space in the direction they look. Keep the face off a bright window.
2. Do a sound test on headphones. Keep the mic close and stable, no handling noise.
3. Ask open questions one at a time, wait for the full answer, and let them finish. Follow up neutrally; never feed them words.
4. Record the specific permission in your project notes (classroom only, portfolio, or public). Avoid filming minors.

**You've got it when:** you have a clear, consented answer with clean sound.

## Try it 4 - Gather B-roll that tells the truth

**Why it matters:** B-roll covers cuts and illustrates points, but misused it fakes a different event.

**Do this now:**
1. For a spoken line like "I check each detail", film the detail actually being checked.
2. Capture several distances: establishing, medium, close-up, detail. Record each long enough to cut.
3. Keep a clip log with date, location, permission and what each shot shows. Never present footage from another date as live coverage.

**You've got it when:** your B-roll matches what the speaker actually says and is honestly logged.

## Try it 5 - Edit in stages inside Resolve

**Why it matters:** a long edit is unmanageable in one pass. Stages keep you sane and the story clear.

**Do this now:**
1. In the Media area, make bins for interviews, B-roll, audio, graphics and sequences. Name clips usefully.
2. Build a **string-out** (candidate clips in rough order), then a **rough cut** (story and timing), then a **fine cut** (pacing, sound, graphics).
3. Use the Cut or Edit area to assemble, Color for consistency, Fairlight for audio, Deliver for export. Learn each area's goal rather than memorising menus.
4. If a line is shortened, play it with its neighbours and confirm the meaning is unchanged.

**You've got it when:** your story plays as a rough cut with the right clips in the right order.

## Low-spec workflow quick wins

- Keep source files on a local drive with room for project, cache and exports.
- Use proxy or optimised media for smooth playback and never delete the originals.
- If proxies are missing, lower the preview resolution, edit fewer clips, or switch to Shotcut.
- Assembling on a phone is acceptable if the deliverable stays accessible and backed up. Record the constraint and what you would revisit on a stronger machine.

### Watch out for

- Starting with a montage before deciding the central idea
- Leading interview questions or editing an answer out of context
- Using B-roll from another event as if it happened during the interview
- Overloading bins with duplicates and losing the originals
- Adding colour and music before the story and dialogue are clear
- Deleting source footage after generating proxies

## This week's build: a two-to-three-minute story

Create a two-to-three-minute brand story or small event highlight, such as the fictional story of Yenagoa Fresh Bites or an event you have permission to film.

1. Use an interview or voice-over, relevant B-roll, titles and proofread captions.
2. Use colour to make shots consistent, not to disguise anything. Music must be licensed for the use or omitted and explained.
3. Use Resolve if your computer supports it; otherwise use Shotcut or CapCut and note why.
4. Ask a reviewer one focused question ("Is the opening clear?") and record the change in the [editing log](/downloads/video-editing/editing-log-template.md). Submit the video, the script and the log.

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Story structure | 30% |
| Editing quality | 25% |
| Audio and colour | 20% |
| Script and log | 15% |
| Technical delivery | 10% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] The video is two to three minutes with a clear central story.
- [ ] Interview or voice-over is permission-cleared and intelligible.
- [ ] B-roll supports the story without misrepresenting when or where it was captured.
- [ ] Captions, titles, colour and sound have been reviewed.
- [ ] Music has a suitable licence or is omitted and explained.
- [ ] Script and editing log are submitted; source and project files remain organised.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Record one answer, then two B-roll shots that illustrate it, and cut them together.
- Try a proxy workflow on a short copy and feel the playback difference.
- Keep a version before and after a big change so you can restore a choice.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 min)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 min | Confirm permission, hardware and story idea |
| 10-25 min | Build a script and storyboard |
| 25-40 min | Demonstrate a consented interview and B-roll plan |
| 40-55 min | Organise media and assemble a string-out |
| 55-70 min | Review rough-cut story, audio and colour |

| 82-90 min | Set feedback question and assignment milestones |

## Facilitator Notes

Do not record a learner or interview subject without explicit permission. Use a short local sample if the machine cannot run Resolve, and do not imply that every computer meets current system requirements. Verify current Resolve version, free features and system requirements before the session; these were not independently verified during course preparation. Keep a CapCut or Shotcut equivalent ready.
