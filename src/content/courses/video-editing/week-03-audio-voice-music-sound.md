**Goal:** Re-cut your Week 2 video with a clear voice-over, balanced sound and a documented licence for every added track. You record, listen and mix this week, with almost no reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Write and record a tight 20 to 30 second voice-over that is easy to understand
- Use meters and your ears to avoid clipping and buried dialogue
- Balance voice, music and natural sound so speech always wins
- Duck music under speech with an automatic or manual control
- Keep a licence log so every sound is cleared for your use

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open your Week 2 project, the [voice-over template](/downloads/video-editing/voice-over-script-template.md) and the [audio checklist](/downloads/video-editing/audio-checklist.md).
- Find your quietest room with soft furnishings (curtains, cushions, clothes) to soak up echo.
- Warm-up: record the same test sentence from close up and from across the room. Compare with headphones. The close one is almost always the keeper.

## Try it 1 - Write for the ear and record short takes

**Why it matters:** a voice-over written like an essay sounds stiff. Short spoken sentences land.

**Do this now:**
1. Write a 20 to 30 second script with one main idea, using the template. Short sentences, familiar words.
2. Read it aloud once and cut any repeated phrase.
3. Record it in several short takes, leaving a pause before and after each sentence. If you fumble, pause and repeat the line rather than stopping.
4. Record a few seconds of room tone. Save the best take in `02-Audio`.

**You've got it when:** you have a clean take where every word is clear and natural.

## Try it 2 - Catch clipping before it ruins a take

**Why it matters:** a clipped recording is distorted for good. You cannot fix it by turning it down later.

**Do this now:**
1. Watch the meter while you speak. If it slams the top and sounds harsh, that is clipping.
2. Lower the input level or move back from the mic, then record again.
3. If a take is very quiet, notice how raising it later also raises room noise. Aim for a comfortable middle.

**You've got it when:** your meter sits below the top and the voice is clear without being harsh.

## Try it 3 - Check the mix on two devices

**Why it matters:** headphones reveal clicks and hum; a phone speaker reveals dialogue that vanishes in the mix. You need both.

**Do this now:**
1. Listen to your voice take on headphones. Note any click, hum, wind or mouth noise.
2. Play it back on a phone speaker or second device. Can you still hear every word?
3. Trust your ears over the waveform size.

**You've got it when:** the voice is clear on both headphones and a small speaker.

## Try it 4 - Place the voice and duck the music

**Why it matters:** ducking lowers the music while someone speaks, so the message always comes through.

**Do this now:**
1. Put your voice-over on its own track, aligned with the picture it explains.
2. Add music on a separate track (only if its licence allows your use, see Try it 5).
3. Use your editor's ducking control, or drop volume keyframes manually so music dips under speech and lifts in the gaps.
4. Add short fades at the start and end. Listen: the viewer should notice the story, not the volume moving.

**You've got it when:** speech stays clear over the music and the dips sound smooth.

## Try it 5 - Clear every sound in a licence log

**Why it matters:** an in-app or "royalty-free" track is not automatically cleared for every client, country or platform. The log is your proof.

**Do this now:**
1. Open the [licence log](/downloads/video-editing/licence-log-template.csv).
2. For each added sound, record: source, creator, exact terms, date checked, and any required attribution.
3. For sounds you recorded yourself, note who recorded them and whether other voices or property appear.
4. If the terms are unclear, do not use the track. Omit it and note why. Music is optional this week.

**You've got it when:** every sound in your edit has a clear, complete log row.

### Watch out for

- Recording in a noisy room and expecting one filter to save it
- Holding the phone too far away or moving it mid-take
- Raising a clipped recording instead of recording a clean one
- Picking music by its waveform instead of listening to the mix
- Assuming an in-app track is cleared for every use
- Editing spoken words so the speaker seems to say something they did not

## This week's build: your Week 2 video, now with voice

Re-edit your Week 2 video with a 20 to 30 second voice-over, balanced audio, and documented sound.

1. Record the voice-over and place it against the right shots.
2. Add music and one sound effect only if the licence permits; otherwise document that you omitted music and why.
3. Apply ducking or manual volume so speech stays clear.
4. Submit the new edit, the script, and a complete licence log. Noise reduction, EQ and normalisation are optional polish; make a duplicate first, apply the smallest change that helps, and compare at equal loudness. Never add a sound effect that fakes an event that did not happen.

### Grading guide

| Criteria | Weight |
|---|---|
| Audio clarity | 30% |
| Levels and ducking | 25% |
| Music choice and licence log | 25% |
| Script quality | 20% |

### Definition of done

- [ ] The voice-over is 20 to 30 seconds and understandable in the full export.
- [ ] Music and sound effect are licensed for the intended use or explicitly omitted.
- [ ] The mix is reviewed on headphones and a second playback option if available.
- [ ] The licence log is complete, including self-recorded and omitted assets where relevant.
- [ ] The edit remains a coherent 30-second piece with no unsupported claims.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Record one line in three tones (calm, warm, energetic) and pick the best fit.
- Try cutting one edit on a musical beat; drop it if it forces an unnatural cut.
- Compare your mix with music removed entirely; decide if the music earns its place.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 min)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 min | Check the script and licence boundaries |
| 10-25 min | Demonstrate quiet-room recording and a phone microphone test |
| 25-40 min | Record and select a voice-over take |
| 40-55 min | Place voice, natural sound and music on separate tracks |
| 55-70 min | Practise ducking or manual volume changes and listen for clipping |
| 70-82 min | Review licence log and export playback |
| 82-90 min | Confirm assignment deliverables and safe asset choices |

## Facilitator Notes

Use only a track whose current terms have been checked for the exact exercise, or ask learners to omit music. Do not distribute copyrighted songs as class starters. The authoring environment could not independently verify current numeric level guidance or library-specific licences; confirm before teaching any fixed target or endorsing a track. Keep recordings local and do not collect voice files without permission.
