**Goal:** Set up the accounts, tools, and workspace you'll use throughout the course, and make a
realistic plan for working through power and data challenges.

Remote work is as much about being **reliable** as being skilled. This week you build the foundation.

## Learning Objectives

By the end you can:

- Create a professional email and the core accounts (LinkedIn, a document tool)
- Set up a workspace you can take video calls from
- Make a backup plan for power and internet
- Organise your job-search files

## Accounts to Create This Week

- A **professional email** (your name, not a nickname). Gmail or Outlook are both fine and free.
- A **LinkedIn** account (we build the profile properly in Week 2).
- A **document tool**: Google Workspace (Docs, Sheets) or Microsoft 365 free - for your CV and tracker.

## Lesson 1: A professional email

Employers judge you by small signals. Create an email like `firstname.lastname@gmail.com`, not
`coolguy2000@...`. Use the same name across your email, LinkedIn, and CV so you're easy to recognise.

> **Tip:** If your ideal name is taken, add a middle initial or your field, e.g.
> `jane.e.okoro@gmail.com` or `jane.okoro.va@gmail.com`.

## Lesson 2: Your workspace

You don't need an office - you need a **quiet, tidy spot** you can take a video call from:

- A plain background (a wall is fine), decent light (face a window), and low noise.
- Your device positioned so the camera is near eye level.
- Test your camera and microphone (search "test my webcam" or use your video app's settings).

## Lesson 3: A power and data plan

Unreliable power and data are real - employers understand that, but they expect you to **manage** it:

- **Power:** keep a charged power bank; know the nearest reliable place to work or charge; do heavy
  tasks when power is on.
- **Data:** keep a second SIM or a data bundle as backup; know a reliable hotspot; download what you
  need when connected so you can keep working offline.
- **Communicate early:** if something fails, a quick "my power is out, I'll be back by X" message keeps
  trust. Reliability is about communication, not perfection.

> **Important:** In interviews you may be asked how you handle power or internet problems. Having a
> clear, calm plan (above) is a strong answer.

## Lesson 4: Organise your files

Create a simple folder for your search:

```
remote-ready/
├── cv/                (your CV versions, as PDFs)
├── applications/      (the application tracker, saved job adverts)
└── portfolio/         (your work samples)
```

Download the **remote-readiness checklist** (in this lesson's files) and keep it open all course.

## Facilitator Notes

Many learners underestimate the "reliability" signal - stress that communication during power/data
outages is what builds trust, not pretending problems don't happen. Check that everyone's camera and
mic actually work in session one; a failed first interview over tech problems is demoralising and
avoidable. Keep personal details off public profiles.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why does a professional email address matter for remote work?
2. What makes a good spot for a video interview?
3. What is your plan if the power goes out during work hours?
4. Why is communicating about a problem often more important than the problem itself?
