**Goal:** By the end of this week you'll have a simple, shareable portfolio of your 2-4 best pieces, each with
a short case study, plus one reference requested. You build it this week from work you already have.

## What you'll be able to do

- Choose and present 2-4 pieces of work that prove your skills
- Build a simple portfolio (a free site, a document, or a profile) with a shareable link
- Write clear case studies: the task, what you did, the result
- Ask for and use references and recommendations properly

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Open the [remote-readiness checklist](/downloads/remote-ready/remote-readiness-checklist.md).
- Warm-up: look at two portfolios in your field (search "[your field] portfolio examples"). Note one thing
  that makes each feel trustworthy.

## Try it 1 - List everything you could show

**Why it matters:** most learners think they have "nothing to show" and are wrong. Coursework and personal
work count.

**Do this now:**
1. In a doc, list every piece of work you could show: InnovaTech projects, volunteer work, personal pages,
   spreadsheets you organised, content you wrote.
2. Add any **before/after** examples (a messy inbox you tidied, a document you cleaned up).
3. Note any **results** others saw (followers grown, time saved, errors reduced).

**You've got it when:** you have a list of at least four possible pieces, even if some feel small.

## Try it 2 - Pick your best 2-4

**Why it matters:** a few strong pieces beat a long list of weak ones. Quality builds trust.

**Do this now:**
1. From your list, star the 2-4 pieces that best match your Week 1 target role.
2. For each, write one line on why it proves a skill the role wants.

**You've got it when:** you have 2-4 chosen pieces, each tied to a skill your target role asks for.

## Try it 3 - Write a case study for each piece

**Why it matters:** a link with no context is weak. A short case study tells the employer what you can do for them.

**Do this now - for each piece, write 3-5 lines:**
1. **The task:** what was needed and for whom (a client, a course brief, yourself).
2. **What you did:** the steps and tools.
3. **The result:** the outcome, with a number if possible.
4. **A link or image** of the work.

**You've got it when:** each chosen piece has a task/action/result case study and something to look at.

> Only show client or personal data with permission, and remove anything private (names, phone numbers,
> financial details). When in doubt, use fictional sample data.

## Try it 4 - Put it somewhere with a shareable link

**Why it matters:** an employer needs one link they can open. On low data, a PDF is light and always opens.

**Do this now - pick what fits your field and internet:**

| Option | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn "Featured" + Projects | Everyone | Free, already part of your profile |
| A PDF or Google Doc | VAs, writers, analysts | Easy, shareable link, works on low data |
| A free site builder | Designers, developers | More polished; check current free tiers |
| GitHub | Developers, data | Code and README (see the Data Analysis / Web Development courses) |

1. Build your portfolio in one of the above.
2. Get a shareable link and test it opens in a private browser window (so you see what others see).
3. Add the link to your CV and LinkedIn, and your two best pieces to LinkedIn's Featured section.

**You've got it when:** you have one link that opens cleanly for anyone, linked from your CV and LinkedIn.

## Try it 5 - Ask for one reference

**Why it matters:** a person who vouches for you is powerful proof, and you can get one even without formal jobs.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick someone who has seen your work: a course facilitator, volunteer coordinator, past client or team lead.
2. Send a short, polite ask: explain the role you're seeking and make it easy ("A line or two about working
   with me would mean a lot").
3. On LinkedIn, you can request a recommendation with their permission.

**You've got it when:** you've asked at least one person, and kept a short list of 1-3 references (name,
relationship, how to contact) private, not public.

### Watch out for

- A portfolio with too many weak pieces instead of a few strong ones.
- Links with no explanation, no context, no result.
- Showing private client data without permission.
- Waiting until you're "ready": start with what you have and improve it.

## This week's build: your published portfolio

Publish a portfolio of 2-4 pieces that an employer could open today:

1. Each piece has a short case study (task, action, result) and a link or image.
2. Hosted somewhere with a shareable link (PDF, doc, or site).
3. Linked from your LinkedIn and CV.
4. One reference or recommendation requested (or secured).

### Grading guide

| Criteria | % |
|---|---|
| 2-4 relevant, strong pieces chosen | 25 |
| Clear case studies (task/action/result) | 30 |
| Hosted with a working shareable link | 20 |
| Linked from CV and LinkedIn | 15 |
| Reference requested; privacy respected | 10 |

### Definition of done

- [ ] A portfolio link that opens and works
- [ ] Each piece has a short case study with a result
- [ ] Linked from CV and LinkedIn
- [ ] At least one reference requested or secured
- [ ] No private data shown without permission

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Make one quick before/after example this week just for your portfolio (tidy a messy sample document).
- Ask a friend to open your portfolio link on their phone and tell you if anything is confusing.
- Write a one-line intro at the top of your portfolio so visitors know who you are instantly.

## Facilitator Notes

Learners often feel they have "nothing to show" - help them see coursework, volunteering and personal
projects as legitimate proof. Push for the task/action/result structure; links without context are weak.
Make privacy a hard rule: no real client data without written permission, fictional samples otherwise.

## Can you do these now?

1. What kinds of work count as proof if you have no paid experience?
2. What are the parts of a good case study?
3. Why might a PDF portfolio be a smart choice on unreliable internet?
4. How should you handle private client data in your portfolio?
5. Who could you reasonably ask for a reference?
