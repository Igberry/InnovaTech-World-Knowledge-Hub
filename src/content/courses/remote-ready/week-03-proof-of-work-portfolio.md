**Goal:** Give employers proof you can do the work - a simple portfolio or profile of your best work,
plus one or two credible references.

Saying you're good is not enough; showing it is what wins trust. This week you assemble evidence.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Choose and present 2-4 pieces of work that prove your skills
- Build a simple portfolio (a free site, a document, or a profile)
- Write clear case studies: the task, what you did, the result
- Ask for and use references/recommendations properly

## Pre-Session Resources (about 2 hours)

- Look at 3-5 portfolios in your field (search "[your field] portfolio examples").
- Watch (search YouTube): *"how to build a portfolio with no experience"*.
- Read about free portfolio options: a LinkedIn profile, a Google Doc/PDF, or a free site builder
  (search their current free tiers).

## Lesson 1: What counts as proof

You don't need paid experience to have proof. Good evidence includes:

- **Projects** you built (your InnovaTech course projects are ideal).
- **Volunteer or personal work** (a page you made, a spreadsheet you organised, content you wrote).
- **Before/after** examples (a messy inbox you organised, a tidied document).
- **Results** others saw (followers grown, time saved, errors reduced).

Pick your **best 2-4** pieces. Quality beats quantity.

## Lesson 2: Where to host your portfolio

Choose what fits your field and your internet:

| Option | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn "Featured" + Projects | Everyone | Free, already part of your profile |
| A PDF or Google Doc | VAs, writers, analysts | Easy, shareable link, works on low data |
| A free site builder | Designers, developers | More polished; check current free tiers |
| GitHub | Developers, data | Code + README (see the Data Analysis / Web Development courses) |

> **Tip:** On unreliable internet, a well-made **PDF portfolio** is light, easy to send, and always
> opens. You can add a nicer site later.

## Lesson 3: Writing a case study

For each piece, write a short case study (3-5 lines):

- **The task:** what was needed and for whom (a client, a course brief, yourself).
- **What you did:** the steps and tools.
- **The result:** the outcome, with a number if possible.
- **A link or image** of the work.

> **Privacy:** Only show client or personal data with permission, and remove anything private (names,
> phone numbers, financial details). When in doubt, use fictional sample data.

## Lesson 4: References and recommendations

A reference is someone who can vouch for your work. Even without formal jobs, you can ask:

- A course facilitator, a volunteer coordinator, a past client, a team lead.
- Ask politely, explain the role you're seeking, and make it easy ("A line or two about working with me
  would mean a lot").
- On LinkedIn, request a **recommendation** (with their permission).

Keep a short list of 1-3 references (name, relationship, how to contact) - never share their details
publicly without consent.

## Common mistakes

- A portfolio with too many weak pieces instead of a few strong ones.
- No explanation - just links, with no context or result.
- Showing private client data without permission.
- Waiting until you're "ready": start with what you have and improve it.

## Practice Exercises

1. **Easy:** List every piece of work you could show (projects, volunteer, personal).
2. **Easy:** Choose your best 2-4.
3. **Medium:** Write a case study for each chosen piece.
4. **Medium:** Put them into a portfolio (PDF, doc, or simple site) with a shareable link.
5. **Medium:** Add your two best pieces to LinkedIn's Featured section.
6. **Stretch:** Ask one person for a reference or LinkedIn recommendation.

## Weekly Assignment (Due Before Week 4)

Publish a **portfolio** with 2-4 pieces:

1. Each piece has a short case study (task, action, result) and a link or image.
2. Hosted somewhere with a shareable link (PDF, doc, or site).
3. Linked from your LinkedIn and CV.
4. One reference or recommendation requested (or secured).

**Grading guide (100%):**

| Criteria | % |
|---|---|
| 2-4 relevant, strong pieces chosen | 25 |
| Clear case studies (task/action/result) | 30 |
| Hosted with a working shareable link | 20 |
| Linked from CV and LinkedIn | 15 |
| Reference requested; privacy respected | 10 |

**Definition of done:**

- [ ] A portfolio link that opens and works
- [ ] Each piece has a short case study with a result
- [ ] Linked from CV and LinkedIn
- [ ] At least one reference requested or secured
- [ ] No private data shown without permission

## Facilitator Notes

Learners often feel they have "nothing to show" - help them see coursework, volunteering and personal
projects as legitimate proof. Push for the task/action/result structure; links without context are weak.
Make privacy a hard rule: no real client data without written permission, fictional samples otherwise.

## Self-Check Questions

1. What kinds of work count as proof if you have no paid experience?
2. What are the parts of a good case study?
3. Why might a PDF portfolio be a smart choice on unreliable internet?
4. How should you handle private client data in your portfolio?
5. Who could you reasonably ask for a reference?
