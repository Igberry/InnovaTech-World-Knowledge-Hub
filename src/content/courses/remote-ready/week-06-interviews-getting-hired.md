**Goal:** Prepare for and handle remote interviews with confidence, deal with test tasks and basic
negotiation, understand how to get paid internationally, and pull everything together into a complete,
remote-ready package (your capstone).

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week you can:

- Prepare for and perform well in a remote interview
- Present well on camera and handle common and test-task questions
- Discuss hours and rate professionally, and agree scope in writing
- Understand the main ways to get paid from abroad (as of late 2026)
- Present a complete, polished application package

## Pre-Session Resources (about 2 hours)

- Download the **interview question bank** (files box) and practise answers out loud.
- Watch (search YouTube): *"remote job interview tips"* and *"how to answer tell me about yourself"*.
- Read about current payment options for freelancers in Nigeria (see Lesson 4 - verify current terms).

## Lesson 1: Interview preparation

- **Research** the company and role; have 2-3 questions ready for them.
- **Practise** the common questions (question bank) using **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
- **Prepare your "tell me about yourself"** - 60-90 seconds, role-focused, confident, not your life
  story.
- **Set up:** quiet, well-lit spot; camera at eye level; test camera, mic and internet beforehand; have
  your CV and a glass of water nearby.

## Lesson 2: On-camera presence

- Look at the **camera** (not the screen) when speaking - it reads as eye contact.
- Sit up, smile, speak clearly and a little slower than normal.
- It's fine to pause and think, or to ask them to repeat a question.
- If tech glitches, stay calm: "Sorry, you cut out - could you repeat that?" Handling a glitch well is
  itself a good signal.

> **Tip:** Record a practice answer on your phone and watch it back. You'll spot filler words, low
> energy, or a distracting background to fix - fast improvement.

## Lesson 3: Test tasks and negotiation

- **Test tasks:** many remote roles include a small paid or short unpaid task. A short, clearly-scoped
  sample is normal; a long "test" that is really free work for their business is a red flag (Week 4).
- **Talking money:** research realistic ranges for the role and region first. When asked your rate, you
  can give a range and say you're flexible for the right fit. For freelance, agree **scope, rate, hours
  and payment terms in writing** before starting, and take a **deposit** for direct clients.
- It's fine to ask for time to consider an offer.

## Lesson 4: Getting paid from abroad (as of late 2026 - verify current terms)

Receiving international payments from Nigeria is very doable. Options commonly used by freelancers and
remote workers include:

| Option | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Payoneer** | Virtual foreign account details; withdraw to a Nigerian bank or a Payoneer card |
| **Grey / Geegpay** (fintechs) | Virtual USD/GBP/EUR accounts with relatively simple onboarding |
| **Domiciliary account** | A foreign-currency account at a Nigerian bank; good for larger, less frequent payments |
| **Deel** and similar | Used by some companies to pay international contractors |
| **PayPal** | Can receive in Nigeria, but you generally cannot withdraw directly to a Nigerian bank |

> **Important:** Fees, limits, licensing and bank rules **change often**. Verify the current terms,
> fees and legality of each option directly with the provider before relying on it (as of late 2026).
> Keep records of what you earn for your own bookkeeping.

## Lesson 5: Onboarding and first impressions

If hired: reply promptly, complete any paperwork carefully, ask good questions, set up the team's tools,
and over-communicate in the first weeks (short updates). A strong first month leads to referrals and
repeat work - the real engine of a remote career.

## Common mistakes

- Not testing camera/mic/internet before the interview.
- Rambling answers with no structure (use STAR).
- Doing large unpaid "tests".
- Agreeing freelance work with no written scope or deposit.
- Not checking current payment option terms.

## Practice Exercises

1. **Easy:** Write and rehearse your 60-90 second "tell me about yourself".
2. **Easy:** Prepare three questions to ask an interviewer.
3. **Medium:** Answer five question-bank questions out loud using STAR.
4. **Medium:** Record one answer on your phone and review it.
5. **Medium:** Research realistic pay ranges for your target role.
6. **Medium:** Research two current payment options and note fees/terms.
7. **Stretch:** Do a full mock interview with a friend or the facilitator.

## Weekly Assignment / Capstone (Due at the final session)

Present your **complete remote-ready package**:

1. Polished **CV** (PDF) + tailored version.
2. Complete **LinkedIn** profile.
3. **Portfolio** with case studies (link).
4. **Application tracker** showing real applications with follow-ups.
5. A rehearsed **"tell me about yourself"** and STAR answers to common questions.
6. A short written plan: your target roles, where you'll apply next, and your chosen payment method.
7. A **mock interview** with the facilitator.

**Grading guide (100%):**

| Criteria | % |
|---|---|
| Complete, polished package (CV, LinkedIn, portfolio) | 30 |
| Real, tracked applications | 15 |
| Mock interview performance (clarity, STAR, presence) | 30 |
| Payment + next-steps plan | 15 |
| Professionalism and reliability throughout the course | 10 |

**Definition of done:**

- [ ] CV, LinkedIn and portfolio all complete and linked together
- [ ] Application tracker shows real activity
- [ ] You can deliver a confident "tell me about yourself" and STAR answers
- [ ] A written next-steps + payment plan
- [ ] Mock interview completed

### Graduation session (about 2 hours, in person)
Each learner does a **mock interview** and presents their package against the rubric; the group gives
feedback; fix quick wins together; award certificates (70%+); agree each person's "first three places
I'll apply" list.

## Facilitator Notes

Run real mock interviews - recorded with permission - so learners see their own progress; it's the
highest-impact activity of the course. Keep money talk realistic and local; discourage both underselling
and fantasy rates. Verify current payment-option terms before the session, since fintech and CBN rules
shift. Reinforce honest outcomes: a strong package plus consistent applying, not guarantees.
