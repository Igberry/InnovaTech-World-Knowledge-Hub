**Goal:** By the end of this week you'll have rehearsed real interview answers on camera, practised handling
test tasks and money questions, chosen a payment method, and assembled your complete remote-ready package (your
capstone). You practise by doing, not by reading.

## What you'll be able to do

- Prepare for and perform well in a remote interview
- Present well on camera and handle common and test-task questions
- Discuss hours and rate professionally, and agree scope in writing
- Understand the main ways to get paid from abroad (as of late 2026)
- Present a complete, polished application package

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Download the [interview question bank](/downloads/remote-ready/interview-question-bank.md).
- Warm-up: pick the one interview question that scares you most. You'll answer it out loud in Try it 1.

## Try it 1 - Rehearse your "tell me about yourself"

**Why it matters:** this is the first question in most interviews. A rehearsed 60-90 second answer sets the tone.

**Do this now:**
1. Write a 60-90 second answer: role-focused, confident, not your life story.
2. Say it out loud three times until it flows.
3. Prepare 2-3 questions to ask the interviewer, and have your CV and a glass of water nearby.

**You've got it when:** you can deliver your "tell me about yourself" smoothly without reading it.

## Try it 2 - Answer questions with STAR

**Why it matters:** rambling loses interviewers. STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) keeps you clear and memorable.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick five questions from the question bank.
2. Answer each out loud using STAR: the situation, your task, what you did, the result.
3. Keep each answer under two minutes.

**You've got it when:** you can answer five common questions out loud, each with a clear result at the end.

## Try it 3 - Record yourself on camera

**Why it matters:** you spot filler words, low energy and a distracting background far faster by watching yourself than by being told.

**Do this now:**
1. Set up as for a real interview: quiet, well-lit, camera at eye level.
2. Record one answer on your phone, looking at the camera (not the screen) when you speak.
3. Watch it back. Fix one thing: light, background, pace or energy.
4. If tech glitches in a real interview, stay calm: "Sorry, you cut out, could you repeat that?" Handling a
   glitch well is itself a good signal.

**You've got it when:** you've watched yourself answer on camera and fixed at least one thing.

## Try it 4 - Practise test tasks and money talk

**Why it matters:** getting these wrong costs you money or respect. A little rehearsal makes you sound professional.

**Do this now:**
1. Decide your line on test tasks: a short, clearly-scoped sample is normal; a long "test" that is really free
   work for their business is a red flag (Week 4).
2. Research realistic pay ranges for your target role and region.
3. Rehearse a rate answer out loud: give a range and say you're flexible for the right fit.
4. For freelance, note what you'll always agree in writing before starting: scope, rate, hours, payment terms,
   and a deposit for direct clients.

**You've got it when:** you can state a realistic rate range out loud and name what you'd put in writing first.

## Try it 5 - Choose how you'll get paid (as of late 2026 - verify current terms)

**Why it matters:** getting paid from Nigeria is very doable, but the details change often and the wrong choice costs fees.

**Do this now:**
1. Read the options and pick two to look into for your situation:

| Option | What it is |
|---|---|
| Payoneer | Virtual foreign account details; withdraw to a Nigerian bank or a Payoneer card |
| Grey / Geegpay (fintechs) | Virtual USD/GBP/EUR accounts with relatively simple onboarding |
| Domiciliary account | A foreign-currency account at a Nigerian bank; good for larger, less frequent payments |
| Deel and similar | Used by some companies to pay international contractors |
| PayPal | Can receive in Nigeria, but you generally cannot withdraw directly to a Nigerian bank |

2. Check the current fees, limits and terms of your two directly with the provider.
3. Write down which one you'll use first and why.

> Fees, limits, licensing and bank rules change often. Verify the current terms, fees and legality of each
> option directly with the provider before relying on it (as of late 2026). Keep records of what you earn for
> your own bookkeeping.

**You've got it when:** you've chosen a payment method and checked its current terms yourself.

### Watch out for

- Not testing camera, mic and internet before the interview.
- Rambling answers with no structure (use STAR).
- Doing large unpaid "tests".
- Agreeing freelance work with no written scope or deposit.
- Not checking current payment option terms.

## This week's build / capstone: your complete remote-ready package

Pull the whole course together into one package you can start applying with:

1. Polished CV (PDF) plus a tailored version.
2. Complete LinkedIn profile.
3. Portfolio with case studies (link).
4. Application tracker showing real applications with follow-ups.
5. A rehearsed "tell me about yourself" and STAR answers to common questions.
6. A short written plan: your target roles, where you'll apply next, and your chosen payment method.
7. A mock interview with the facilitator.

### Grading guide

| Criteria | % |
|---|---|
| Complete, polished package (CV, LinkedIn, portfolio) | 30 |
| Real, tracked applications | 15 |
| Mock interview performance (clarity, STAR, presence) | 30 |
| Payment + next-steps plan | 15 |
| Professionalism and reliability throughout the course | 10 |

### Definition of done

- [ ] CV, LinkedIn and portfolio all complete and linked together
- [ ] Application tracker shows real activity
- [ ] You can deliver a confident "tell me about yourself" and STAR answers
- [ ] A written next-steps + payment plan
- [ ] Mock interview completed

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Do a full mock interview with a friend and swap honest feedback.
- Write your "first three places I'll apply" list and pin it where you'll see it.
- Draft a warm thank-you message to send after a real interview.

## Mentoring Session Plan (90 min + final graduation session)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 | Review the remote-operations kit from Week 5 |
| 10-35 | Live: interview prep; practise "tell me about yourself" and STAR |
| 35-55 | On-camera practice; handling test tasks and money questions |
| 55-75 | Payment options overview; agreeing scope/deposit in writing |
| 75-90 | Brief the capstone + graduation session |

### Graduation session (about 2 hours, in person)
Each learner does a mock interview and presents their package against the rubric; the group gives
feedback; fix quick wins together; award certificates (70%+); agree each person's "first three places
I'll apply" list.

## Facilitator Notes

Run real mock interviews - recorded with permission - so learners see their own progress; it's the
highest-impact activity of the course. Keep money talk realistic and local; discourage both underselling
and fantasy rates. Verify current payment-option terms before the session, since fintech and CBN rules
shift. Reinforce honest outcomes: a strong package plus consistent applying, not guarantees.
