**Goal:** By the end of this week you'll have found real openings, sent tailored applications, logged them in
a tracker, and built a scam radar that protects your money. This is a real search, done this week, not a reading exercise.

## What you'll be able to do

- Search the main platforms and boards for real openings in your field
- Write tailored applications and short outreach messages
- Track applications and follow up professionally
- Spot common scams and protect yourself

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Download the [application tracker](/downloads/remote-ready/application-tracker.csv) and
  [outreach templates](/downloads/remote-ready/outreach-templates.md).
- Warm-up: open the tracker and add the column headings as your map for the week (date, company, role, source,
  link, status, follow-up date).

## Try it 1 - Find where your remote work lives

**Why it matters:** relying on one channel is slow. Mixing channels is how people actually get hired.

**Do this now - search each of these for your target role and save what fits:**

| Channel | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn Jobs + search | Employed roles | Use filters: Remote, your role |
| Remote job boards | Remote-first companies | Search "remote job board [your field]" |
| Freelance platforms | Short projects, building a track record | e.g. Upwork, Fiverr; read current fees/terms |
| Referrals and networking | Often the best when starting | Tell people what you're looking for |
| Direct outreach | Small businesses needing help | Use the outreach templates, personalise each |

**You've got it when:** you've saved 10+ real openings that match your Week 1 goal, from at least two channels.

## Try it 2 - Send a tailored application

**Why it matters:** a generic blast gets ignored. A tailored application that matches the advert's words gets read.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick one saved opening and reread the advert's key words (Week 2 tailoring).
2. Use an outreach template, then change the first line to be specific to that company.
3. Lead with your single most relevant point. Keep it short.
4. Send it. Then do two more.

**Rule of thumb:** if one application takes more than about 15 minutes, you're overthinking it. Tailor the key
points and move on. Consistency across many applications beats perfection on one.

**You've got it when:** you've actually sent at least three genuinely tailored applications.

## Try it 3 - Track and set a follow-up

**Why it matters:** a tracker turns a scattered search into a system, and follow-ups win replies others miss.

**Do this now:**
1. Log every application in the tracker: date, company, role, source, link, status.
2. Set a follow-up date about 5-7 days out for each.
3. Draft one polite follow-up message now (template 4 in the outreach file) so it's ready to send.

**You've got it when:** every application is logged with a follow-up date, and you have a follow-up message ready.

## Try it 4 - Build your scam radar (do this carefully)

**Why it matters:** new remote workers in Nigeria are actively targeted, and these scams cause real money loss.

**Do this now:**
1. Read the one rule that catches most scams:

> Never pay to get a job. Legitimate employers and clients do not charge you a "fee", "training cost",
> "equipment deposit" or "registration" to start work. If they ask you to pay, it's a scam.

2. In your notes, write five red flags in your own words. Use these as prompts:
   - **Overpayment / fake cheque:** a "client" overpays and asks you to refund the difference. The payment is
     fake; you lose your own money. Never refund from your own funds.
   - **"Trial tasks" that are real work:** a long unpaid "test" that is actually a finished deliverable for them.
     A tiny, clearly-scoped sample is fine; building their real project free is not.
   - Too-good-to-be-true pay, or urgent pressure to act fast.
   - Vague company, no real online presence, or contact only via a personal chat app.
   - Requests for sensitive data (bank PIN, BVN, passwords) or to move to crypto immediately.
3. Write the one-line response you'd give to each flag.

**You've got it when:** you have five red flags in your own words, each with how you'd respond.

## Try it 5 - Verify one company before engaging

**Why it matters:** two minutes of checking saves you from most scams and wasted applications.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick one company you're applying to.
2. Check its website, LinkedIn page and any reviews.
3. Note what you found, and whether anything feels off. If it does, walk away.

**You've got it when:** you've verified one company and written down what you found.

### Watch out for

- Applying to one job a week and expecting results.
- The same untailored message to everyone.
- Not tracking applications, so follow-ups never happen.
- Ignoring red flags because the offer is exciting.

## Extra quick wins (optional)

- Tell three people in your network exactly what role you're looking for. Referrals often beat job boards.
- Set a small daily target (e.g. two applications a day) and tick it in the tracker.
- Save one scam message you receive (don't reply) as a real example for your red-flags note.

## Facilitator Notes

Make the scam section vivid and non-negotiable - new remote workers in Nigeria are heavily targeted,
and the "pay to get a job" and overpayment scams cause real losses. Push volume with quality: learners
who send one application a week stall. Check that outreach messages are personalised, not blasted. Keep
learners' application details private.
