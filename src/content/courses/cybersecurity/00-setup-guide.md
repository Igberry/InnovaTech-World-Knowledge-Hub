**Goal:** In this setup hour you will actually install the two tools that stop most attacks, turn them on for
your own email, and write down the three ethics rules. Almost no reading - you set things up by doing them.

## What you'll be able to do

- Install and use a password manager on your own phone or laptop
- Turn on two-step verification for your real email account
- Save your backup codes somewhere you can find them again
- Say, in your own words, the three ethics rules this course runs on

## Before you start (about 10 minutes)

- Grab a device you own (phone or laptop) and open a blank note for jotting things down.
- Warm-up: quickly list the accounts that would hurt most if you lost them - your email, your mobile money, your
  main social account. Keep this list; you will secure these first.

## Try it 1 - Install a password manager

**Why it matters:** reused and weak passwords are the number one way accounts get taken. A password manager
fixes this in one move - you remember one master password, it remembers the rest.

**Do this now:**
1. Pick a reputable password manager and install it on your phone or laptop (check its own website for current
   free tiers).
2. Create a **long master password** - a passphrase of 3 or 4 random words is strong and easy to recall. Never
   reuse it anywhere.
3. Add your email account as your first saved login.

**You've got it when:** your password manager is installed and your email login is saved in it.

## Try it 2 - Turn on 2FA for your email

**Why it matters:** your email resets every other account, so it is the master key. A stolen password alone
should never be enough to open it.

**Do this now:**
1. Open your email account's security settings and find "two-step verification" or "2FA".
2. Choose an **authenticator app** or a **passkey** if offered (stronger than SMS codes, which can be
   SIM-swapped).
3. Follow the steps to link it, then sign out and back in to confirm it asks for the second step.

**You've got it when:** logging in to your email now needs a code or a tap, not just a password.

## Try it 3 - Save your backup codes

**Why it matters:** if you lose your phone, backup codes are how you get back in. People skip this step and
lock themselves out.

**Do this now:**
1. In the same 2FA settings, find "backup codes" or "recovery codes" and generate them.
2. Save them somewhere offline you can reach without your phone - written in a safe place, or printed.
3. Note in your checklist where you put them.

**You've got it when:** your backup codes are saved somewhere safe and offline.

## Make it yours - the three ethics rules

Write these somewhere you will see them. They are what make security people trustworthy:

1. **Defence only.** I learn to protect, not to attack.
2. **Own systems only.** I practise on what is mine.
3. **Permission always.** If I ever help someone else, I get their clear permission first.

> **Warning:** Never test, probe, or try to access accounts, devices, or networks that are not yours. It is
> unsafe, unethical, and usually illegal. This whole course is about protecting, never attacking.

### Watch out for

- A weak or reused master password (it protects everything else - make it strong)
- Choosing SMS codes when an app or passkey is offered
- Skipping backup codes, then getting locked out
- Setting all this up only in your head instead of actually doing it today

Open the [password and 2FA audit](/downloads/cybersecurity/password-2fa-audit.csv) and the

## Plan for power and data

- Set up the password manager and 2FA while you have power and data; afterwards they work with very little data.
- Keep your backup codes saved offline so a network outage never locks you out.

## Setup checklist

- [ ] Password manager installed with a strong master password
- [ ] 2FA on for email (an app or passkey where possible)
- [ ] Backup/recovery codes saved safely offline
- [ ] Three ethics rules written down
- [ ] Password-and-2FA audit started

## Facilitator Notes

The goal of setup is that learners leave with real tools installed and their email genuinely secured, not just
notes. Walk through a password manager and 2FA setup live on their own devices. Hammer the "never share your
one-time code" rule early, since OTP-sharing scams are extremely common locally (mobile money, WhatsApp).
Explain SIM-swap simply so learners prefer app-based 2FA over SMS. Reinforce the three ethics rules; this course
only ever teaches defence. Be patient with learners who have many reused passwords to fix.
