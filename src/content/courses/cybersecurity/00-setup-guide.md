**Goal:** Set up the core tools you will use (a password manager and two-step verification), prepare a safe
way to practise, and agree the ethics rules that guide everything in this course.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this setup you can:

- Set up a password manager and two-step verification
- Explain, in plain words, why these two tools matter most
- Describe the ethics rules: defence only, own systems only, permission always
- Start a password-and-2FA audit of your accounts

## 1. The two tools that matter most

If you do only two things from this whole course, do these. They stop the large majority of common attacks.

| Tool | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Password manager | Creates and stores strong, unique passwords; you remember one master password | Reused and weak passwords are the number one way accounts get taken |
| Two-step verification (2FA) | A second step at login (a code or a tap on your phone) | A stolen password alone cannot get in |

Tools and free tiers change, so **check the current details on each tool's own website**.

## 2. Set up a password manager

- Choose a reputable password manager and create an account with a **strong master password** you do not use
  anywhere else.
- Start adding your important accounts (email first).
- Let it generate new, unique passwords as you update each account over the course.

> **Important:** Your master password is the one you must never forget and never reuse. Make it long (a
> passphrase of several random words is strong and memorable) and store a recovery method safely.

## 3. Turn on two-step verification (2FA)

- Turn on 2FA for your **email first** (it resets everything else), then banking, then social accounts.
- Prefer an **authenticator app** or **passkeys** over SMS codes where possible (SMS can be intercepted or
  SIM-swapped), but SMS 2FA is still far better than none.
- Save any **backup/recovery codes** the service gives you, somewhere safe.

## 4. Prepare a safe way to practise

Everything in this course you practise on **your own** accounts and devices. You do not need a lab or special
software. You need:

- Your own phone and/or laptop
- Your own accounts (to audit and secure)
- The checklists and templates from this course

> **Warning:** Never practise on, test, or try to access accounts, devices, networks, or systems that are not
> yours. That is unsafe, unethical, and usually illegal. This course is about protecting, never attacking.

## 5. The ethics rules (agree these now)

Write these somewhere you will see them:

1. **Defence only.** I learn to protect, not to attack.
2. **Own systems only.** I practise on what is mine.
3. **Permission always.** If I ever help someone else, I have their clear permission first.

These rules are what make security people trustworthy. Breaking them can cause real harm and serious legal
trouble.

## 6. Start your audit

Open the [password and 2FA audit](/downloads/cybersecurity/password-2fa-audit.csv) and list your important
accounts. You will fix the gaps in Week 2. Begin the
[security checklist](/downloads/cybersecurity/security-checklist.md) too.

## 7. Plan for power and data

- Set up the password manager and 2FA while you have power and data; after that they work with little data.
- Keep backup/recovery codes saved offline (written down safely) in case you are without a network.

## 8. Setup checklist

- [ ] Password manager set up with a strong master password
- [ ] 2FA on for email (and ideally banking)
- [ ] Backup/recovery codes saved safely
- [ ] Ethics rules written down and understood
- [ ] Password-and-2FA audit started

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why are a password manager and 2FA the two most important tools?
2. Why secure your email account first?
3. What are the three ethics rules, and why do they matter?
